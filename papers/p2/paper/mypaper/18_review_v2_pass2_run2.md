## Reviewer A (robotics and tactile manipulation)

### Topic adherence
The paper stays on its declared topic: a ramp-down friction-onset probe, CRC on the physical binary slip loss, a comparison against the strongest non-tactile conformal baseline, an attribution ablation and an online binary-feedback study under shift. No environment or tooling issue is presented as a contribution. The appendix on compute and deviations is appropriate housekeeping. Two places drift toward over-elaboration rather than off-topic material: the label-route secondary rows, and the long hedging around every secondary number.

### Strengths
1. **A clear, honest experimental question with a fair comparator.** The comparator was selected on a disjoint split before calibration and test data were read (CSF-1 at 0.7686 against Mondrian-CSF 0.8294 and CertGrip-P 0.8335; verified in `comp_selection.json`). The friction-free preload, identical for every arm, is a clean design for isolating the probe as the only friction channel.
2. **The attribution story is unusually candid, and the data support it.** The simple Coulomb plug-in on the onset is about as good as the learned model. Onset-only beats CertGrip-full on all six route × grid rows (verified: 0.4464/0.4168, 0.4412/0.4289, 0.4736/0.4563, 0.4629/0.4475, 0.4148/0.4043, 0.4303/0.4015). Static touch and the squeeze add nothing. The paper correctly concludes that the method is "probe + simple rule + CRC".
3. **Failure regimes are reported.** These are the σ curve (D 0.6789, 0.5825, 0.4168, 0.1401), the narrow-friction sub-pool reversal (D −0.2614) and the S2 oracle that itself slips above α. The scope exclusions (peak force, fragile objects, micro-slip during the probe) are stated plainly.
4. **The results reproduce exactly.** I recomputed the headline D = 0.4168, all per-stratum slips and the λ̂ indices from the raw replay files without using the repository's analysis code.

### Weaknesses
1. **Simulation only, with a modelled sensor and an assumed noise level.** That is the central problem for an RSS tactile paper. The whole effect size is set by σ, which is assumed, not measured. D drops from 0.68 to 0.14 between σ = 0 and σ = 0.4, so without even a bench measurement of onset-force repeatability on a real sensor the headline cannot be placed on the curve. The contact model is rigid MuJoCo with pads sharing the object's μ, and disturbances are body forces with the hand still. RSS reviewers will expect at least one hardware experiment, or a measured σ, for a tactile grip-regulation contribution.
2. **The "minimal force" framing conflicts with the protocol.** Every object, including 50 g objects whose F\* is below 1 N (for example F1 object 0: F\*_mid 0.81 N), first receives 32.7 N for 0.4 s and a 49 N squeeze. In the simulated episode (0.4 s preload, 0.3 s squeeze, 0.25 s probe, 0.6 s disturbance window), the force-time integral of the shared protocol is comparable to or larger than that of the hold for most objects. The 42 % saving therefore applies to a fraction of the force the object actually experiences. The paper acknowledges this, but RE is the only efficiency metric reported. Peak force, impulse and absolute (N) excess per arm are missing, and no adaptive-preload variant was run. That variant would show whether the probe still works when the preload is not 30 to 600 times the weight.
3. **No tactile competitor.** Related Work names two families (reactive slip tightening, and friction estimation plus a safety factor) as the natural competitors, and neither is evaluated. The only stand-in, B2 (probe plus one uniform margin), is weak by construction, as the paper admits: the D3/D4 plug-in ignores the hidden load. RAW-Qv shows that one validation-tuned shift already lands near α in distribution. A per-stratum validation-tuned margin on CSF-μ̂ would therefore very likely match CRC in force and roughly in slip. Without it, the practical added value of the certificate over standard practice is unquantified.
4. **Is this tactile at all?** The friction information is carried by the onset force of a ramp-down. Static touch is uninformative in this model, and the detector is a ratio threshold on summed shear and normal readings. Gripper-side signals (motor current and encoder), wrist F/T or vision of object motion could plausibly detect the same onset. The title's "tactile" claim needs either a non-tactile onset-detector ablation or a narrower wording.
5. **Robot-side risk of the probe.** The probe drives every object to incipient slip. The maximum displacement at onset is 1.92 mm against a 2 mm slip threshold (verified: median 0.126 mm, p95 0.254 mm). On real hardware, with sensor latency and less clean signals, some fraction of probes would become slips or drops. That rate is the dominant safety number for deployment and is not estimated, for example with added detector latency.
6. **The online result under shift is mixed for the probe arm and the text understates it.** Frozen-CertGrip breaks more than Frozen-COMP under S3 (window slip 0.2461 against 0.1910, `h3_analysis.json`), which suggests that the tactile arm is more fragile to amplitude shift. Table 3 shows this, but Section 5.6 does not mention it. The B3 shifted windows skip steps 501 to 1,000, where the catch-up transient occurs (audited physical slip 0.134 for OQT-CertGrip in S1, steps 501 to 1,000). The catch-up cost is therefore not reported for the physical-feedback route a robot would use.
7. **Length and readability.** The main text runs to about 10,300 words (Abstract 252, Introduction 1,197, Related Work 693, Method 2,242, Setup 1,155, Results 2,879, Discussion 1,019, Limitations 551, Conclusion 306), plus about 1,900 words of appendix. No section is under the minimum lengths in the task, but the paper is far over an RSS 8-page budget. Every number carries route, grid and adjustment qualifiers, and hypothesis codes still appear in table rows. A robotics reader will struggle to find the message.

### Actionable revisions (Reviewer A)
- Paper-level: rewrite the efficiency claim as "hold force after a fixed high-force protocol". Add peak force, impulse and absolute excess (N) per arm; these can be computed from existing replays and labels. Shorten the main text to fit 8 pages by moving label-route rows, H-codes and the second-round descriptive studies to the appendix. Mention Frozen-CertGrip's larger S3 break in the text. Report the 501 to 1,000 transient for the physical-feedback streams if those steps were logged.
- Evidence-level: run (i) a reactive slip-tightening controller and (ii) friction estimate plus a per-stratum validation-tuned margin, both compared at matched slip. Run an adaptive or low-preload variant. Ablate the onset detector with a non-tactile signal. Measure onset-force repeatability on at least one real sensor and gripper, or else retitle the paper as a simulation study of a probe primitive.

### Score (Reviewer A): **4 / 10 (borderline, leaning reject at RSS)**
The work is careful, reproducible and honest, but the contribution rests on an assumed sensor-noise level in rigid-contact simulation. It omits the tactile baselines it names, and its "minimal force" is measured after a protocol that applies far more force than the hold. With hardware grounding of σ and one tactile competitor it would move to a weak accept.

### Strengths
1. **The certificate is applied correctly.** The monotonised loss L̃ ≥ L, which is non-increasing in λ, the (nR̃ + 1)/(n + 1) ≤ α rule, per-stratum calibration and the treatment of unavoidable instances all match the CRC theorem (Angelopoulos et al.). Switching from split conformal on labels to CRC on the physical loss after the monotonicity gate failed, as the registered failure branch prescribed, is the right call.
2. **The inference is careful.** The bootstrap resamples objects (four instances per object move together) and refits λ̂ in every draw, so calibration uncertainty is included in the CI\*\*. Robustness to F\*_lo/hi, five predictor seeds, two grids, matched slip and LTT are all reported, and the paper correctly says that the route variants re-analyse the same replays and are not replications.
3. **The confirmatory ledger is transparent.** One of four core decisions was confirmed, and the deviations, including dropping Holm and the post hoc grid, are listed. The paper states the in-expectation, marginal and per-stratum nature of the bound and the 4δ union-bound caveat for LTT correctly.

### Weaknesses
1. **Misstated pre-registration of H2b** (see the summary). It must be corrected in five places. The current wording also weakens the paper's own argument: the correct account (registered TOST, run, failed on the decision route, passed on the label route) is more informative than "not registered".
2. **"σ and σ_p are different noise models and are never placed on one scale" is not defensible as written.** Because μ̂ = m g / (2 F_onset), log-noise σ on F_onset is log-noise σ on μ̂, on the same scale as σ_p, up to clipping and the detector's intrinsic error. From the label files (4,800 calibration and test instances) I computed the probe's effective error in log μ̂ − log μ. After removing the constant bias of about +0.32, which CRC absorbs, the SD is 0.098, 0.143, 0.227 and 0.417 at σ = 0, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.4. The correlation with log μ is 0.959, 0.916, 0.816 and 0.600. A CSF-proxy at σ_p = 0.2 has correlation about 0.87, so it is a **better** friction estimate than the probe at the primary σ = 0.2. The H5 null at σ_p = 0.2 is therefore exactly what one should expect, and it is the most interpretable result of H5. The paper should state this equivalence instead of disclaiming it, and report the probe's effective friction-estimation error at each σ.
3. **The H5 superiority rows at σ_p 0.4/0.8, and the "learner beats plug-in at σ = 0.4" finding, compare against dominated estimators.** CSF-proxy at σ_p 0.4 (RE 0.9213) and 0.8 (RE 2.786), and CSF-μ̂ at σ 0.4 (RE 0.9877), are all *worse* than the friction-blind CSF-1 (0.7832). They plug a noisy reading in without shrinkage, while the prior SD of log μ is only 0.347. A Bayes or linear-shrinkage plug-in can never be worse than CSF-1. In my computation, linear shrinkage at σ = 0.4 lowers the residual log-friction SD from 0.417 to 0.278. So "held as predicted" at σ_p ≥ 0.4 is uninformative, and the learned model's advantage at σ 0.4 is plausibly just shrinkage. The paper's own D-μ diagnostic hints at this ("as if the learner partly shrank"). A shrunk plug-in is the right physics baseline.
4. **The empirical validity of the certificate is shown on one calibration draw only.** CRC bounds the expectation over calibration draws. The evidence is a single split, on which CSF-1 exceeds α in D1 (0.1157) and D3 (0.1057). On the refined grid, CertGrip-full exceeds α in D1 (0.1043) and D2 (0.1014); the paper lists only the primary-grid per-stratum slips, all at or below α, which reads more favourably than the full picture. Because every instance has been replayed on the full grid, repeated random calibration/test re-splits of objects need no new simulation. They would show directly that mean per-stratum test slip is at most α and how wide the realised-slip distribution is, which is the quantity a practitioner cares about. The bootstrap with replacement is not a substitute, because duplicated calibration instances change the order statistics.
5. **The certificate is not deployable, so the title's "certified" is simulation-only.** Both offline certificates need replays of the same grasp at all 20 or 41 grid forces. The deployable route (OQT) has only the long-run accounting identity, which the paper correctly calls "expected behaviour rather than a finding". The one deployable-route study (B3) has 5 orderings, no intervals, no S2, and omits the transient window. The statistical contribution is therefore an application of published tools (CRC, LTT, OQT) with no new guarantee. The paper says this. For RSS that is acceptable only if the robotics side carries the novelty, and in simulation it does so only partly.
6. **Grid design and multiplicity.** The primary grid (pooled q̂_sel ± 0.4) had a floor defect for the plug-ins in D1/D2, so the decision-bearing H2b estimate is biased towards the learned arm, as the paper admits. The refined grid is post hoc. Holm was registered and dropped. All this is disclosed, but nothing has been done to remedy it. The Holm-adjusted versions of the secondary families can be computed from existing bootstrap output and should be reported. LTT at δ/4 per stratum (family-wise δ) also needs no new simulation and should be added.
7. **H2-R, the reversal regime, rests on the weakest route.** It uses the label route, whose premise failed. The sub-pool has 352 calibration instances in total, about 88 per stratum, which makes the split-conformal quantile noisy. Its upper limit (−0.0325) would probably not survive multiplicity correction. CRC replays already exist for every calibration and test instance, so the sub-pool can be redone on the CRC route, and for onset-only and CSF-μ̂, without new simulation.
8. **Minor points.** (i) The H2a decision rule applies the SESOI to the point estimate only, which is why σ = 0.4 is still labelled "superiority" while its lower limit (0.0832) is below the SESOI. This is handled through σ\*, but it should be stated in Table 1. (ii) The H2-H heterogeneity Q = 31.04 on 15 df (p ≈ 0.009) indicates real cell heterogeneity, driven by D3, and should be called that rather than only "pass". (iii) The matched-slip comparison is test-selected, as the paper says. A calibration-set-located matched level would make it a deployable comparison.

### Actionable revisions (Reviewer B)
- Paper-level: correct the H2b registration statement everywhere and report the registered TOST outcome on both routes. Replace the "never on one scale" sentence with the effective friction-error SD and correlation of the probe at each σ. Re-describe H5 at σ_p ≥ 0.4 and CSF-μ̂ at σ 0.4 as comparisons with dominated, unshrunk estimators. Add the refined-grid per-stratum slips next to the primary ones. Cite 08d (not 08c) for H3 feedback. Report H2-H heterogeneity as significant.
- Evidence-level (no new simulation): repeated object re-splits to check the CRC bound empirically and show the distribution of realised per-stratum slip; LTT at δ/4; Holm-adjusted secondaries; H2-R on the CRC route, including onset-only and CSF-μ̂.
- Evidence-level (new replays, cheap at about 0.01 s per replay): a Bayes or linear-shrinkage plug-in on the onset, and a shrunk CSF-proxy; physical-feedback streams with intervals, more orderings, S2 and the 501 to 1,000 transient; a matched-slip H3 comparison.

### Score (Reviewer B): **5 / 10 (borderline)**
The statistics are correct, the analysis reproduces exactly from raw outputs, and the reporting is unusually honest. The statistical contribution, however, is an application of known tools under a simulator-only certificate. The H2b registration is misdescribed. The noise-scale disclaimer hides the most informative reading of H5, and two headline "strong baseline" rows compare against estimators dominated by the friction-blind baseline.
