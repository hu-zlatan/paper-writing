# When Does a Friction-Onset Probe Pay Off? Conformal Slip-Risk Control of Grip Hold Force, Mapped in Simulation

## Abstract

We choose a grip *hold* force whose physical slip rate is at most α; without friction information, a conformal rule must cover the whole friction range. After a fixed preload, a ramp-down probe reads friction from the incipient-slip onset, a one-line Coulomb plug-in (or a learned model) turns it into a force, and conformal risk control on the binary physical-slip loss calibrates it per disturbance stratum: a marginal, in-expectation bound, in simulation. In pre-registered tests on fresh objects at assumed onset noise σ 0.2, the plug-in lowers relative excess hold force against the strongest friction-blind conformal baseline by D = 0.4314 [0.3824, 0.4835] at matched test slip (0.4482 at certified points), holding 0.66 N (9.3 %) less (post hoc). On relative excess at matched slip, one learned model is equivalent to the plug-in (±0.10); on absolute excess force (post hoc) it beats the plug-in by 0.2667 [0.1980, 0.3196], so the attribution is metric-dependent. The saving reverses when friction is predictable from the visible object family (family prior wins, matched D −0.5814), for the plug-in at high probe noise (matched −0.2065; the learned model keeps 0.1675 on the certified route) and as the friction range narrows. In force-time, the probe is repaid only for holds beyond 17.74–27.07 s, where slip was not simulated. The bound is marginal: single strata exceed α in up to 70 % of re-splits, and slip concentrates on low-friction objects (post hoc). Online, binary-feedback tracking returns slip near α after shift; efficiency versus oracle recalibration is inconclusive.

## 1 Introduction

A robot holding an object must choose a grip force. Force above need wastes actuation and loads the object for the whole hold; force below need lets it slip. Humans scale grip to friction after brief contact \cite{johansson1984roles} and re-regulate when friction changes \cite{willemet2024grip}. Tactile controllers react to incipient slip \cite{dong2019maintaining, veiga2020grip, berman2025autonomous}, estimate friction online \cite{khamis2021real, niu2026synchronized} or learn a gentle-grasp margin \cite{boonstra2024estimate}, but none states a distribution-free bound on the resulting slip rate. We study a narrower problem than minimal-force grasping: choosing the *hold* force after a fixed, high-force probe protocol.

Why a bound on the slip rate, rather than a lower average force? A grip controller is judged by its worst common failure, and an object that slips is usually a dropped object. Controllers that add a fixed safety margin to an estimated friction limit \cite{khamis2021real, johansson1984roles} implicitly trade force for safety, but the margin is set by hand and its slip rate on a new population of objects is unknown until objects are dropped. A calibrated statement of the form "on objects drawn like these, the hold force slips in at most a fraction α of grasps" turns that trade-off into one user-set number. The price of such a statement depends on what the force rule knows. If the rule knows mass, commanded acceleration and disturbance type but not friction, the calibrated multiplier must be large enough for nearly all of the slippery objects in the pool; with friction drawn between 0.3 and 1.0, high-friction objects are then held well above the force they need, and on our fresh objects the friction-blind baseline's mean relative excess at matched slip is 0.7518. Information about friction is exactly what a tactile probe can supply, and it is the only quantity our rules do not receive: every rule in this paper is given the true mass, the commanded acceleration and the disturbance stratum.

The probe is not free. Reading friction from the incipient-slip onset means squeezing, ramping the force down until the object starts to slip, and returning to a safe force, all before the hold begins. In our protocol this probe-and-return sequence costs more force-time than it saves in short holds: over a 1 s hold the integrated grip force is 22.20 N·s for a grasp that skips the probe and 39.10 N·s for the learned probe model, whereas over a 30 s hold the order flips (228.8 versus 216.4 N·s). Whether touch "pays off" is therefore not a single yes-or-no question; it depends on probe noise, on how widely friction varies, on whether friction can be guessed from appearance, and on how long the object is held. This paper maps those conditions under one certificate, so that the comparison between tactile and non-tactile rules is always made at the same certified slip level.

Conformal risk control certifies any force rule from a held-out calibration set \cite{angelopoulos2022conformal, lei2017distribution}. A rule that cannot see friction, however, must cover the whole friction range with one calibrated multiplier. The fair comparator for a tactile method is therefore the *strongest* friction-blind conformal baseline, chosen beforehand on a disjoint selection split: CSF-1 (mean relative excess 0.7686) beat a Mondrian variant (0.8294) and a learned proprioceptive model (0.8335). Where friction is predictable from the visible object family, we also compare against a deployable family prior.

We ask *when a short tactile probe makes a distribution-free physical-slip bound cheaper in hold force, and which component carries the saving*, where "cheaper" is measured, as registered, by relative excess hold force and, post hoc, also in newtons: (i) does the probe lower hold force at certified physical slip ≤ α (in simulation, marginal over the pool, in expectation), and is a learned model needed? (ii) Under which conditions (probe noise, friction range, friction-informative prior, hold duration) does the saving hold or disappear? (iii) Does calibration recover under shift when the only feedback is whether the object slipped?

**Contributions.**
- **(C-i) Method.** A protocol in which a friction-uninformative preload leaves the ramp-down onset as the only friction channel; per-stratum CRC on the monotonised physical binary slip loss (the label rule is not exactly monotone, 8 of 800) turns a one-line Coulomb plug-in on that onset into a rule with marginal expected slip ≤ α in simulation.
- **(C-ii) Confirmatory evaluation on fresh objects.** Five pre-registered, Holm-corrected decisions: the plug-in beats the baseline (C1); one trained learned model is equivalent to the plug-in in *relative* excess hold force at onset noise σ 0.2 on the registered matched-slip route (C2; not shown on the certified CRC route, and not in absolute excess force, where post hoc the learner is better); the earlier result replicates (C3); on a friction-correlated pool a family prior beats both tactile arms (C4, C5).
- **(C-ii′) Post hoc absolute-force and per-friction rows.** Re-reads of the same replays, not pre-registered: absolute excess force and hold-force savings in newtons, and slip by friction tercile, which shows what the marginal bound leaves to the slippery objects.
- **(C-iii) Boundary map.** A σ band, a friction-width sweep, a break-even against a simulated no-probe grasp, a 200-re-split check of the certificate, and an online binary-feedback study under three shifts.

Three of five confirmatory predictions are met; the two unpredicted rows (C4, C5) are not superior, with intervals below 0. The earlier pre-registered learned-versus-plug-in and label-validity decisions were not confirmed, and online efficiency is inconclusive. The attribution of the saving to the probe rather than to the learner is a σ 0.2 result on relative excess: at σ 0.4 the learned model beats the plug-in (D 0.3238 at matched slip), so a noisier sensor would need the learner. It is also a result on one metric. On absolute excess force at matched slip, a post hoc metric, the learned model beats the plug-in by 0.2667 [0.1980, 0.3196], about as much as the plug-in gains over CSF-1 on that metric (0.2372 [0.1401, 0.3328]), and a two-parameter shrunk plug-in comes close to the learner. The two metrics differ because relative excess divides each instance's excess by its own minimal force F\*, so light or high-friction instances with small F\* weigh most, whereas absolute excess counts newtons and is dominated by heavy or slippery instances, where the learner's correction of the raw onset estimate saves most. We therefore report both and do not claim that the learner adds nothing in general. Section 2 places the work, Section 3 defines the problem, protocol, force rules and calibration, Section 4 the simulator, pools and statistics, Section 5 the results, and Section 6 what they do and do not license.

## 2 Related Work

**Friction reading and tactile grip control.** Reading friction from incipient slip is established \cite{marconi1996incipient}; reviews treat incipient slip as the key friction cue \cite{chen2018tactile, romeo2020methods}, and human grip adapts to friction \cite{johansson1984roles, willemet2024grip}. Robotic controllers detect slip and tighten \cite{james2020slip, grover2022detect, ayral2026reactive}, keep grasps within slipping bounds \cite{dong2019maintaining}, estimate friction with a safety margin \cite{khamis2021real, niu2026synchronized}, learn incipient-slip margins or force policies \cite{boonstra2024estimate, kuo2025tracing, feng2026tactilereflex, lach2024zero}, or stabilise grasps with adaptive or independent-finger control \cite{almohammed2022switched, veiga2020grip}. Pre-grasp force can also be chosen from prior experience \cite{shang2026exp}, which relates to our family-prior result. None of these gives a coverage guarantee on slip rate.

**Slip margins and what our plug-in is.** The classical rule, in human grip \cite{johansson1984roles} and in robotic friction-estimating controllers such as \cite{khamis2021real}, is to hold at the estimated slip limit plus a safety margin. Our probe plug-in is a rule of this kind: because the onset force is read at the same gravity load, mass cancels and the plug-in holds at a calibrated multiple of the measured onset force scaled by the commanded acceleration (up to a clip on μ̂). The difference is how the margin is set. In such controllers it is a design constant chosen by the designer; here it is the CRC multiplier, set from a calibration set so that the expected slip rate on new objects from the same distribution is at most α. Section 5.4 shows that, on the first-round pool, a margin tuned on the same calibration data lands at the same operating point, so what CRC adds is the finite-sample guarantee, not a better controller.

**Vision and experience priors.** A friction-informative prior need not come from touch. Vision-language models conditioned on retrieved grasp experience predict a minimum feasible force from a single image \cite{shang2026exp}, and risk margins can be computed from a friction distribution before contact \cite{enwerem2026firmgrasp}. Our family prior CSF-fam is the simplest member of this class, one friction value per visible family, and our C4 / C5 result is the expected outcome whenever such a prior is sharp. A rule that combines a prior with the probe is the natural next comparator and was not run.

**Robust grasping under uncertainty.** Force optimisation under parameter uncertainty \cite{cloutier2018examining}, control barrier functions \cite{cortez2019control, kim2024robust}, friction-informed risk margins \cite{enwerem2026firmgrasp} and belief parameterisations \cite{enwerem2026variational} bound grasp risk relative to a model and its assumed uncertainty set. Our bound needs no friction model but is marginal and needs calibration data.

**Conformal prediction and risk control.** Conformal methods give finite-sample guarantees under exchangeability \cite{vovk2005algorithmic, romano2019conformalized, angelopoulos2023conformal, bian2023training, ding2023class}; CRC extends them to monotone losses \cite{angelopoulos2022conformal}, non-monotone losses \cite{angelopoulos2026conformal} and high-probability control \cite{angelopoulos2025learn}. Online variants track coverage under shift \cite{gibbs2021adaptive, gibbs2022conformal, angelopoulos2023conformala, angelopoulos2024online, farinhas2023non, ge2024stochastic}. In robotics they certify decisions, planning, verification and perception \cite{lekeufack2024conformal, lindemann2025formal, dixit2022adaptive, marques2026particle, lindemann2023conformal, ren2023robots, huang2023conformal, strawn2023conformal, zhang2024bayesian, zhao2024conformalized, kumar2026learnable}. None calibrates a contact outcome as a binary loss in force space.

**Conditional coverage.** Marginal guarantees can hide systematic under-coverage of sub-groups. Mondrian or class-conditional calibration \cite{vovk2005algorithmic, ding2023class} calibrates separately within groups at the cost of fewer calibration points per group, and training-conditional analyses \cite{bian2023training} separate the guarantee over the calibration draw from the guarantee for a fixed calibration set. We calibrate per known disturbance stratum, which is a Mondrian split by stratum, and use LTT for a high-probability version; we do not calibrate per object family or per friction level, and Section 5.3 shows where that matters: on the fresh pool every arm's slip concentrates on the lowest-friction tercile (post hoc), and on the correlated pool on the most slippery family.

**Novelty.** Onset-based friction reading, CRC, Learn-then-Test (LTT) and quantile tracking are used as published. What is new is (i) a protocol in which the onset is the only friction channel, so that its value can be isolated; (ii) a marginal bound on the *physical* slip rate of the resulting hold force, in simulation; and (iii) confirmatory tests that locate the relative-excess saving in the probe at σ 0.2, with post hoc absolute-force rows showing where a learned model still adds, and map where the saving disappears.

## 3 Problem Formulation and Method

### 3.1 Problem

An instance x comprises an object (family, geometry, mass m, friction μ), a pose offset and a disturbance stratum d ∈ {D1, …, D4}. Slip is a tangential displacement above 2 mm, a rotation above 3° or loss of contact. The minimal no-slip hold force F\*(x) is bracketed by 11-replay log-space bisection on [0.1, 100] N into F\*_lo, F\*_hi and F\*_mid = √(F\*_lo F\*_hi), labels that exist only in simulation. With relative excess RE(F) = E_x[(F(x) − F\*_mid(x))/F\*_mid(x)] of the hold force, the task is

$$\min_F \ \mathrm{RE}(F) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbb{P}(\mathrm{slip\ at\ } F(x)) \le \alpha .$$

Two rules are compared by D(a vs b) = 1 − RE(a)/RE(b); D > 0 means that a needs less excess force than b.

### 3.2 Grasp protocol and friction-onset probe

Every instance receives the same preload F₀ = 32.7 N, which encodes neither m nor μ. The gripper lifts and holds, squeezes F₀ → 1.5 F₀ → F₀, and runs the **ramp-down probe**: the force falls at a fixed rate until a touch-only detector on the pad shear/normal ratio reports incipient slip, giving F_onset and μ̂ = m g/(2 F_onset). Simulated touch is not clean: the taxel model adds per-taxel multiplicative and additive noise and a per-instance channel gain, and on the first-round pool the resulting friction estimate at no added noise has log error log μ̂ − log μ with mean +0.3186 and SD 0.1103 (a constant log bias is absorbed by the calibrated multiplier). On top of this detector we add onset noise, log F_onset + σε with ε ~ N(0, 1), σ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.4}; σ is therefore added, not total, noise, and σ 0.2 is primary and assumed. The grip then ramps to the chosen hold force F; a slip during the probe counts. The protocol is fixed: the squeeze peak 1.5 F₀ exceeds every instance's F\*_mid, while the preload itself exceeds nearly all of them but not every one (a few calibration and test instances need slightly more than F₀), the probe induces micro-slip by design, and the method chooses only the hold force. The **no-probe grasp** uses the same preload and skips the squeeze and probe phases.

### 3.3 Force rules

Every rule has the form F(x; λ) = min(F_max, F_base(x) e^λ), with λ set by calibration. Every rule receives the true mass m, the commanded acceleration a_cmd (the lift acceleration in D1, the peak shake acceleration in D2, zero for the external disturbances D3 and D4, whose amplitude the robot does not know) and the disturbance stratum d; only friction is unknown.
- **Probe plug-in:** F_base = m(g + a_cmd)/(2μ̂). With μ̂ = m g/(2 F_onset), mass cancels and F_base = F_onset (1 + a_cmd/g) up to the clip of μ̂ to [0.1, 3.0], so the plug-in is a calibrated multiple of the measured onset force.
- **Shrunk plug-in:** log μ̂_s = a + b log μ̂, with ordinary least squares on the selection split only, frozen from the previous round; at σ 0.2, a = −0.3858 and b = 0.7397.
- **Learned probe model (option):** a gradient-boosted quantile predictor on proprioception, static touch, squeeze and onset features. Its fit is not changed here, but it is reproduced rather than loaded: the first round stored predictions, not models, so the seed-0 model is re-fitted deterministically (fixed random state) from its stored configuration on the same training rows and accepted only if it reproduces the stored first-round log-predictions to within 1e-5 (an assertion in the code). Every fresh-pool and correlated-pool row uses this one fit of training seed 0.
- **Friction-blind baseline CSF-1:** F_base = m(g + a_cmd)/(2μ̄) with one fixed friction value μ̄ = √(0.3 · 1.0), the geometric mean of the design friction range (hard-coded; any constant is absorbed by e^λ). The selection split chose the baseline *type* (CSF-1 against a Mondrian variant and a learned proprioceptive model) before any calibration or test access; it did not choose μ̄.
- **Family prior CSF-fam (deployable, non-tactile):** the same formula with one μ̄ per visible family, the geometric-mean true μ of the family's 50 selection objects of the correlated pool (disjoint from calibration and test): F1 0.379, F2 0.496, F3 0.677, F4 0.852.
- **Earlier-round supporting arms:** onset-only learner; oracle-side friction prior μ̃ = clip(μ e^{σ_p ε_p}, 0.1, 3.0); probe + tuned per-stratum margin; reactive slip-tightening (registered negative control).

### 3.4 Calibration on the physical binary loss

For calibration instance i, the loss is L_i(λ) = 𝟙{physical slip in a direct replay at F(x_i; λ)}, monotonised on the grid Λ as L̃_i(λ) = max_{λ′ ≥ λ} L_i(λ′). Since L̃_i ≥ L_i, a bound on L̃ bounds physical slip. An *unavoidable* instance, one that slips even at F_max, counts as a slip at every λ in calibration and test and is excluded from RE (it has no finite F\*_mid).

**CRC (primary).** Per stratum, with R̃_n(λ) the mean of L̃_i over n calibration instances,

$$\hat\lambda = \min\Big\{\lambda \in \Lambda : \frac{n \tilde R_n(\lambda) + 1}{n + 1} \le \alpha\Big\}.$$

By \cite{angelopoulos2022conformal}, an exchangeable test instance then has E[slip] ≤ α. The bound is marginal over instances and friction and holds in expectation over the calibration draw; it is not conditional on a stratum realisation or object family.

**LTT (secondary).** λ is chosen so that the slip risk is ≤ α with probability ≥ 1 − δ, δ = 0.1, per stratum \cite{angelopoulos2025learn, bian2023training}.

**Why the physical loss.** The label route (split conformal on bisection labels \cite{romano2019conformalized}) transfers to physical slip only if slip is monotone in F. The monotonicity gate failed: 8 of 800 instances were non-monotone (registered one-sided 98.75 % Clopper–Pearson upper limit 0.02115 > 0.02; 7 of 8 in D2), so the label route is secondary. The level decides the gate: the count is small, and a less strict level could have passed it.

**Deployability.** Offline CRC needs a replay of each calibration grasp at every grid force, so it is a simulation procedure; the online tracker needs one binary outcome per grasp.

```
Algorithm 1: probe, then calibrate (per arm, per stratum d)
1  every instance: preload F0, squeeze, ramp-down probe -> F_onset (+ noise sigma); F_base(x) uses it
2  for calibration i, lambda in Lambda: L[i,lambda] <- slip of replay at min(F_max, F_base(x_i) e^lambda)
3  Ltilde[i,lambda] <- max_{lambda' >= lambda} L[i,lambda']
4  lambda_hat <- first lambda with (sum_i Ltilde[i,lambda] + 1)/(n + 1) <= alpha
5  deploy: probe, then command F = min(F_max, F_base(x) e^lambda_hat)
```

### 3.5 Online calibration from binary feedback

Score-space quantile tracking \cite{angelopoulos2023conformala} updates one log-force offset per stratum, q_{t+1} = q_t + η(slip_t − α), with η = 0.03412 (frozen) and anti-windup at F_max; score space, rather than the level update of \cite{gibbs2021adaptive}, tolerates scores beyond the calibration support after a friction drop. References: a decaying step \cite{angelopoulos2024online}, frozen calibration, oracle recalibration.

## 4 Experimental Setup

**Simulator and objects.** MuJoCo 4.0.0, a parallel-jaw gripper and one touch grid per pad (on tactile simulation see \cite{wang2022tacto, akinola2025tacsl, leins2025hydroelastictouch, luu2025manifeel, birtalan2026impact}). Families: F1 boxes, F2 cylinders, F3 ellipsoids / capsules, F4 off-centre-of-mass composites; F5 thin plates / disks only under shift. The summed pad normal reading differs from F₀ by at most 0.1597 N, and no instance slipped during the preload. The simulation step is 2 ms; each pad carries an 8 × 8 touch grid, and the incipient-slip detector sees only these taxels (shear/normal ratio and micro-displacement), never the true friction. Contact is rigid MuJoCo contact with Coulomb friction; no soft-pad, hydroelastic or viscoelastic effects are modelled.

**Pools.** The first-round pool draws μ log-uniform on [0.3, 1.0], independently of every non-tactile input, and m log-uniform on [0.05, 1.0] kg (2,000 calibration / 2,800 test instances). The **fresh pool (independent friction)** repeats this design with new objects (500 calibration / 700 test objects) and carries C1–C3, S1–S3, S6 and S7. The **friction-correlated pool** draws friction in narrow, mostly non-overlapping family bands (500 / 700 objects) and carries C4, C5 and S4. Every object appears once in each of the four strata, so a pool of 700 test objects gives 2,800 test instances; calibration and test objects never overlap, and a separate selection split (used only to choose the baseline type, fit the shrinkage, set the family prior and centre the force grids) overlaps neither.

**Labels and grid.** For every instance the simulator replays the full grasp deterministically at any commanded hold force, so the minimal no-slip force is bracketed by bisection and each calibration and test instance is replayed at every point of an 81-point log-force grid per arm and stratum, centred on a quantile fixed from selection rows only and capped at F_max = 100 N. These replays give the physical slip outcome L_i(λ) used by CRC and by every test-slip figure; no calibration or test label enters the grid. Unavoidable instances (Section 3.4) are rare: one calibration and one test instance on the fresh pool and one calibration instance on the correlated pool (post hoc count), so the convention cannot move any reported row by more than one instance in 2,800.

**Strata and shifts.** Strata: D1 lift acceleration (1–10 m/s²), D2 shake (5–20 mm amplitude at 2–5 Hz), D3 lateral pull on the object and D4 added payload, the last two ramped in over 0.1 s and applied during the 0.6 s disturbance window that follows the ramp to the hold force. The stratum is known to every rule and is the calibration group; D3 and D4 amplitudes are not known to the rules. Online shifts: friction (μ × 0.6), family (new F5) and amplitude (× 1.5), on streams of T = 2,000 steps, shift from step 501, 20 orderings.

**Statistics.** CI\*\* is a two-sided 97.5 % object-cluster bootstrap interval (2,000 draws, all valid, calibration and test objects resampled within family, λ̂ refitted in each); it is shown for display and does not decide the confirmatory tests. Two comparison routes are used. On the *CRC route* each arm is evaluated at its own certified λ̂, so arms may differ in realised slip. On the *test-slip-matched route* each stratum's λ̂ index is shifted by up to ±8 grid steps and RE is interpolated at a realised test slip of 0.10; this compares arms at equal realised slip, uses test outcomes, and is therefore a frontier comparison rather than a deployable certified rule. It is the registered route for C1, C2, C4 and C5; C3 is on the CRC route. The smallest effect size of interest (SESOI) is 0.10. Bootstrap p-values are p = min(1, 2 · min(share of draws ≤ 0, share ≥ 0)). As registered, C1, C3, C4 and C5 declare superiority iff the Holm-adjusted p over the five confirmatory tests is < 0.05 and the point estimate is > 0; C2 declares equivalence iff both one-sided TOST p-values at ±0.10 pass Holm. Secondary equivalence statements use the same TOST criterion, unadjusted. A grid-boundary check (no λ̂ at the first or last grid index in any stratum, which would make a row uninterpretable) passed for every arm and row of the confirmatory round. First-round core decisions used Bonferroni over 4, its 52 secondaries Holm within family (no verdict changes); S1–S7 and later-round rows are unadjusted.

**Post hoc rows (PH).** After the confirmatory read-out, reviewers asked for forces in newtons and for slip by friction level. These rows re-read the existing confirmatory replays; they are report-only, were not pre-registered and decide nothing. They use the same split, the same per-stratum CRC and the same matched-slip recipe, and 2,000 object-cluster draws (seed 2027). Absolute excess AE(F) = E_x[F(x) − F\*_mid(x)] in newtons on avoidable test instances, and D_abs(a vs b) = 1 − AE(a)/AE(b), on either route. Hold-force savings are differences of mean hold force against CSF-1 at each arm's CRC point. Slip by friction tercile splits the 2,800 fresh test instances at the tercile edges of true μ (0.4457 and 0.6553). Relative and absolute excess answer different questions: RE asks how far above its own need each grasp is held, AE how many newtons are spent above need on average, so an arm that corrects the onset estimate mainly on heavy or slippery objects gains more on AE than on RE.

**Deviations.** (1) The CRC route was written after the monotonicity counts were seen, before any predictor existed. (2) A first-round grid floor was handled by a post hoc refined grid (earlier-round rows only). (3) The first-round pre-registration misstates the selection objects once; the code uses 150–199. (4) Later-round analyses were registered after earlier results were seen and cannot change a first-round decision. (5) The registered physical-replay online oracle was not produced. (6) The confirmatory protocol was registered before any of its code was written or run; its decision code was committed before any confirmatory replay and run once. (7) C4 / C5 had no registered prediction; the rule defines only superiority there. (8) The break-even S6 was registered as a "measured break-even … from the replay's commanded profile"; it is delivered as a closed-form computation (hold force × hold time plus fixed phase integrals), whose squeeze term uses the mean measured pad-normal reading during the squeeze rather than the commanded profile; it has no interval. (9) S3 was listed among secondary rows but carried a registered directional prediction (D shrinks with w, with D ≤ 0 expected near w 0.25); we report its outcome (Section 5.2). (10) The PH rows above were added after the read-out at reviewers' request and are not part of the registered analysis.

## 5 Results

Status codes (CONF, PRE, SEC, DESC, PH) are defined under Table 1; α = 0.10 and σ 0.2 unless stated. Every effect size carries its route: *matched* (test-slip-matched, the registered route for C1, C2, C4, C5) or *CRC* (each arm at its certified λ̂).

### 5.1 Confirmatory tests on fresh objects

**On 700 fresh test objects at σ 0.2, all three registered predictions hold. On the friction-correlated pool, the family prior beats both tactile arms.**

*C1 (CONF).* The plug-in needs less excess hold force than CSF-1 at matched slip: D = 0.4314 [0.3824, 0.4835], superiority; on the CRC route the same contrast is 0.4482 [0.3552, 0.5017]. *C2 (CONF).* The learned model is equivalent to the plug-in within ±0.10 at σ 0.2 on the registered matched-slip route, D = 0.01117 [−0.05552, 0.05686]; both one-sided TOST p-values are < 0.0005 and pass Holm. On the CRC route the evidence is weaker: −0.06291 [−0.1226, 0.08396]. Here two criteria disagree: the unadjusted TOST p is 0.037, below 0.05, but the 97.5 % interval is not inside ±0.10. We therefore do not claim equivalence on the CRC route; C2's equivalence holds on the registered matched route and for one trained model only. C2 concerns *relative* excess at matched slip only: the learned model holds less mean absolute force than the plug-in (6.115 versus 6.463 N) and slips less at its CRC point (0.08107 versus 0.09786), and C2, C3 and C5 rest on a single trained model (seed 0). *C3 (CONF).* The learned model beats CSF-1 on the CRC route, D = 0.4135 [0.3578, 0.4903], replicating the first-round pre-registered result (0.4168 [0.3484, 0.4778]; seeds 1–4: 0.4343 / 0.4197 / 0.3932 / 0.4494).

*C4 / C5 (CONF, no prediction registered).* On the friction-correlated pool, the plug-in against CSF-fam gives −0.5814 [−0.7208, −0.4563] and the learned model −0.5461 [−0.6590, −0.4290]: not superior under the registered rule, with each CI\*\* wholly below 0 (the registered decision code labels this "inferiority"; the rule itself defines only superiority). CSF-fam's matched RE, interpolated at test slip 0.10, is 0.2674; at its own CRC operating point it slips at only 0.05929, with RE 0.2995. The tactile arms still beat CSF-1 on this pool (0.4285, 0.4412), and the CRC-route rows agree in sign (−0.5166 / −0.4733).

Mean hold force on the fresh pool is 6.115 N (learned), 6.463 N (plug-in) and 7.125 N (CSF-1); CSF-fam holds 5.933 N on the correlated pool.

*Absolute force (PH, post hoc, not pre-registered).* In newtons the savings are modest: against CSF-1, mean hold force at the CRC point is 0.66 N (9.3 %) lower for the plug-in, 1.01 N (14.2 %) lower for the learned model and 0.94 N lower for the shrunk plug-in. This is set against a probe protocol whose extra phases add force-time to every grasp: squeeze 12.91, ramp-down 1.444, return 0.3480 and re-hold 3.27 N·s, on top of the shared preload (Section 5.2). On absolute excess force at matched slip (mean F − F\*_mid: CSF-1 2.359 N, plug-in 1.800 N, shrunk 1.386 N, learned 1.320 N), the plug-in beats CSF-1 by D_abs 0.2372 [0.1401, 0.3328] and the learned model beats the plug-in by 0.2667 [0.1980, 0.3196], about as much as the probe's own gain on this metric; the shrunk plug-in against the learned model gives −0.05046 [−0.1226, 0.01035], close to it. On the CRC route the absolute-excess rows agree in sign (plug-in vs CSF-1 0.2670 [0.09526, 0.3705]; learned vs plug-in 0.1911 [0.1194, 0.3349]; learned vs CSF-1 0.4071 [0.3283, 0.5087]). So the C2 equivalence is a property of the relative metric: by relative excess the probe carries the saving, by absolute excess the learner (or a shrinkage of the raw onset estimate) adds about as much again. On the correlated pool the family prior also wins on absolute excess (plug-in vs CSF-fam −0.6644 [−0.8376, −0.5043], learned vs CSF-fam −0.3236 [−0.4537, −0.1945], matched). The confirmed saving is against a friction-blind prior: when friction class is visible, a one-number family prior beats a σ 0.2 probe (no hybrid prior + probe arm was tested).

### 5.2 Boundary map: noise, friction range, hold time

**The saving shrinks and then reverses as probe noise rises or the friction range narrows, and it repays the probe's force-time only for longer holds.**

*σ band (S1, SEC; Fig. 2a).* D(plug-in vs CSF-1) is 0.6486 [0.6100, 0.6733] / 0.4314 / −0.2065 [−0.3280, −0.1112] at σ 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.4; at σ 0.4 the plug-in's test slip is 0.1014, above α. Learned versus plug-in is −0.1076 [−0.1819, −0.02922] at σ 0.1 (learned worse; TOST p 0.5445) and 0.3238 [0.2778, 0.3817] at σ 0.4 (learned better), so C2's equivalence is a σ 0.2 result. The reversal at σ 0.4 is the plug-in's alone: on the test-slip-matched route the learned model's RE is 0.2926 / 0.4227 / 0.6134 at σ 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.4 against CSF-1's 0.7518 (the plug-in's is 0.2642 / 0.4275 / 0.9071), and on the CRC route the learned model still beats CSF-1 at σ 0.4 (0.1675 [0.1064, 0.2560]). At high probe noise the learner, not the raw probe estimate, carries the saving. An earlier round found the opposite at σ 0.4 on the first-round pool, where a shrunk plug-in beat the learned model (−0.1395 [−0.1832, −0.01264]); the cause of the difference is not tested.

*Friction-width sweep (S3, SEC, matched route; Fig. 2b).* Restricting the fresh pool to centred friction windows of relative log-width w, D(plug-in vs CSF-1) runs 0.4314 → 0.2414 → −0.1094 → −0.6967 for w = 1.0 → 0.75 → 0.5 → 0.25 (CI\*\* [−0.2383, 0.01393] at w 0.5; [−0.9507, −0.4238] at w 0.25, with 134 calibration / 179 test objects); the learned model gives 0.4377 / 0.2793 / −0.01792 / −0.5039. The sweep crosses 0 between w 0.75 and w 0.5. The registered directional prediction (D shrinks with w, with D ≤ 0 expected near w 0.25) is met by the point estimates; the sweep has no confirmatory test. CSF-1's matched RE falls from 0.7518 to 0.2307 while the plug-in stays between 0.3915 and 0.4275, so the decline is driven mainly by CSF-1 improving. We hypothesise, but did not test, that this reflects friction spread relative to probe noise; the trend matches the direction of the earlier mid-friction, mid-amplitude sub-pool (Section 5.4), whose sign is grid-dependent, with no mechanism claimed.

*Computed break-even (S6, DESC, CRC point; Fig. 2c).* Integrated force-time is computed in closed form (hold force × hold time plus the fixed phase integrals), not measured over simulated long holds as the registration's wording ("measured break-even") suggested (Deviation 8). ### 5.3 How the certificate behaves

**Over 200 re-splits of the fresh pool, mean slip stays below α for every arm, but the bound is marginal: single strata, one object family and the lowest-friction objects often exceed α.**

Under CRC (S7, SEC), across 200 fresh-pool re-splits, 31.5–70 % have at least one stratum above α (Table 2); LTT is more conservative. On the single correlated-pool split (S4, DESC, no intervals), family F1 exceeds α for three of four arms (CSF-1 0.3157, learned 0.2014, plug-in 0.1143); in F2–F4 all arms are ≤ 0.09, and only CSF-fam stays ≤ α in every family. Per family, the plug-in against CSF-fam gives −0.4441 / −0.3420 / −0.5557 / −0.7373 (F1–F4); the learned model's F1 value of 0.1102 comes at slip 0.2014, not a like-for-like gain. The first-round label monotonicity gate failed (8 / 800), but the label-versus-physical check passed in all four arm × α combinations. The shrunk plug-in (S2, SEC) improves on the raw plug-in; whether the gain exceeds the SESOI is unresolved.

*Slip by friction tercile (PH, post hoc, not pre-registered).* "Marginal" here means that the bound averages over the friction distribution of the pool: it promises E[slip] ≤ α for an object drawn like the calibration objects, not for an object of a given friction. Splitting the 2,800 fresh test instances into thirds by true μ (edges 0.4457 and 0.6553) at each arm's CRC point shows where the slips go (Table 2). Every arm concentrates slip on the low-friction third, and the concentration is strongest for CSF-1 (0.264 in the low tercile, 0.000 in the other two) and for the learned model (0.173 / 0.062 / 0.009); the plug-in spreads slip most evenly (0.131 / 0.088 / 0.075), and the shrunk plug-in resembles the learned model (0.185 / 0.058 / 0.007). For the friction-blind baseline this is expected: one calibrated multiplier is tight for slippery objects and generous for grippy ones. For the tactile arms it qualifies the absolute-force result of Section 5.1: the pattern is consistent with the learned model and the shrunk plug-in saving newtons mostly on slippery, high-force objects, which they hold closer to their need and which therefore slip more often than under the raw plug-in, while grippy objects get more margin (we did not test this mechanism). All four arms meet the marginal bound; none meets α within the low-friction third. A friction-conditional (Mondrian by μ̂) calibration would trade force for that, and was not run.

### 5.4 Supporting attribution and competitors from earlier rounds

**On the first-round pool the same picture appears: a tuned margin lands where CRC does, the registered reactive design fails, and in a mid-friction, mid-amplitude sub-pool the sign of the learned model's comparison with the baseline depends on the calibration grid.**

The pre-registered learned-versus-plug-in contrast (H2b, PRE) was D 0.0569 [−0.0399, 0.1454], not confirmed (TOST not met); C2 now answers it at σ 0.2 on the matched route. In the feature chain (SEC), adding the onset gives D 0.4739 [0.4148, 0.5229] on the label route; on the CRC route the full model against the onset-only model gives −0.0534 [−0.1233, 0.0508]; and on the label route there is no evidence that the squeeze helps (−0.0263). The routes differ, so these rows are not one additive decomposition. Against an oracle-side friction prior of log-noise σ_p 0.2, no advantage is shown on the CRC route with the refined grid (−0.0299 [−0.1903, 0.0816]), consistent in direction with C4 / C5. A per-stratum margin tuned on calibration data (SEC) reproduces the plug-in's CRC test point: at 500 calibration grasps per stratum, CRC adds the finite-sample guarantee, not a different operating point. The registered reactive slip-tightening design (DESC, negative control) fails, slipping at ≥ 0.3086 in every arm and stratum; its probe variant uses a raw μ̂ with log bias +0.3186, so the failure is at least partly a design artefact, and we make no claim against reactive control in general. The mid-friction sub-pool (H2-R, SEC) restricts the first-round pool to μ ∈ [0.5, 0.8] *and* to disturbance amplitudes inside each stratum's inter-quartile range, recalibrates inside the sub-pool, and compares the learned model (not the plug-in) with CSF-1. On the label route D = −0.2614 (Holm p 0.03 within its family, 0.12 over all 52 first-round secondaries); on the CRC route it is −0.3152 on the refined grid but +0.3754 on the primary grid. The sign therefore depends on the grid, slip is unmatched, and no mechanism is claimed.

### 5.5 Online calibration under shift

**Frozen calibration breaks under friction and amplitude shift. Binary-feedback tracking returns slip near α after a transient, and the probe arm keeps its force advantage; efficiency against oracle recalibration stays inconclusive.**

With label feedback (PRE, 20 orderings), the frozen probe arm slips at 0.3056 (friction shift) and 0.2461 (amplitude shift), the frozen baseline at 0.5101 (friction shift); tracking holds label slip at 0.0999–0.1016. H3 is inconclusive under its registered rule (CI\*\* upper limit of the efficiency ratio R_E = RE(tracking)/RE(oracle recalibration) below the registered non-inferiority margin, 10 % above parity, in all three shifts). ## 6 Limitations and Discussion

**What the evidence licenses.** In simulation, with friction independent of what the robot can see and at σ 0.2, a ramp-down probe plus a CRC-calibrated one-line plug-in lowers relative excess hold force against a friction-blind conformal baseline, with pooled slip near α on average; at σ 0.2 one trained learned model is not needed for *relative* excess on the registered test-slip-matched route (it is not shown equivalent on the CRC route). The attribution is metric-dependent: in absolute excess force (post hoc) the learned model beats the plug-in by about as much as the plug-in beats CSF-1, and a two-parameter shrinkage of the raw estimate recovers most of that, so "the probe, not the learner" holds for relative excess only. In newtons the saving is modest (0.66 N, 9.3 %, for the plug-in; 1.01 N, 14.2 %, for the learned model; post hoc). The saving disappears when friction is family-predictable, for the plug-in at σ 0.4 (matched route; on the CRC route the learned model keeps a saving, and on the matched route it beats the plug-in), as the friction range narrows, and, by closed-form force-time accounting, for holds shorter than 17.74 s (learned) to 27.07 s (plug-in). Slip over 17–27 s holds at the certified force is untested: the certificate covers a 0.6 s disturbance window. Hence a conditional reading only: *if* the certified force stays safe over long holds, probing pays in force-time when friction is not predictable from appearance and holds exceed the break-even, and a learned model or shrinkage helps if the probe is noisy or if newtons, rather than relative excess, are the cost; establishing that condition needs long-hold simulation or hardware.

**Limits.** (1) *Simulation only; σ assumed:* one simulator, rigid contact, a modelled detector; the sign changes across σ 0.1 → 0.4 (0.6486 → −0.2065), and σ was not grounded on hardware or published detector error. σ is noise *added* to a modelled taxel detector whose own log friction error has SD 0.1103 at no added noise, so the total probe error is larger than the nominal σ and its relation to a real sensor is unknown. (2) *Stylised correlated pool:* one band layout, one σ, and no hybrid prior + probe arm, so C4 / C5 do not show that touch is useless where a prior exists. (3) *Width sweep descriptive:* one σ, no family prior, small samples at w 0.25. (4) *Marginal guarantee:* over the pool, in expectation; 31.5–70 % of re-splits have a stratum above α, on the single correlated-pool split F1 exceeds α for three of four arms, and on the fresh pool every arm exceeds α in the lowest-friction tercile (post hoc; CSF-1 0.264, learned 0.173, plug-in 0.131), so slippery objects carry most of the risk; LTT is the conservative alternative, and no friction-conditional calibration was run. (5) *Deployability:* offline CRC needs replays at every grid force; the online route has ordering-only intervals, overshoot to 0.1389 and no physical-replay oracle. (6) *Fixed high-force protocol:* the 32.7 N preload exceeds nearly every instance's F\*_mid (not all: a few need slightly more), the squeeze peak exceeds every one, and together they dominate force-time; no adaptive preload was run, so the work concerns hold force, not first-contact force. (7) *Competitors:* published controllers \cite{khamis2021real, dong2019maintaining, boonstra2024estimate, niu2026synchronized} were not reproduced; the reactive design is a negative control, not re-designed. (8) *Multiplicity:* Holm covers C1–C5 and the first-round secondaries; S1–S7, later-round and post hoc (PH) rows are unadjusted, and the break-even has no interval and is a closed-form computation (Deviation 8), not a measurement. (9) *Long holds not simulated:* slip is evaluated over a 0.6 s disturbance window; the 17.74–27.07 s break-even assumes the certified force stays safe for the whole hold, which creep, repeated disturbances or soft-contact effects could violate. (10) *Known inputs and one model:* every rule receives the true mass, the commanded acceleration and the disturbance stratum; noisy mass or an unknown stratum would change all arms. All confirmatory learned-model rows use one frozen training seed, while the first round's seed spread on the C3 contrast (0.3932–0.4494) is more than half the SESOI, so C2 is a statement about this fitted model. A probe-only protocol without the squeeze and a family prior + probe hybrid were not run. Per-family slip is reported for the correlated pool (S4) and slip by friction tercile for the fresh pool (post hoc, Section 5.3); fresh-pool per-family slip is not reported. (11) *Metric dependence:* the registered metric is relative excess; the post hoc absolute-excess rows reverse the learner's role (it adds about as much as the probe), so which component "carries" the saving depends on whether the cost is relative or in newtons, and neither metric includes the probe's fixed force-time.

**Future work.** A hybrid family prior + probe rule, friction-conditional (Mondrian by μ̂) calibration, probe-noise measurement on hardware with an adaptive preload, a probe-only protocol without the squeeze, long-hold simulation at the certified force, learned-model seeds beyond seed 0, per-family (Mondrian) calibration with its force cost, and replay-free online calibration.

## 7 Conclusion

In simulation, a friction-onset probe with conformal risk control on physical slip lowers relative excess hold force against the strongest friction-blind conformal baseline under a marginal, in-expectation bound (C1, matched route, D 0.4314 [0.3824, 0.4835], CRC route 0.4482; C3, CRC route, 0.4135). In newtons the gain is modest: 0.66 N (9.3 %) for the plug-in and 1.01 N (14.2 %) for the learned model (post hoc). At σ 0.2 and on relative excess at matched slip, the probe, not the learner, carries the saving (C2 equivalent for one trained model; not shown on the CRC route). On absolute excess force, a post hoc metric, the learned model beats the plug-in (0.2667, matched route), so that attribution holds for relative excess only. At σ 0.4 the learner matters on relative excess too: the plug-in's saving reverses (matched route −0.2065), while the learned model keeps one on the CRC route (0.1675) and beats the plug-in on the matched route (0.3238). A family prior wins when friction is visible (C4, matched route, −0.5814), and the saving shrinks and reverses as friction narrows. The bound is marginal in a concrete sense: slip concentrates on low-friction objects for every arm (post hoc). By closed-form force-time accounting, the probe pays for itself only in holds beyond 27.07 s (plug-in) or 17.74 s (learned model), a regime whose slip was not simulated, so any advice to probe for long holds is conditional on a long-hold test. Online tracking is consistent with convergence to α; efficiency is inconclusive, and in the family shift tracking needs more excess force than oracle recalibration.
