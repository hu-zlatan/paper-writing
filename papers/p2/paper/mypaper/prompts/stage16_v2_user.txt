## 3 Problem Formulation and Method (≈ 800 words)
- **Goal:** Give a self-contained technical description. Every symbol used in Results should be defined here. The section describes the approach, not a workflow.
- **3.1 Problem (≈ 150 w).**
  - Instance x: object, pose, and disturbance type D1–D4.
  - F\*(x) is the minimal no-slip force, defined by a slip criterion: 2 mm tangential displacement, 3° rotation, or contact loss.
  - Goal: choose F(x) to minimise E[(F − F\*)/F\*] subject to P(slip at F(x)) ≤ α.
  - Labels: F\*_lo / F\*_hi / F\*_mid come from 11-replay log-space bisection on [0.1, 100] N. They exist only in simulation, which is why §3.4 matters.
- **3.2 Grasp protocol and friction-onset probe (≈ 200 w).**
  - Fixed preload F₀ = 32.7 N, the same for every instance, so it encodes neither m nor μ. Lift and hold. Squeeze F₀ → 1.5 F₀ → F₀.
  - **Ramp-down probe:** decrease force at a fixed rate. A touch-only detector watches the shear/normal ratio of the pad touch grids and stops at incipient slip, giving F_onset. A Coulomb reading gives μ̂ = m g / (2 F_onset).
  - Onset noise model: log F_onset + σ ε, with σ ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4}. The primary level is σ 0.2, and σ 0 is only an upper bound. Then ramp to the chosen F and run the disturbance window. A slip during the probe counts as a slip.
  - Hardware-availability table (one compact table, or inline): which inputs each arm receives.
- **3.3 Predictors and baselines (≈ 150 w).**
  - Gradient-boosted quantile predictor ĝ(x) with feature sets P (proprioception) ⊂ S (+ static touch) ⊂ full (+ squeeze) ⊂ CertGrip-full (+ onset). Onset-only = P + onset.
  - Physics plug-ins:
    - CSF-1: F = F_nom · e^q̂, with F_nom = m(g + a_cmd)/(2 μ̄).
    - Mondrian-CSF: one q̂ per family × disturbance cell. It is given privileged family ID.
    - CSF-μ̂: F = m(g + a_cmd)/(2 μ̂) · e^q̂, using the same noisy onset as CertGrip-full.
  - COMP is selected on the selection split before any calibration or test access.
- **3.4 Conformal risk control on the physical binary loss (≈ 200 w).**
  - Force family: F_i(λ) = min(F_max, ĝ(x_i) e^λ). Loss: L_i(λ) = 1{physical slip in direct replay}. Monotonised loss: L̃_i(λ) = max_{λ' ≥ λ} L_i(λ').
  - Per disturbance stratum, choose λ̂ = min{λ ∈ Λ : (n R̃_n(λ) + 1)/(n + 1) ≤ α} (`angelopoulos2022conformal`). This gives E[slip] ≤ α for an exchangeable test instance.
  - Λ is a 20-point grid centred on the selection-split quantile.
  - Motivation: H1-M (the label-rule monotonicity gate) failed. There were 8 non-monotone instances of 800, CP upper limit 0.02115 > 0.02, and 7 of the 8 were in D2. So the plain split-conformal certificate on labels is not guaranteed, and CRC on the physical loss is the valid route.
  - The label-rule split-conformal version (CQR scores, `romano2019conformalized`) is reported as a secondary route.
- **3.5 Online calibration from binary feedback (≈ 100 w).**
  - Score-space online quantile tracking (OQT, `angelopoulos2023conformala`): q_{t+1} = q_t + η (slip_t − α). There is one tracker per stratum, initialised at the offline q̂. η = 0.03412 (frozen). Anti-windup applies at F_max.

1. **CertGrip vs the friction-blind baseline (CSF-1) is robust across every v2 route.** The v1 H2a superiority
   (D 0.4168, CI\*\* [0.3483, 0.4777]) is reproduced by the matched-slip frontier (0.4563 on 08c, 0.4475 on 08e), by
   the high-probability LTT certificate (0.4042 / 0.4014) and by the 08e CRC route (0.4289). Every CI\*\* lower bound
   is above 0; the smallest across all A rows is 0.2952 (D_ltt CSF-muhat, 08c).

2. **CertGrip vs friction-informed baselines: no advantage is shown, in v1 or v2.** v1 H2b was not confirmed
   (D 0.0569, CI\*\* [-0.0399, 0.1453]). In v2, at matched slip 0.1 the CSF-muhat@0.2 RE (0.4190 on 08c, 0.4045 on 08e)
   is at or below the CertGrip RE (0.4290 / 0.4292), with overlapping intervals, and D_match vs CSF-1 is 0.4689 /

   CI\*\* [-0.1903, 0.0815]). No direct matched-slip or LTT contrast CertGrip vs CSF-muhat was computed, so this is
   a reading of separate intervals, not a test.

3. **The v1 H2b point comparison mixed slip levels.** At k = 0 on the 08c grids CertGrip ran at test slip 0.0828 and
   CSF-muhat@0.2 at 0.0657 (λ̂ at grid index 0 in D1 / D2). Matching slip removes the apparent 0.0569 CertGrip edge:
   the two RE_match values are close (0.4290 vs 0.4190) with overlapping CI\*\* ([0.4099, 0.4559] vs [0.4005, 0.4410]).

   arm has RE_match 0.4153 / 0.4173 against 0.4290 / 0.4292 for CertGrip-full, and its D vs CSF-1 is the larger one

## 4. Limitations

- Simulation only (MuJoCo 4.0.0); the onset-noise σ is not grounded on hardware (amendment C).
- All v2 results are secondary; they cannot overturn v1 H2b (not confirmed) or v1 H3 (inconclusive).
- No direct CertGrip vs CSF-muhat contrast at matched slip or under LTT was registered or computed; the "no tactile
  advantage" reading rests on overlapping separate intervals plus v1 H2b and H5@0.2.
- Matched slip uses one target (0.1) by linear interpolation between adjacent k; shape of the frontier away from that
  point is only described.
- CSF-proxy is an oracle-side construction (uses true μ through a noisy proxy), so H5 calibrates "how good a friction
  prior must be" rather than comparing against a deployable estimator.
- LTT guarantees hold per stratum at δ = 0.1, not family-wise across strata.
- B2 is one replay per instance per margin, with no interval.
- B3: 5 orderings (v1 H3 used 20), S1 and S3 only, no interval; window slip ranges overlap α, so "holds α" is a
  descriptive statement.
- 08c and 08e grids give slightly different point values (e.g. CSF-muhat RE_match 0.4190 vs 0.4045); conclusions are
  stated only where both agree.

---

## 5. Conclusion

- The efficiency gain of calibrated tactile grip selection over a friction-blind CSF-1 is robust: superiority on the
  CRC, matched-slip and LTT routes, on both grid recipes (D from 0.4014 to 0.4563 for CertGrip-full).
- The gain is not specific to tactile sensing: a friction estimate of σ 0.2 quality (CSF-muhat, or the non-tactile
  CSF-proxy at σ_p 0.2) matches CertGrip at matched slip (RE 0.4190 vs 0.4290) and H5@0.2 is not shown
  (CI\*\* [-0.0673, 0.1659]; edge rule [-0.1903, 0.0815]). CertGrip is superior only to proxies of σ_p 0.4 or worse
  (D 0.5042, 0.8360).
- Onset-only is as efficient as the full pipeline (RE_match 0.4153 vs 0.4290).
- Calibration itself is what delivers per-stratum risk control: fixed margins miss α in every stratum
  (B2), while CRC / LTT and the online physical-feedback tracker keep slip near 0.1.
- Recommended framing for the paper: CertGrip as a calibration layer that turns any friction signal (tactile onset
  included) into a slip-rate guarantee, not as evidence that tactile features beat a good friction prior.

3. **H1 is not confirmed** because H1-M failed by one instance (8 vs ≤ 7; CP upper 0.02115 vs 0.02). The violations are
   small (≤ 0.9 % of F_hi) and concentrated in D2 (7 / 8). This triggered the CRC validity route for H2 (08c).
4. **H3 is inconclusive in all three shifts**, as pre-registered (08b §5: reduced power, half-width ≈ 0.12). Point

   (window slip 0.0751 vs OQT 0.1016), so OQT spends less force; in S2 the oracle under-protects (0.1346), so it looks
   cheaper than OQT. R_E is therefore not a like-for-like efficiency comparison in S1 and S2.

### 2.2 Secondary (what explains H2a)
- **The gain comes from the ramp-down onset probe, not from static touch or the squeeze.** H4a: S vs P equivalence
  (D −0.0084), squeeze adds nothing (D −0.0263; not confirmed only because the CI lower −0.0558 passes −0.03), onset adds
  D 0.4739. H4e: CertGrip-full vs onset-only is not distinguishable (08c −0.0534, label route equivalence); the point
  estimates favour onset-only. H4c: shuffled touch gives RE 0.8029 vs P 0.7831 (no gain). D-loc: only the onset arm's
  prediction moves under the friction shift (mean |Δĝ|/ĝ 0.4469 vs ≤ 0.0019 for P / S / full).
- **The gain is a friction-information effect.** D-μ: no-probe arms leave R² 0.60–0.79 of log μ in their residuals;
  CertGrip-full leaves 0.0105 / 0.0452 / 0.2142 / 0.5655 at σ 0 / 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.4. H2-M (no mass) leaves D unchanged
  (change 0.0042), as does 10 % mass noise (change 0.0312, CI includes 0). H4b: adding family one-hot keeps D_FID 0.4638
  vs D 0.4555 — family identity does not explain the gain, although touch-only features identify family well (D-FID
  0.7257 vs chance 0.25).
- **Where it fails:** H2-R. In the restricted sub-pool (μ ∈ [0.5, 0.8], mid-amplitude disturbances) CertGrip is worse
  than CSF-1: D −0.2614, CI\*\* [−0.5320, −0.0325] (upper < 0). No prediction was registered, but the paper must report
  it: when friction varies little, the probe's information is worth less than its noise, and the fixed-friction
  plug-in wins. The sub-pool is small (n_cal 352, n_test 548), hence the wide interval.
- **Noise dependence (σ curve, H2a):** D 0.6789 / 0.5825 / 0.4168 / 0.1401 at σ 0 / 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.4 (08c); σ\* = 0.4
  (lower limit 0.0832 < 0.10; 08e 0.0868, label 0.0983 — σ\* = 0.4 on every route). At σ 0.4 the decision label is
  still "superiority" (D 0.1401 ≥ 0.10, lower > 0).
- **Learned vs plug-in by σ (H2b σ curve; secondary at σ ≠ 0.2):** on the two unbiased routes the plug-in wins at
  σ 0 (inferiority) and at σ 0.1 (label route inferiority; 08e not confirmed), the two tie at σ 0.2, and the learned arm
  wins at σ 0.4 (D 0.3275–0.3315; CSF-μ̂ RE 0.9371–0.9876). The D-μ values are consistent with an explanation (an
  interpretation, not a tested claim): the plug-in uses μ̂ at face value (residual R² with log μ ≈ 0.003–0.006 at every
  σ, but RE grows with σ), while the learned arm shrinks the noisy onset feature, leaving friction in the residual
  (R² up to 0.5655) but with lower RE at high noise.
- **Tail and safety secondaries pass:** H2-T T 0.5918 (upper 0.6564 < 1.10); H2-H guard no flagged cell; H2-n
  confirmed at both α.
- **Oracle gap:** CertGrip closes 0.5172 [0.4489, 0.5762] of the CSF-1 → oracle RE gap (oracle RE 0.1271).
- **H4d (CoM mechanism) is inconclusive** for both arms (CIs include 0).
- **Online adaptation (H3 secondaries):** OQT restores window slip to ≈ α in every shift (0.0999–0.1016) while frozen
  arms break (Frozen-CertGrip S1 0.3056, S3 0.2461; Frozen-COMP S1 0.5101). OQT-CertGrip keeps its RE advantage over

## 4. Limitations

- **Simulation only** (MuJoCo 4.0.0) with a modelled tactile detector; the detector constants were tuned on pilot
  objects 0–24, and at σ 0 the onset estimate is near-perfect (pilot corr 0.988), which is why σ 0 is only an upper
  bound. The primary σ 0.2 is an assumed noise level, and the advantage shrinks to D 0.1401 (lower 0.0832) at σ 0.4.
- **H1 failed**, so validity rests on the post-failure CRC route (08c) — designed after the H1-M counts were seen but
  before any predictor or test statistic existed.
- **The 08c CRC grid had a defect** that biases the CRC-route H2b towards CertGrip; the fix (08e) is post hoc.
- **H2b is unresolved:** the pre-registered route cannot separate "no difference" from a gain of up to ≈ 0.145, and
  equivalence on the post hoc route is missed by 0.0001 on the upper limit.
- **H3 was underpowered by design** and its matched-rate co-requirement fails in S1 and S2; the stream feedback is the
  label rule, with direct-replay audits only for ordering 0.
- **H2-R shows a regime where CertGrip loses** (narrow friction range); the overall gain depends on the friction spread
  of the object distribution.
- **Several secondaries carry stated deviations** (H3-B probe arm untested; D-μ on cal + test instead of train; RAW-Qv
  log-shift variant; H1c small-n arm dropped; H2-M mass-noise draw fixed only in 08h).
- **Secondaries are unadjusted** for multiplicity.
- The violations in H1-M are concentrated in D2 (7 / 8), which the files do not explain.

---

## 5. Conclusion

Of the four pre-registered core decisions, only **H2a** is confirmed: CertGrip-full at σ 0.2 needs substantially less
excess grip force than the best non-tactile comparator CSF-1 (D 0.4168, CI\*\* [0.3484, 0.4778]). The result holds on
every robustness, route, seed, cell and comparator check in the files. The secondaries show where the gain comes from:
the ramp-down onset probe's friction estimate. Static touch and the squeeze add nothing (H4a, H4c, H4e), mass and
family identity do not explain it (H2-M, H4b), and the gain falls with onset noise (σ\* = 0.4) and turns negative when
friction varies little (H2-R, D −0.2614). **H2b is not confirmed:** learned touch does not beat a physics plug-in using
the same onset estimate (08c D 0.0569, CI\*\* [−0.0399, 0.1454]). Equivalence appears only on the secondary label route
and is missed narrowly on the post hoc grid (upper 0.1001), so it may not be claimed. **H1 failed** narrowly (8 / 800
non-monotone vs ≤ 7), with a small and conservative label-to-physics gap (H1-V pass). **H3 is inconclusive** in S1, S2
and S3, as the reduced-power design expected, and the matched-rate requirement fails in S1 and S2. Online OQT still
restores slip to ≈ α under shift where the frozen arms break. The defensible headline is: "a tactile friction probe
plus conformal calibration cuts the relative excess force of a calibrated physics baseline by D ≈ 0.42. The probe's onset
estimate carries the friction information, and a learned model adds no demonstrated value over a plug-in that uses the
same estimate."
