# When Does a Friction-Onset Probe Pay Off? Conformal Slip-Risk Control of Grip Hold Force, Mapped in Simulation

## Abstract

We choose a grip *hold* force whose physical slip rate is at most α; without friction information, a conformal rule must cover the whole friction range. After a fixed preload, a ramp-down probe reads friction from the incipient-slip onset, a one-line Coulomb plug-in (or a learned model) turns it into a force, and conformal risk control on the binary physical-slip loss calibrates it per disturbance stratum: certified slip as a marginal, in-expectation bound, in simulation. In pre-registered tests on fresh objects, the plug-in lowers relative excess hold force against the strongest friction-blind conformal baseline by D = 0.4314 (CI [0.3824, 0.4835]); a learned model adds nothing at the assumed onset noise (equivalent within ±0.10). The saving reverses when friction is predictable from the visible object family (a family prior wins, D −0.5814), at high probe noise (−0.2065) and as the friction range narrows; it repays the probe's force-time only for holds beyond 17.74 s. The bound is marginal (single strata exceed α in up to 70 % of re-splits); in a narrow mid-friction band the baseline was better in earlier rounds. Online, binary-feedback tracking returns slip near α after shift; efficiency versus oracle recalibration is inconclusive.

## 1 Introduction

A robot holding an object must choose a grip force. Force above need wastes actuation and loads the object for the whole hold; force below need lets it slip. Humans scale grip to friction after brief contact \cite{johansson1984roles} and re-regulate when friction changes \cite{willemet2024grip}. Tactile controllers react to incipient slip \cite{dong2019maintaining, veiga2020grip, berman2025autonomous}, estimate friction online \cite{khamis2021real, niu2026synchronized} or learn a gentle-grasp margin \cite{boonstra2024estimate}, but none states a distribution-free bound on the resulting slip rate. We study a narrower problem than minimal-force grasping: choosing the *hold* force after a fixed, high-force probe protocol.

Conformal risk control certifies any force rule from a held-out calibration set \cite{angelopoulos2022conformal, lei2017distribution}. A rule that cannot see friction, however, must cover the whole friction range with one calibrated multiplier. The fair comparator for a tactile method is therefore the *strongest* friction-blind conformal baseline, chosen beforehand on a disjoint selection split: CSF-1 (mean relative excess 0.7686) beat a Mondrian variant (0.8294) and a learned proprioceptive model (0.8335). Where friction is predictable from the visible object family, we also compare against a deployable family prior.

We ask *when a short tactile probe makes a distribution-free physical-slip bound cheaper in hold force, and which component carries the saving*: (i) does the probe lower hold force at certified physical slip ≤ α (in simulation, marginal over the pool, in expectation), and is a learned model needed? (ii) Under which conditions (probe noise, friction range, friction-informative prior, hold duration) does the saving hold or disappear? (iii) Does calibration recover under shift when the only feedback is whether the object slipped?

**Contributions.**
- **(C-i) Method.** A protocol in which a friction-uninformative preload leaves the ramp-down onset as the only friction channel; per-stratum CRC on the monotonised physical binary slip loss (the label rule is not exactly monotone, 8 of 800) turns a one-line Coulomb plug-in on that onset into a rule with marginal expected slip ≤ α in simulation.
- **(C-ii) Confirmatory evaluation on fresh objects.** Five pre-registered, Holm-corrected decisions: the plug-in beats the baseline (C1); the learned model is equivalent to the plug-in at onset noise σ 0.2 on the registered matched-slip route (C2); the earlier result replicates (C3); on a friction-correlated pool a family prior beats both tactile arms (C4, C5).
- **(C-iii) Boundary map.** A σ band, a friction-width sweep, a break-even against a simulated no-probe grasp, a 200-re-split check of the certificate, and an online binary-feedback study under three shifts.

Three of five confirmatory predictions are met; the two unpredicted rows (C4, C5) are not superior, with intervals below 0. The earlier pre-registered learned-versus-plug-in and label-validity decisions were not confirmed, and online efficiency is inconclusive.

## 2 Related Work

**Friction reading and tactile grip control.** Reading friction from incipient slip is established \cite{marconi1996incipient}; reviews treat incipient slip as the key friction cue \cite{chen2018tactile, romeo2020methods}, and human grip adapts to friction \cite{johansson1984roles, willemet2024grip}. Robotic controllers detect slip and tighten \cite{james2020slip, grover2022detect, ayral2026reactive}, keep grasps within slipping bounds \cite{dong2019maintaining}, estimate friction with a safety margin \cite{khamis2021real, niu2026synchronized}, learn incipient-slip margins or force policies \cite{boonstra2024estimate, kuo2025tracing, feng2026tactilereflex, lach2024zero}, or stabilise grasps with adaptive or independent-finger control \cite{almohammed2022switched, veiga2020grip}. Pre-grasp force can also be chosen from prior experience \cite{shang2026exp}, which relates to our family-prior result. None of these gives a coverage guarantee on slip rate.

**Robust grasping under uncertainty.** Force optimisation under parameter uncertainty \cite{cloutier2018examining}, control barrier functions \cite{cortez2019control, kim2024robust}, friction-informed risk margins \cite{enwerem2026firmgrasp} and belief parameterisations \cite{enwerem2026variational} bound grasp risk relative to a model and its assumed uncertainty set. Our bound needs no friction model but is marginal and needs calibration data.

**Conformal prediction and risk control.** Conformal methods give finite-sample guarantees under exchangeability \cite{vovk2005algorithmic, romano2019conformalized, angelopoulos2023conformal, bian2023training, ding2023class}; CRC extends them to monotone losses \cite{angelopoulos2022conformal}, non-monotone losses \cite{angelopoulos2026conformal} and high-probability control \cite{angelopoulos2025learn}. Online variants track coverage under shift \cite{gibbs2021adaptive, gibbs2022conformal, angelopoulos2023conformala, angelopoulos2024online, farinhas2023non, ge2024stochastic}. In robotics they certify decisions, planning, verification and perception \cite{lekeufack2024conformal, lindemann2025formal, dixit2022adaptive, marques2026particle, lindemann2023conformal, ren2023robots, huang2023conformal, strawn2023conformal, zhang2024bayesian, zhao2024conformalized, kumar2026learnable}. None calibrates a contact outcome as a binary loss in force space.

**Novelty.** Onset-based friction reading, CRC, Learn-then-Test (LTT) and quantile tracking are used as published. What is new is (i) a protocol in which the onset is the only friction channel, so that its value can be isolated; (ii) a marginal bound on the *physical* slip rate of the resulting hold force, in simulation; and (iii) confirmatory tests that locate the saving in the probe and map where it disappears.

## 3 Problem Formulation and Method

### 3.1 Problem

An instance x comprises an object (family, geometry, mass m, friction μ), a pose offset and a disturbance stratum d ∈ {D1, …, D4}. Slip is a tangential displacement above 2 mm, a rotation above 3° or loss of contact. The minimal no-slip hold force F\*(x) is bracketed by 11-replay log-space bisection on [0.1, 100] N into F\*_lo, F\*_hi and F\*_mid = √(F\*_lo F\*_hi), labels that exist only in simulation. With relative excess RE(F) = E_x[(F(x) − F\*_mid(x))/F\*_mid(x)] of the hold force, the task is

$$\min_F \ \mathrm{RE}(F) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbb{P}(\mathrm{slip\ at\ } F(x)) \le \alpha .$$

Two rules are compared by D(a vs b) = 1 − RE(a)/RE(b); D > 0 means that a needs less excess force than b.

### 3.2 Grasp protocol and friction-onset probe

Every instance receives the same preload F₀ = 32.7 N, which encodes neither m nor μ. The gripper lifts and holds, squeezes F₀ → 1.5 F₀ → F₀, and runs the **ramp-down probe**: the force falls at a fixed rate until a touch-only detector on the pad shear/normal ratio reports incipient slip, giving F_onset and μ̂ = m g/(2 F_onset). Simulated touch is clean, so we add onset noise, log F_onset + σε with ε ~ N(0, 1), σ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.4}; σ 0.2 is primary and assumed. The grip then ramps to the chosen hold force F; a slip during the probe counts. The protocol is fixed: preload and squeeze exceed every test instance's F\*_mid, the probe induces micro-slip by design, and the method chooses only the hold force. The **no-probe grasp** uses the same preload and skips the squeeze and probe phases.

### 3.3 Force rules

Every rule has the form F(x; λ) = min(F_max, F_base(x) e^λ), with λ set by calibration.
- **Probe plug-in:** F_base = m(g + a_cmd)/(2μ̂).
- **Shrunk plug-in:** log μ̂_s = a + b log μ̂, with ordinary least squares on the selection split only, frozen from the previous round; at σ 0.2, a = −0.3858 and b = 0.7397.
- **Learned probe model (option):** a gradient-boosted quantile predictor on proprioception, static touch, squeeze and onset features.
- **Friction-blind baseline CSF-1:** F_base = m(g + a_cmd)/(2μ̄) with one friction value μ̄, selected on the selection split before any calibration or test access.
- **Family prior CSF-fam (deployable, non-tactile):** the same formula with one μ̄ per visible family, the geometric-mean true μ of the family's 50 selection objects of the correlated pool (disjoint from calibration and test): F1 0.379, F2 0.496, F3 0.677, F4 0.852.
- **Earlier-round supporting arms:** onset-only learner; oracle-side friction prior μ̃ = clip(μ e^{σ_p ε_p}, 0.1, 3.0); probe + tuned per-stratum margin; reactive slip-tightening (registered negative control).

### 3.4 Calibration on the physical binary loss

For calibration instance i, the loss is L_i(λ) = 𝟙{physical slip in a direct replay at F(x_i; λ)}, monotonised on the grid Λ as L̃_i(λ) = max_{λ′ ≥ λ} L_i(λ′). Since L̃_i ≥ L_i, a bound on L̃ bounds physical slip.

**CRC (primary).** Per stratum, with R̃_n(λ) the mean of L̃_i over n calibration instances,

$$\hat\lambda = \min\Big\{\lambda \in \Lambda : \frac{n \tilde R_n(\lambda) + 1}{n + 1} \le \alpha\Big\}.$$

By \cite{angelopoulos2022conformal}, an exchangeable test instance then has E[slip] ≤ α. The bound is marginal over instances and friction and holds in expectation over the calibration draw; it is not conditional on a stratum realisation or object family.

**LTT (secondary).** λ is chosen so that the slip risk is ≤ α with probability ≥ 1 − δ, δ = 0.1, per stratum \cite{angelopoulos2025learn, bian2023training}.

**Why the physical loss.** The label route (split conformal on bisection labels \cite{romano2019conformalized}) transfers to physical slip only if slip is monotone in F. The monotonicity gate failed: 8 of 800 instances were non-monotone (Clopper–Pearson upper limit 0.02115 > 0.02; 7 of 8 in D2), so the label route is secondary.

**Deployability.** Offline CRC needs a replay of each calibration grasp at every grid force, so it is a simulation procedure; the online tracker needs one binary outcome per grasp.

```
Algorithm 1: probe, then calibrate (per arm, per stratum d)
1  every instance: preload F0, squeeze, ramp-down probe -> F_onset (+ noise sigma); F_base(x) uses it
2  for calibration i, lambda in Lambda: L[i,lambda] <- slip of replay at min(F_max, F_base(x_i) e^lambda)
3  Ltilde[i,lambda] <- max_{lambda' >= lambda} L[i,lambda']
4  lambda_hat <- first lambda with (sum_i Ltilde[i,lambda] + 1)/(n + 1) <= alpha
5  deploy: probe, then command F = min(F_max, F_base(x) e^lambda_hat)
```

### 3.5 Online calibration from binary feedback

Score-space quantile tracking \cite{angelopoulos2023conformala} updates one log-force offset per stratum, q_{t+1} = q_t + η(slip_t − α), with η = 0.03412 (frozen) and anti-windup at F_max; score space, rather than the level update of \cite{gibbs2021adaptive}, tolerates scores beyond the calibration support after a friction drop. References: a decaying step \cite{angelopoulos2024online}, frozen calibration, oracle recalibration.

## 4 Experimental Setup

**Simulator and objects.** MuJoCo 4.0.0, a parallel-jaw gripper and one touch grid per pad (on tactile simulation see \cite{wang2022tacto, akinola2025tacsl, leins2025hydroelastictouch, luu2025manifeel, birtalan2026impact}). Families: F1 boxes, F2 cylinders, F3 ellipsoids / capsules, F4 off-centre-of-mass composites; F5 thin plates / disks only under shift. The summed pad normal reading differs from F₀ by at most 0.1597 N, and no instance slipped during the preload.

**Pools.** The first-round pool draws μ log-uniform on [0.3, 1.0], independently of every non-tactile input, and m on [0.05, 1.0] kg (2,000 calibration / 2,800 test instances). The **fresh pool (independent friction)** repeats this design with new objects (500 calibration / 700 test objects) and carries C1–C3, S1–S3, S6 and S7. The **friction-correlated pool** draws friction in narrow, mostly non-overlapping family bands (500 / 700 objects) and carries C4, C5 and S4.

**Strata and shifts.** Strata: D1 lift acceleration, D2 shake, D3 lateral pull, D4 payload. Online shifts: friction (μ × 0.6), family (new F5) and amplitude (× 1.5), on streams of T = 2,000 steps, shift from step 501, 20 orderings.

**Statistics.** CI\*\* is a two-sided 97.5 % object-cluster bootstrap interval (2,000 draws, all valid, λ̂ refitted in each). Matched-slip RE is interpolated at slip 0.10, the registered route for C1, C2, C4 and C5; the smallest effect size of interest (SESOI) is 0.10. C1–C5 use Holm at 5 % over five p: superiority if the CI\*\* lower limit exceeds 0, equivalence (C2) by TOST within ±0.10. Every v4 row has `edge: false`, which we take, per the read-out code's flag, as no λ̂ at grid index 0 or 80. First-round core decisions used Bonferroni over 4, its 52 secondaries Holm within family (no verdict changes); S1–S7 and later-round rows are unadjusted.

**Deviations.** (1) The CRC route was written after the monotonicity counts were seen, before any predictor existed. (2) A first-round grid floor was handled by a post hoc refined grid (earlier-round rows only). (3) The first-round pre-registration misstates the selection objects once; the code uses 150–199. (4) Later-round analyses were registered after earlier read-outs and cannot change a first-round decision. (5) The registered physical-replay online oracle was not produced. (6) The confirmatory amendment was registered before any v4 code or run; its read-out code was committed before any v4 replay and run once. (7) C4 / C5 had no registered prediction; the rule defines only superiority there.

### 5.2 Boundary map: noise, friction range, hold time

**The saving shrinks and then reverses as probe noise rises or the friction range narrows, and it repays the probe's force-time only for longer holds.**

*σ band (S1, SEC; Fig. 2a).* D(plug-in vs CSF-1) is 0.6486 [0.6100, 0.6733] / 0.4314 / −0.2065 [−0.3280, −0.1112] at σ 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.4; at σ 0.4 the plug-in's test slip is 0.1014, above α. Learned versus plug-in is −0.1076 [−0.1819, −0.02922] at σ 0.1 (learned worse; TOST p 0.5445) and 0.3238 [0.2778, 0.3817] at σ 0.4 (learned better), so C2's equivalence is a σ 0.2 result. An earlier round found the opposite at σ 0.4 on the first-round pool, where a shrunk plug-in beat the learned model (−0.1395 [−0.1832, −0.01264]); the cause of the difference is not tested.

*Friction-width sweep (S3, SEC, descriptive; Fig. 2b).* Restricting the fresh pool to centred friction windows of relative log-width w, D(plug-in vs CSF-1) runs 0.4314 → 0.2414 → −0.1094 → −0.6967 for w = 1.0 → 0.75 → 0.5 → 0.25 (CI\*\* [−0.2383, 0.01393] at w 0.5; [−0.9507, −0.4238] at w 0.25, with 134 calibration / 179 test objects); the learned model gives 0.4377 / 0.2793 / −0.01792 / −0.5039. The sweep crosses 0 between w 0.75 and w 0.5. CSF-1's matched RE falls from 0.7518 to 0.2307 while the plug-in stays between 0.3915 and 0.4275, so the decline is driven mainly by CSF-1 improving. We hypothesise, but did not test, that this reflects friction spread relative to probe noise; the trend matches the direction of the earlier mid-friction sub-pool (Section 5.4), with no mechanism claimed.

*Measured break-even (S6, DESC; Fig. 2c).* Measured against a simulated no-probe grasp (point values, no interval): that grasp slips at 0.08714 and holds 7.125 N (λ̂ at grid index 41 in all strata). Integrated force-time at T = 1 / 5 / 30 s is 22.20 / 50.70 / 228.8 N·s without the probe, 39.10 / 63.57 / 216.4 for the learned model, 39.47 / 65.32 / 226.9 for the plug-in and 40.17 / 68.67 / 246.8 for CSF-1 with the probe. Break-even is 17.74 s (learned) and 27.07 s (plug-in); CSF-1 with the probe never breaks even. Phase integrals are 13.08 (preload), 12.91 (squeeze), 1.444 (ramp-down), 0.3480 (return) and 3.27 N·s (re-hold): for 1 s and 5 s holds the no-probe grasp uses the least force-time, and the fixed preload and squeeze dominate.

## 6 Limitations and Discussion

**What the evidence licenses.** In simulation, with friction independent of what the robot can see and at σ 0.2, a ramp-down probe plus a CRC-calibrated one-line plug-in lowers relative excess hold force against a friction-blind conformal baseline, with pooled slip near α on average; a learned model is not needed on the registered route. The saving disappears when friction is family-predictable, at σ 0.4, as the friction range narrows, and for holds shorter than 17.74–27.07 s. Hence: probe when friction is not predictable from appearance and holds are long.

**Limits.** (1) *Simulation only; σ assumed:* one simulator, rigid contact, a modelled detector; the sign changes across σ 0.1 → 0.4 (0.6486 → −0.2065), and σ was not grounded on hardware or published detector error. (2) *Stylised correlated pool:* one band layout, one σ, and no hybrid prior + probe arm, so C4 / C5 do not show that touch is useless where a prior exists. (3) *Width sweep descriptive:* one σ, no family prior, small samples at w 0.25. (4) *Marginal guarantee:* over the pool, in expectation; 31.5–70 % of re-splits have a stratum above α, and on the single correlated-pool split F1 exceeds α for three of four arms; LTT is the conservative alternative. (5) *Deployability:* offline CRC needs replays at every grid force; the online route has ordering-only intervals, overshoot to 0.1389 and no physical-replay oracle. (6) *Fixed high-force protocol:* the 32.7 N preload and squeeze exceed every instance's F\*_mid and dominate force-time; no adaptive preload was run, so the work concerns hold force, not first-contact force. (7) *Competitors:* published controllers \cite{khamis2021real, dong2019maintaining, boonstra2024estimate, niu2026synchronized} were not reproduced; the reactive design is a negative control, not re-designed. (8) *Multiplicity:* Holm covers C1–C5 and the first-round secondaries; S1–S7 and later-round rows are unadjusted, and the break-even has no interval and depends on commanded profiles.

**Future work.** A hybrid family prior + probe rule, probe-noise measurement on hardware with an adaptive preload, and replay-free online calibration.

## 7 Conclusion

In simulation, a friction-onset probe with conformal risk control on physical slip lowers relative excess hold force against the strongest friction-blind conformal baseline (C1 D 0.4314 [0.3824, 0.4835]; C3 0.4135) under a marginal, in-expectation bound. The probe, not the learner, carries the saving (C2 equivalent at σ 0.2, matched route). A family prior wins when friction is visible (C4 −0.5814); the saving reverses at σ 0.4 (−0.2065) and as friction narrows, and breaks even after 17.74 s. Online tracking is consistent with convergence to α; efficiency is inconclusive.

1. **The "learner adds nothing" conclusion depends on the metric.** C2 is equivalence on *relative* excess (D 0.011). On the CRC route the learned model is not shown to be equivalent (-0.063 [-0.123, 0.084]), yet it holds less absolute force (6.115 vs 6.463 N) *and* slips less (0.081 vs 0.098). My recomputation on absolute excess force at matched slip 0.10 gives 1.320 N for the learned model vs 1.800 N for the plug-in. That is a point D_abs of about 0.27, well beyond the SESOI. The 2-parameter shrunk plug-in reaches 1.386 N. So the defensible statement is "a learner is not needed *once the onset estimate is shrunk*; the raw plug-in leaves about 0.5 N on the table". The paper's own Figure 1 and break-even use absolute force, and on that scale the learned model is the best arm. The conclusion "the probe, not the learner, carries the saving" should be qualified accordingly.
2. **The matched-slip route uses test outcomes.** RE is interpolated at the *test* slip of 0.10 by shifting lambda-hat indices. This makes the route a frontier comparison, not a comparison of deployable certified rules. C1, C2, C4 and C5 all rest on it. That is acceptable as registered, but Sec. 4 should say "test-slip-matched" explicitly. The CRC-route rows (deployable) should be shown alongside in the main table, not only as grey ticks.
3. **Route mixing goes undisclosed** (items 14, 15, 19): Fig. 2a puts matched plug-in values next to CRC-route learned values, the feature chain mixes label-route and CRC-route numbers, and the CSF-fam sentence conflates two operating points.
4. **What sigma means is misdescribed** (item 12). Touch is not clean (taxel noise is modelled), and the sigma-0 detector already has log-error SD 0.11 with bias +0.32. A reader will take "sigma 0.2" as the total probe error, but the effective error is about 0.23. Because the main boundary claim is about noise, this needs correcting.
5. **C4 / C5 are largely predictable by construction.** The within-family log-friction SD of the c4 bands is about 0.10-0.12 (log-uniform over ratios of 1.43-1.5), below the probe's effective log-error of about 0.23. The width sweep is consistent with the same rule: the full-range log-SD is 0.35, and windows w 0.75 / 0.5 give SD 0.26 / 0.17, so the crossover sits where the friction spread falls to the probe error. The paper "hypothesises but did not test" this. A one-paragraph variance argument, or a plot of D against spread/error, would turn a set of disconnected boundary facts into a single explanation, and would show readers that C4 / C5 follow from the design.
6. **Single learned seed in v4** (item 21). The v1 seed spread of H2a (0.39-0.45, range 0.056) is more than half the SESOI, so an equivalence claim based on one fitted model should state that and ideally re-run seeds 1-4 (they already exist in `results/v1/preds/`).
7. **Statistical wording.** "p 0.915 is consistent with equivalence" should be removed. The restated C1 rule should match the registered rule. "D_RE 0.3107-0.4432 across shifts and windows" includes the pre-shift window.
8. **Undisclosed oracle inputs.** True mass and commanded acceleration are given to every rule. For the plug-in, mass cancels (F_base = F_onset (1 + a/g)), which is worth stating: it shows the "friction reading" is really a direct measurement of the static holding force, and that CSF-1 depends on an oracle mass.

**Actionable revisions.**
- A1. Report D on absolute excess force (N) alongside RE for C1, C2, C4 and S2, with CI\*\*. Rephrase C2's conclusion to "equivalent on relative excess; on absolute excess the learned and shrunk arms save more than the raw plug-in".

- A3. Fix Fig. 2a (one route per panel, or mark the routes) and the feature-chain sentence (state the label route).

## Reviewer B (robotics significance, novelty and practicality)

**Summary.** See Reviewer A. My focus is on whether the result changes how a roboticist would choose grip force.

**Strengths.**
1. A clear, well-posed question ("when does a short probe pay off?") with an honest answer that includes the conditions under which it does not.
2. The measured break-even against a no-probe grasp is the right practical yardstick, and few tactile-grasping papers report one.
3. The protocol is designed so that the onset is the *only* friction channel (preload encodes neither m nor mu; 0 preload slips and 0 missed onsets in 10,400 v4 instances, verified). This isolates the probe's value cleanly.
4. The online study shows binary-feedback quantile tracking returning slip to α after friction and amplitude shifts (1,501-2,000 window intervals contain 0.10), using only the one bit a real robot would observe.

**Weaknesses.**
1. **Limited novelty.** The components (onset-based friction reading, Coulomb force, CRC, LTT, quantile tracking) are used as published, as the paper says. Once mass cancels, the plug-in is "hold at a calibrated multiple of the measured slip-onset force times (1 + a/g)". That is essentially the classical slip-margin rule (Johansson-style grip scaling, robotic safety-margin controllers), with the margin set by CRC instead of by hand. The paper's own Sec. 5.4 finds that a per-stratum margin tuned on calibration data lands on the same operating point as CRC. So what is new is the *certificate* and the *confirmatory map*, not the controller. That is a modest contribution for NeurIPS/ICML.
2. **The practical saving is small, and negative for typical holds.** D 0.43 on relative excess corresponds to 7.125 → 6.463 N mean hold force (-9 %) for the plug-in, and -14 % for the learned model. The protocol's preload and squeeze (13.08 + 12.91 N·s) are about 5x the hold force. For 1-5 s holds the no-probe grasp uses the least force-time, and the plug-in needs holds longer than 27 s to break even. The abstract's "beyond 17.74 s" quotes the learned model's figure for the plug-in (item 16).
3. **The squeeze inflates the break-even and is not needed by the plug-in.** The plug-in does not use squeeze features, and the paper finds no evidence that the squeeze helps the learner (-0.0263). Removing the squeeze phase (12.91 N·s) from the S6 intercepts gives break-evens of about **5.0 s (learned) and 7.6 s (plug-in)**. This is my arithmetic and would need replays of a probe-only protocol, since removing the squeeze changes the contact state. Even so, it suggests the paper understates the probe's practical value under a leaner protocol, and that "probe-only (no squeeze)" is the obvious missing arm.
4. **Simulation only, with an assumed noise model.** The sign of the main effect flips between sigma 0.1 and 0.4, and sigma is not grounded on hardware or on published detector error. The deployable story therefore hinges on an unmeasured quantity. Offline CRC also needs replays at every grid force, which cannot be done on a real robot (acknowledged). For a robotics-heavy claim at an ML venue, at least a small hardware sigma measurement, or a citation-grounded sigma, is expected.
5. **The comparators are weak or not reproduced.** No published tactile controller (Khamis 2021, Dong 2019, Boonstra 2024) is reproduced. The only reactive design is a negative control with a known artefact (raw mu-hat with log bias +0.32). The family prior that wins C4 / C5 is built from *true* friction of selection objects, and no hybrid prior + probe arm was run, so the most useful practical question (does a probe help on top of a prior?) is unanswered.
6. **The marginal guarantee is weak in practice.** One object family (F1, low friction) slips at 0.11-0.32 for three of four arms on the correlated pool, and 31.5-70 % of re-splits have a stratum above α. A practitioner usually needs per-object-class guarantees. LTT helps (0.07-0.16), and its force cost should be reported next to the CRC operating point.
7. **Presentation.** Intro, Related Work and Setup fall below the length targets. Sec. 5 is a ledger of status codes (CONF / PRE / SEC / DESC, S1-S7, H2b, H2-R, C-i...) that is hard for a reader outside the project to follow. The mid-friction sub-pool omits its amplitude condition (item 13). The CSF-1 mu-bar provenance is misdescribed (item 11).

**Actionable revisions.**
- B1. Correct the abstract and conclusion: plug-in 27.07 s, learned 17.74 s. Lead with absolute hold-force numbers next to D.
- B2. Add a probe-only protocol arm (preload → ramp-down → hold, no squeeze), or at least report the S6 break-even with the squeeze phase excluded, labelled as an estimate.
- B3. Add a hybrid family-prior + probe rule (for example, shrink mu-hat toward the family mu-bar) on the correlated pool. That is the arm that decides practical value.
- B4. Ground sigma: cite measured onset-detection error from tactile hardware, or run a small hardware calibration. Report the total, not the added, noise.
- B5. Report per-family (Mondrian) CRC and LTT operating points with their force cost, so that readers can pay for conditional coverage.
- B6. State the mid-friction sub-pool definition fully (mu in [0.5, 0.8] and mid-IQR amplitude; learned vs CSF-1) and the CSF-1 mu-bar source (geometric mean of the design range, not selected on the split).
- B7. Expand the Introduction (motivation, a worked example of the force-time trade-off), Related Work (classical slip-margin control, Mondrian / conditional conformal, safe-grasp CBFs in more depth) and Setup (gripper, detector, strata parameters, label procedure) to the target lengths. Replace most status codes with plain-language labels.

**Would I recommend presentation at NeurIPS/ICML?** Not in its current form. The work is rigorous and honest, but the contribution is a careful empirical map of a known controller with a standard certificate, in simulation, with a small and conditional practical gain. A version with a probe-only protocol, a hybrid prior arm and a grounded sigma would be a solid workshop or RSS/CoRL-track paper.

**Score: 4 / 10 (Borderline, leaning reject).**

## Reviewer A (robotics systems and grasping; RSS area chair perspective)

**Summary.** The paper asks when a short tactile probe (a ramp-down from a fixed 32.7 N preload until a modelled touch detector flags incipient slip) makes a conformal physical-slip bound cheaper in hold force. In MuJoCo, with friction independent of appearance and onset noise σ = 0.2 assumed, a Coulomb plug-in on the onset, calibrated by per-stratum CRC, needs about 43 % less relative excess hold force than the best friction-blind conformal rule (6.46 N vs 7.13 N mean). A learned model adds nothing at σ 0.2. The saving vanishes when friction is predictable from the object family, for the plug-in at σ 0.4, as the friction range narrows, and for holds shorter than about 18–27 s.

**Strengths.**
1. The framing is unusually honest for this area. The paper's headline result includes its negative boundary conditions (family prior wins; break-even ≥ 17.7 s; σ 0.4 reversal), and the limitations section is concrete.
2. The comparator is chosen properly: CSF-1 was selected on a disjoint split against a Mondrian variant and a learned proprioceptive model, so the tactile gain is not measured against a straw man.
3. The confirmatory round is clean. I verified that the amendment, the read-out code, the labels and the replays were committed in that order, and that every C1–C5 number reproduces exactly from the raw replays.
4. Calibrating on the physical binary loss, rather than on bisection labels, is justified by a failed monotonicity gate (8/800). This is the right choice and is rarely made.
5. The force-time accounting against a no-probe grasp is the question a practitioner would ask, and the answer (no gain for 1–5 s holds) is reported rather than buried.

**Weaknesses.**
1. **No hardware, and σ is assumed.** The central effect changes sign across σ ∈ [0.1, 0.4] (0.65 → −0.21 for the plug-in), and σ is not grounded in any measured detector. For RSS this is the decisive weakness. The result is a map conditional on an unknown parameter, and the paper cannot say where on that map a real GelSight- or BioTac-class sensor sits. Even the simulator's own clean detector has a log bias of +0.32 and an SD of 0.11 (`19_report_rows`), and the paper does not relate that SD to σ.
2. **The force saving is about 1 N on a protocol that applies 32.7–49 N first.** The preload and squeeze exceed every object's F\*_mid, which defeats the usual motivation for minimal-force grasping (fragile objects). The paper acknowledges this (Limit 6), but then the practical claim reduces to actuator effort during long holds, which leads to weakness 3.
3. **Break-even beyond 17.7 s is not backed by long-hold physics (E6).** Slip is certified over a 0.6 s disturbance window. A 17–27 s hold at the lower force, with creep, repeated disturbances or thermal and viscoelastic effects, is never simulated, yet the paper's practical recommendation ("probe when … holds are long") depends on exactly that regime. The break-even is also a closed-form calculation from commanded profiles, not a measurement.
4. **The σ 0.4 statements overreach (E3, E2).** At σ 0.4 the learned model still saves force and beats the plug-in by 0.32, so for a noisy real sensor the learner may be what matters. The abstract and conclusion say the opposite. Figure 2a plots matched-route plug-in values next to CRC-route learned values.
5. **C4/C5 are close to built in.** The correlated pool's family bands are about 0.3 of the full log-width. The width sweep already shows that CSF-1 wins at w ≤ 0.5, so a per-family prior that reduces each family to a narrow band will beat a σ 0.2 probe almost by construction. The interesting experiment, a prior + probe hybrid, is missing. Without it, C4/C5 do not tell a practitioner whether to add touch to a perception prior.
6. **Deployability of the certificate.** Offline CRC needs a replay of every calibration grasp at 81 forces, which is impossible on hardware. The online tracker is the deployable piece, but its physical-feedback evaluation has ordering-only intervals and no oracle. The rules also use the **true mass** and a **known disturbance stratum** (E10), neither of which is stated.
7. **Competitors.** No published tactile grip controller is reproduced. The only reactive baseline is a registered design with a known biased μ̂ (+0.32 log), and it fails at slip ≥ 0.31. The paper is right to call it a negative control, but then the paper has no competitive tactile baseline.
8. **Presentation.** Neither figure exists. The prose is number-dense, with five-to-ten four-significant-figure values per sentence, and pipeline jargon ("v4 row", "read-out code", "first round") pervades the text. Sections 1, 2 and 4 are under-developed (§0.3).

**Actionable revisions.**
- A1. Either ground σ (a bench measurement of onset-force repeatability on one real sensor, or a published incipient-slip detector error converted to log F_onset noise) or recast the title and abstract explicitly as a conditional map. At minimum, report the simulator's own detector SD (0.11) next to σ.
- A2. Rewrite the abstract, §6 and §7 so that "reverses at σ 0.4" names the **plug-in**, and add that the learned model retains a saving (0.1675 CRC; matched value from `RE_match`) and beats the plug-in at σ 0.4. Make Figure 2a single-route (matched for both arms, with CRC as ticks).
- A3. Simulate at least one long hold (e.g. 20–30 s, with repeated disturbances) at the certified force, or state plainly that slip over long holds is untested and that the break-even is computed, not measured. Report the first-round seed range of the break-even.
- A4. Add a hybrid arm (family prior as F_base centre, probe as a correction, for instance a shrinkage of log μ̂ toward the family μ̄) on the correlated pool, or remove the practical recommendation that depends on its absence.
- A5. State the known-mass and known-stratum assumptions, and add the existing noisy-mass variant (`_mn` features, 08h) as a robustness row if it was run.
- A6. Produce both figures, expand Introduction / Related Work / Setup to the thresholds, move the Deviations list to an appendix, and replace pipeline terms with scientific ones ("pre-registered confirmatory round", "grid-boundary check").

**Questions to authors.** (i) What σ does your own simulated detector imply after bias removal, and is σ 0.2 added on top of it? (ii) Why is CSF-fam's CRC slip so low (0.059) compared with every other arm (0.08–0.10)? (iii) On `c4` F1, the tactile arms slip at 0.20 (learned) and 0.11 (plug-in). Is low-friction under-coverage systematic on the fresh pool too (per-μ-quintile slip)?

**Recommendation.** Weak reject at RSS. The experimental hygiene is excellent, but there is no hardware, the effect depends on an ungrounded σ, the protocol undercuts the minimal-force motivation, and the practical break-even claim lacks long-hold evidence. A workshop or a robotics-learning venue with a simulation track would be a better fit as it stands.
**Score: 4 / 10** (borderline, significant weaknesses).

## Reviewer B (conformal / statistical methodology; methodology-evidence consistency)

**Summary.** The paper applies standard CRC (Angelopoulos et al.) per disturbance stratum to a monotonised binary physical-slip loss on an 81-point log-force grid, with LTT as a high-probability alternative and quantile tracking for online shift. Its contribution is an evaluation design: pre-registered confirmatory tests on fresh objects, a matched-slip comparison route, and a boundary map.

**Strengths.**
1. The CRC construction is correct. L̃_i(λ) = max_{λ′≥λ} L_i(λ′) is non-increasing and dominates L, and the threshold (n R̃ + 1)/(n+1) ≤ α is the B = 1 CRC rule. Per-stratum calibration uses one instance per object per stratum, so within-stratum exchangeability holds. I re-implemented λ̂ independently and obtained identical indices.
2. The paper is candid about what the guarantee is: marginal, in expectation over the calibration draw, not stratum- or family-conditional. The 200-re-split check (mean slip 0.084–0.087 < α; LTT 0.068–0.071) is the right sanity check and matches the theory.
3. The registered decision code is real and was frozen before the data existed. Holm, TOST and the bootstrap p are implemented as described in the amendment, and the 2,000-draw intervals reproduce exactly (seed 2027).
4. Multiplicity is tracked honestly. Unadjusted rows are labelled, and first-round secondaries carry family-wise and global Holm values (e.g. H2-R 0.03 / 0.12).

**Weaknesses.**
1. **The confirmatory estimand is not the certified rule.** C1, C2, C4 and C5 are on the matched-slip route, which shifts each stratum's λ̂ index by up to ±8 using **test-set** slip and interpolates RE at slip 0.10. That is a test-tuned operating point, not something the CRC certificate covers. It is a legitimate comparison device, but the conclusions should be stated as "at equal realised slip", and route sensitivity matters: C2's equivalence holds on the matched route but not on the certified CRC route at the 97.5 % level (−0.063 [−0.123, 0.084]). The paper's own TOST p for that row is 0.037 (E5), so the CRC-route C2 status depends on which criterion is applied. Report both criteria explicitly.
2. **Factual and route-mixing errors in the results text (E1, E2, E7, E8).** "Matched RE 0.2674 at slip 0.05929" pairs one route's RE with the other route's slip (the CRC-point RE is 0.2995). Figure 2a mixes matched and CRC routes. The feature chain mixes label and CRC routes. The mid-friction sub-pool omits its amplitude restriction and its arm. None of these changes a confirmatory verdict, but in a paper whose selling point is methodological care, each one undermines the reader's trust.
3. **Decision rule misdescribed (E4).** §4 states a CI-based superiority rule. The registered and coded rule is Holm-adjusted bootstrap p < 0.05 with a positive point. Note also that a 97.5 % CI is the Bonferroni-4 interval of the first round and is not Holm-calibrated for five tests. Say what the CI\*\* is for (display) and what decides (p).
4. **"Equivalence" phrasing (E9).** Quoting the two-sided p = 0.915 as "consistent with equivalence" is a textbook error. Keep only the TOST evidence.
5. **Single learned-model seed.** C2, C3 and C5 rest on the frozen seed-0 fit. The first round's seed spread on C3 (0.39–0.45) is comparable to the C2 SESOI band. An equivalence claim within ±0.10 for "a learned model" in general needs at least the existing seeds 1–4 replayed on `f4`, or it should be scoped to "this fitted model".
6. **Conditional coverage is where the safety question lives, and it is under-analysed.** The "single strata exceed α in up to 70 % of re-splits" statistic is largely expected binomial noise (about 700 test instances per stratum, SD ≈ 0.011 around a mean of about 0.085). Presented as a worrying failure, it is misleading in the opposite direction. The informative failure is systematic: on `c4` the low-friction family F1 slips at 0.20 (learned) and 0.11 (plug-in) and CSF-1 at 0.32, while F2–F4 sit far below α. That points to friction-dependent miscoverage that the marginal bound hides. Report slip by true-μ quintile on the fresh pool and consider Mondrian CRC by family (on `c4`) or by μ̂ bin. This is a natural, cheap extension and it bears directly on the claimed safety.
7. **σ 0.4 conclusion (E3).** As Reviewer A notes, the attribution "probe, not learner" is a σ 0.2 result. At σ 0.4 the data say the learner matters (+0.32 vs plug-in). The abstract and conclusion must be scoped accordingly.
8. **Novelty is incremental methodologically.** CRC, LTT and score-space quantile tracking are used off the shelf, and the paper says so (§2 Novelty). The contribution is therefore the empirical protocol and boundary map, which raises the bar on robustness: more than one simulator or contact model, more than one σ on the correlated pool, and more than one model seed. Most of these are absent.

**Methodology-evidence consistency verdict.** The paper is high-fidelity to its evidence: every number I checked (more than 120 values across v1–v4) is present in the result files, and every count matches. There is **no fabrication and no trial-count inflation**. The defects are one factual mis-pairing (E1), route-mixing in a figure and two text passages (E2, E7, E8), a scope overclaim on σ 0.4 (E3), and a misdescribed decision rule (E4), all listed in §0.2.

**Actionable revisions.**
- B1. Correct E1 (CSF-fam: matched RE 0.2674 at slip 0.10; CRC point slip 0.0593 / RE 0.2995), E2 (single-route Figure 2a), E7 (name the arm and the amplitude restriction; soften the abstract sentence), E8 (label each feature-chain row's route), E9.
- B2. Restate the confirmatory rule as registered (Holm-adjusted bootstrap p, point sign). For the CRC-route C2, state the criterion and report the TOST p = 0.037 alongside the CI.
- B3. Replay seeds 1–4 of the learned model on `f4` for C2 and C3 (labels and grids exist; this is replay-only compute), or scope C2 to the fitted model.
- B4. Add per-μ-quintile slip on `f4` and a Mondrian-by-family CRC row on `c4`, and reframe the "70 % of re-splits" sentence with its binomial expectation.
- B5. Scope the abstract, discussion and conclusion attribution to σ 0.2, and add the σ 0.4 learned-arm saving.
- B6. State that mass is known and that the stratum is known at deployment (E10), and state that the m draw is log-uniform.

**Recommendation.** Borderline. The statistical machinery is sound and verifiably pre-registered, and the results reproduce exactly. The paper falls short of a strong methods contribution because the method is off the shelf, the confirmatory estimand is a test-matched route, the attribution claim rests on one seed and one σ, and several route-mixing slips sit in a paper whose main asset is rigour.
**Score: 5 / 10** (borderline, significant weaknesses).
