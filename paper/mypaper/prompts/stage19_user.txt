# Can Cost-Aware Information-Gain Touch Selection Be Amortized? Look-Ahead Headroom and Learnability under Travel Cost

## Abstract

Active tactile localization chooses where to touch next from a belief over object identity and pose. When moving the
finger costs time, that choice must trade expected information against travel. We study two questions in this
setting: how much headroom one-step look-ahead leaves over greedy cost-aware information-gain touch selection, and
whether that greedy planner can be amortized by a learned policy, trained either by distillation from the planner or by
reinforcement learning (RL) on the same objective. The task uses a joint identity × planar-pose particle belief, a
finger that moves on a stand-off circle with travel cost $c(a) = 1 + \kappa|\Delta\phi|/\pi$, and the cost-aware greedy
planner G\*, which maximises expected ball-information gain per unit cost. In a pre-registered characterisation, one-step
rollout over the reduced greedy completion gains ≤ 2.2 % of the remaining expected ball information at κ = 12 (one-sided
95 % upper bound). With an identical belief-free DeepSets head, neither DAgger distillation nor PPO was shown to reach
G\*: the student failed the pre-registered non-inferiority gate (−4.40 pp against a threshold of −1.68 pp), and RL trails G\* by 7.0 pp in test success (ci95 [+0.2, +13.7] for G\* − RL). On RL's own objective, no improvement of distillation over RL was detected (pre-registered
test rejected; Δ = +0.0002 nats, one-sided 95 % bounds [−0.31, +0.30]); this is a non-detection, not equality. Per
decision, the learned head's median latency is about 2,200× below full-size G\*, not at equal accuracy. All hypotheses,
thresholds and outcome readings were pre-registered; the experiments cover a single cell (set S, κ = 12, n\* = 4) and
are preliminary.

## 1 Introduction

A robot that localizes an object by touch receives sparse, ambiguous measurements: one contact point and one surface
normal per probe, or a miss. A single contact rarely fixes the pose, and near-symmetric object families make even the
identity uncertain. The standard remedy is to maintain a belief over identity and pose, typically a particle filter, and
to choose the next touch by its expected information gain \cite{petrovskaya2011global, hebert2013next,
zheng2024bayesian, zheng2026bayesian}. Probing is not free. The finger has to travel to the next approach direction,
and travel time competes with probes within a fixed time budget. Cost-aware selection therefore scores each candidate
by information per unit cost \cite{ottenhaus2018active}, and surveys of active haptic perception identify this trade-off
as central to practical exploration \cite{seminara2019active}.

Two questions follow for every information-driven touch selector that pays for travel. The first concerns planning
depth. Greedy information gathering is backed by (adaptive) submodularity: for touch-based localization, entropy
reduction is submodular under certain assumptions, and greedy selection is then comparable to the optimal offline policy
\cite{javdani2012efficient, vien2015touch}. These guarantees weaken when actions constrain the future
\cite{chen2015submodular, esfandiari2019adaptivity}, and travel cost is exactly such a coupling: where the finger ends up
after a probe determines what the next probe costs. A greedy selector may strand the finger far from the region that
separates the remaining hypotheses. Whether paying for look-ahead is worth it under travel cost is an empirical question
that, to our knowledge, has not been measured directly.

The second question concerns amortization. Online particle-filter EIG re-optimises at every decision, with a cost that
grows with particles × candidates × outcome samples. Amortized Bayesian experimental design replaces this with a policy
network trained once, through RL on contrastive information bounds or related approaches \cite{foster2021deep,
blau2022optimizing, ivanova2021implicit, hedman2025step, rainforth2024modern}, and tactile RL methods learn exploration
policies end to end \cite{riou2024reinforcement, niemann2024when}. A validated greedy planner offers a second route:
supervising the policy with the planner's dense per-action scores through DAgger \cite{ross2011reduction}, which removes
long-horizon credit assignment and 512-way exploration from the learning problem. The question is whether such dense
supervision beats RL on the same objective with the same head, and whether either learner reaches the planner.

**What we did and found.** We ran a pre-registered study in which hypotheses, thresholds and the full outcome map were
frozen before any test episode. Three hypotheses were registered: H1, a distilled student non-inferior to G\* (with a
fallback, H1-fb, in which the student prunes G\*'s candidates); H2, distillation beating RL on RL's own objective; and
H3, small one-step look-ahead headroom over G\*. The record reached the pre-written characterisation outcome. One-step
rollout over the reduced greedy completion gains ≤ 2.2 % of the remaining expected ball information at κ = 12 (S,
n\* = 4; one-sided 95 % UB of G(12) = 0.0221). With the same relative DeepSets head, neither learner was shown to reach
G\*: the student failed the D3 non-inferiority rule on validation (Δ̂_succ −4.40 pp vs threshold −1.68 pp), and on test
it trails by −4.4 pp (significant one-sided only) while RL trails by −7.0 pp (ci95 [+0.2, +13.7] for G\* − RL). Students
fit the teacher's training labels to the pre-registered criterion but fail validation, which the pre-registered
label-fit rule assigns to generalisation rather than capacity; covariate shift is inferred, not observed. On RL's own
objective, ball-sPCE, no advantage of distillation was detected (H2 rejected), and passing would have required an
observed difference above 0.41 nats. The learned head is about 2,200× cheaper per decision than full-size G\*, but not at
equal accuracy. H1-fb was withdrawn before any accuracy number because candidate pruning cannot reach the registered
10× latency ratio.

**Contributions.**
- **C1.** A measured, pre-registered bound on one-step look-ahead headroom over cost-aware greedy touch selection under
  travel cost (H3, Hold), with three instrument checks and an on-belief bias check.

## 2 Related Work

**Tactile localization and next-best touch.** Estimating object pose from touch is usually posed as Bayesian filtering.
Scaling-series Monte Carlo achieves global localization from sparse contacts on polygonal meshes
\cite{petrovskaya2011global}; manifold particle filters sample on the contact manifold to avoid degeneracy in planar
contact manipulation \cite{koval2015pose}; Tac2Pose returns a pose distribution from a single high-resolution touch
\cite{bauza2023tac2pose}. On top of such estimators, information-driven selection picks the next touch. The next best
touch maximises information gain on pose uncertainty \cite{hebert2013next}; Bayesian frameworks jointly track class and
pose with customised particle filters and choose exploratory actions by expected information \cite{zheng2024bayesian,
zheng2026bayesian}; recent work chooses contacts by expected information gain for rigid-body pose and shape
\cite{gordon2026active} or learns exploration policies that balance global search and local refinement
\cite{kamtikar2026tactful}. Cost-aware information gain combines expected gain with expected cost in one score
\cite{ottenhaus2018active}, and reviews of active haptic perception summarise the space \cite{seminara2019active}.
Per-step (myopic) selection is the norm in this line. Its headroom under travel cost, and the per-decision compute it
costs, are rarely measured; C1 and C4 address both for one task.

**Greedy versus non-myopic information gathering.** Submodularity explains why greedy selection works well for touch:
Shannon entropy reduction is submodular under certain assumptions, and adaptive submodularity gives online guarantees
\cite{javdani2012efficient}. Hierarchical action structure can be folded into the same framework through sequential
submodular optimisation \cite{vien2015touch}. Outside touch, submodular surrogates approximate the value of information
when the objective itself is not submodular \cite{chen2015submodular}, and semi-adaptive policies show how much
adaptivity is needed to retain guarantees \cite{esfandiari2019adaptivity}. These results assume structure that travel
cost can break: a probe changes the price of every later probe, so the information an action buys is no longer
independent of where the finger ends up. Rather than bound the gap theoretically, we measure the one-step rollout
headroom over the cost-aware greedy planner directly, with an estimator validated on constructions whose exact gap is
known (H3).

**Amortized and learned active sensing.** Deep adaptive design trains a design network on contrastive information
bounds so that each decision is a forward pass \cite{foster2021deep}; implicit and RL formulations remove the need for
likelihoods or differentiable simulators \cite{ivanova2021implicit, blau2022optimizing}, semi-amortized variants refine
the policy online \cite{hedman2025step}, nested particle filters amortize design in dynamical systems
\cite{iqbal2024recursive}, and transformer policies amortize inference and acquisition jointly \cite{huang2026aline}; a
recent review covers the field \cite{rainforth2024modern}. In touch, RL agents explore for recognition with rewards for
correct classification \cite{riou2021seeing, fleer2020efficient}, for classification, exploration and pose
\cite{riou2024reinforcement}, or learn when to stop \cite{niemann2024when}; TANDEM3D co-trains exploration and decision
making on 3D contacts \cite{xu2023tandem3d}, and Tactile MNIST provides a benchmark including localization
\cite{schneider2025tactile}. A second route imitates an information-gathering expert: imitation of a clairvoyant oracle
\cite{choudhury2017adaptive} and deep imitation of a nonmyopic active-search expert \cite{nguyen2024amortized}, both
building on DAgger's no-regret reduction \cite{ross2011reduction}. What is missing is a controlled comparison of RL
against supervised distillation of a validated planner, with the same head, the same action set and the same
objective, evaluated on the objective RL itself optimises. C2 and C3 supply that comparison for one cell.

## 3 Problem and Methods

### 3.1 Task, belief and metrics

**State and observations.** The object is a planar polygon with circumradius R = 40 mm, identity
$k \in \{1,\dots,K\}$ from library S (K = 8, near-symmetric base/variant pairs), and unknown pose. The state is
$s = (k, x, y, \theta)$ with prior $k \sim U\{1..K\}$, $x, y \sim U[-15, 15]$ mm, $\theta \sim U[0, 2\pi)$, and $G_k$
is the exact symmetry group of shape $k$. A probe returns either a contact point with noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_p^2 I)$,
$\sigma_p = 1.0$ mm, and a normal with noise $\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_n^2)$, $\sigma_n = 5°$, or a miss
$y = \varnothing$. Every method uses the same ε-softened likelihood $p_\varepsilon(y \mid s, a)$ with ε = 10⁻³.

**Actions and travel cost.** The finger rests on a stand-off circle of radius $R_s$ = 100 mm at angle $\phi_t$, with
start angle $\phi_0 \sim U[0, 2\pi)$ shared by all methods on an episode. An action $a = (\phi', \psi)$ selects one of 64
target angles $\phi'$ (5.625° spacing) and one of 8 offsets $\psi \in \{-35°, -25°, \dots, +35°\}$, giving
$|\mathcal{A}| = 512$. The finger travels the shorter way to $\phi'$ and probes from $100(\cos\phi', \sin\phi')$ mm in
direction $\phi' + \pi + \psi$ with a 200 mm guarded stroke; afterwards $\phi_{t+1} = \phi'$. In units of one stroke,

$$c(a) = 1 + \kappa\,\frac{|\Delta\phi|}{\pi}, \qquad \Delta\phi = \mathrm{wrap}(\phi' - \phi_t) \in (-\pi, \pi],$$

so κ is the time to travel half the circle. Each episode has a time budget $\tau_\kappa$; with remaining time
$\tau_{\mathrm{rem}}$ the feasible set is $\mathcal{F}(\tau_{\mathrm{rem}}) = \{a \in \mathcal{A} : c(a) \le
\tau_{\mathrm{rem}}\}$, every method chooses only within it, and the episode ends when $\tau_{\mathrm{rem}} < 1$.
Budgets are **probe-matched**: $\tau_\kappa$ is calibrated so that G\* executes $n^\*$ probes on average at every κ. For
set S, $n^\* = 4$, $\tau_0 = 4.0$, $\tau_2 = 4.7$ and $\tau_{12} = 4.9$; at $\tau_{12}$, G\* executes 3.96 probes on
average over 200 calibration episodes. Only the price of moving differs between κ levels, not the horizon.

**Metrics.** From the evaluator's posterior after the last probe, the identity estimate $\hat k$ is the maximum-mass
identity and the pose is refined by 30 Nelder–Mead iterations. Success is
$[\hat k = k_0] \wedge [\|(\hat x, \hat y) - (x_0, y_0)\| < 5\,\mathrm{mm}] \wedge [\min_{g \in G_{k_0}}
|\mathrm{wrap}(\hat\theta - \theta_0 - g)| < 5°]$. With $B(s_0)$ the success ball around the true state, the ball
information is

$$\mathrm{BI}(h) = \log \hat P(B(s_0) \mid h) - \log P(B(s_0)) \quad \text{[nats]},$$

with the posterior mass floored at $1/(2 \cdot 32{,}000)$ and the prior mass computed exactly. BI is the shared
information target of every information-based method below.

### 3.2 Cost-aware greedy planner G\*

G\* maintains an online particle filter $b = \{s_n, w_n\}_{n=1}^{N}$ and selects

$$G^\*(b) = \arg\max_{a \in \mathcal{F}(\tau_{\mathrm{rem}})} \frac{\mathrm{EIG}_B(a; b)}{c(a)},$$

where the ball EIG is estimated by nested Monte Carlo with $j_m \sim w$, $y_m \sim p_\varepsilon(\cdot \mid s_{j_m}, a)$:

$$\mathrm{EIG}_B(a;b) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Big[\log\!\!\sum_{n \in B(s_{j_m})}\!\! w_n p_\varepsilon(y_m \mid s_n,a)
- \log\!\!\sum_{n \in B(s_{j_m})}\!\! w_n - \log \sum_n w_n p_\varepsilon(y_m \mid s_n, a)\Big].$$

This is the expected one-step increment of $\log P(B(s_0) \mid h)$. Full size is $(N, C, M) = (2{,}000, 512, 64)$. At
κ = 0 G\* reduces to plain ball greedy. At each decision G\* also exports the full score vector
$s(a) = \max(0, \mathrm{EIG}_B(a;b))/c(a)$ over feasible $a$ (zero elsewhere), whose argmax equals G\*'s action; this
vector is the distillation target. **Complexity.** Per decision, G\* evaluates $M \cdot N \cdot C + N \cdot C$
likelihoods and builds ball-neighbour lists in $O(N^2)$, on top of the particle-filter assimilation of the last
observation.

### 3.3 One-step look-ahead headroom estimator

To ask whether depth is worth paying for, we compare G\* with a policy that may deviate from it for one step. For a
belief $b$ reached by G\*, let $V_{G^\*}(b)$ be the expected ball information gained over the remaining budget by
following the greedy completion, and $V_{LA}(b)$ the value of the best first action followed by the same completion.
The headroom is the dimensionless ratio of means over a bank of beliefs:

$$G(\kappa) = \frac{\overline{V}_{LA} - \overline{V}_{G^\*}}{\overline{V}_{LA}}.$$

The estimator screens $C_1 = 32$ first actions, including structured set-up moves, with common random numbers and
$M_{\text{outer}} = 64$, then re-estimates the top 4 together with G\*'s action using 4 × 64 samples. Rollouts are
completed by a **reduced greedy**: $N_{LA} = 500$ particles resampled from G\*'s decision filter, 128 candidates, M = 16,
cost-aware and feasibility-restricted. G(κ) is therefore one-step-deviation headroom over a reduced greedy completion,
not the gap to the optimal policy, and it makes no success claim. Hold requires three instrument checks: (a) a notch
construction with a known exact relative gap (pass band [0.573, 0.860], 5 runs); (b) a bank of 100 beliefs with exact
gap 0 (mean in [−0.01, 0.02], UB < 0.03); (c) a travel-notch whose exact gap is computed by enumeration (estimate within
±20 %). A bias check compares the estimator's $V_{G^\*}$ with an independent 4×-sample full-G\* rollout on 20 beliefs;
a ratio above 1.02 (over-reading greedy, which deflates G) would make Hold unavailable.

### 3.4 Amortized policies with one shared head

**Shared architecture.** Both learners use the same belief-free, finger-relative DeepSets head. Each touch in the history
is encoded in the finger frame (contact indicator, contact point, normal, and the action that produced it), summed by
a DeepSets encoder, and concatenated with a 5-dimensional relative context containing the finger angle and the
remaining-budget fraction $\tau_{\mathrm{rem}}/\tau$. The encoder and head are 2 × 128 MLPs and output 512 relative
logits, masked to $-\infty$ outside $\mathcal{F}(\tau_{\mathrm{rem}})$. The architecture, input and action set are the
controlled variables of C2 and C3; only the training signal differs. **Complexity.** The implementation re-encodes the
full history at each decision, $O(T^2 H^2 + T H A)$ for $T$ touches, hidden width $H$ and $A = 512$ actions.

**Distillation.** The student $\pi_\theta$ minimises

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\sum_{a \in \mathcal{F}} q(a)\log \pi_\theta(a \mid h) - \lambda \log \pi_\theta(a^\* \mid h),
\qquad q = \mathrm{softmax}\big(\beta_T\, s(a)/\sigma_s\big)_{a \in \mathcal{F}},$$

with $\sigma_s$ the per-decision standard deviation of $s$ over feasible actions (uniform over the argmax set if
$\sigma_s < 10^{-6}$) and $a^\* = \arg\max_a s(a)$. Hyperparameters were selected on held-out floored normalised regret,

$$r = \frac{s_{\max} - s(a_\pi)}{\max(s_{\max} - s_{\mathrm{med}},\ \rho\, s_{\max})}, \qquad \rho = 0.05,$$

whose mean $\bar R$ also drives the stopping rule; decisions with $s_{\max} = 0$ are excluded. No success-based
selection is used anywhere.

> **Algorithm 1 (DAgger distillation of G\*).**
> 1. Round 0: roll out G\* on 1,500 label episodes, store $(h, \phi_t, \tau_{\mathrm{rem}}, \mathcal{F}, s, a^\*)$ at
>    every decision; hold out episodes 1,200–1,499 for $\bar R$. Train $\pi_\theta$ on $\mathcal{L}$.
> 2. For round $r = 1, 2$ with mixing $\beta_{\mathrm{DAgger}} \in \{0.5, 0\}$: roll out 400 episodes per seed, executing
>    G\*'s action with probability $\beta_{\mathrm{DAgger}}$ and $\pi_\theta$'s otherwise; run G\*'s filter alongside and
>    label every visited belief with $s$; aggregate all data; continue training from the previous weights.
> 3. In every round, evaluate held-out $\bar R$ after each epoch; stop when it improves by < 0.005 over 3 evaluations
>    (cap 50 epochs); keep the last checkpoint.
> 4. Remedy (pre-registered, applied once if gate D3 fails): double round 0 and both DAgger rounds and retrain.

The applied remedy raised the end-of-round-2 training set from 7,957 to 17,083 decisions per seed.

**RL (PPO).** The RL policy uses the same head and action set and is trained with PPO-clip \cite{schulman2017proximal},
γ = 1 and a critic baseline. Its reward is the increment of ball-sPCE, a contrastive estimate of ball information in the
style of \cite{foster2021deep}: per episode, $L = 4095$ contrastive states $s_{1:L} \sim p$ and $J = 16$ states
$s_0^{(j)}$ uniform in $B(s_0)$ are fixed, and

$$g_t = \log \hat p_B(h_t \mid s_0) - \log\Big[\tfrac{1}{L+1}\Big(\hat p_B(h_t \mid s_0) + \sum_{l=1}^{L} p(h_t \mid
s_l)\Big)\Big], \quad \hat p_B(h \mid s_0) = \tfrac{1}{J}\sum_j p(h \mid s_0^{(j)}),$$

with $r_t = g_t - g_{t-1}$. The return telescopes to $g_T$ (capped at $\log(L+1) = 8.32$ nats), so **$g_T$ is RL's own
objective**. Each RL seed trains for 200,064 episodes; the learning rate was chosen by a 4-configuration sweep (Section
4).

**Belief-input control.** The same distillation recipe with the history encoder replaced by a 48-feature summary of
G\*'s particle filter (per identity: weight, weighted mean and SD of $x$ and $y$, and the resultant length of
$|G_k|\theta$) plus the relative context, trained on first-attempt-size data. It is used only by the encoder sentence of
H2's fail branch.

### 3.5 Baselines

Random selects uniformly among feasible actions. Three heuristics use a budget-reservation rule, allowing $a$ only if
$c(a) \le \tau_{\mathrm{rem}} - \max(0, n^\* - t - 1)$ after $t$ probes (else the cheapest feasible action):
**B-spread** (belief-free) maximises the minimum angular distance to previous probe angles with ψ = +5°; **B-fan**
(belief-free, travel-nearest) stays at $\phi_t$ when allowed and cycles ψ through +5°, −5°, +15°, …, −35°, otherwise
moving to the nearest allowed $\phi'$; **B-cov** (belief-using) maximises coverage under an online filter. The best
heuristic on pilot data, H★, was frozen before test; for S12 it is B-fan.

## 4 Experimental Protocol

**Pre-registration and routing.** Hypotheses, thresholds, the outcome map (rows 1–4) and a list of analyses not allowed
after the run were frozen in design documents before any v3 run, and the main-run set was frozen before any test
episode. The record reached row 4 through the gates: the D3 learnability gate failed on the first attempt and again
after its one remedy (H1 dead, never tested on the test split), and H1-fb was withdrawn at the latency gate Lat′.
Under row 4 the remaining pre-registered decisions are H2, H3 and the encoder sentence; all other numbers are gate data
or reported rows without a test.

**Cell.** All learning results are for set S at κ = 12 with $n^\* = 4$ (S12). H3 also uses κ ∈ {0, 2}.

**Splits.** Disjoint episode streams: calibration (200), pilot3 (G\* 500 episodes; random and heuristics 200 each, for
margins and H★), val3 (500 paired episodes, for D3 and its remedy), test3 episodes 0–499 (H2, $g_T$) and 0–299 (success
and BI rows). Within a split, all methods run on the same episodes with the same initial state, start angle and
observation stream (paired design). H3 uses 100 G\*-reached beliefs per κ, at a uniformly drawn step in
$\{0, \dots, n^\*-1\}$.

**Evaluator.** Success and BI are read from an adaptive grid posterior that rebuilds each history exactly. Its gate E′
compared grid subdivision 4 with 8 on 16 episodes and failed (one counted success flip outside the [0.45, 0.55]
true-identity-mass tie band), so subdivision 8 is used for every reported number. $g_T$, H3, latency and label fit do
not use the grid.

**Statistics.** Paired differences use a crossed episode × seed bootstrap with B = 10,000; bounds are one-sided 95 %
unless labelled ci90/ci95 (two-sided). Each claim has one test and there is no correction across claims, because H2 and
H3 are separate claims with separate decisions. The test-split success and BI intervals are descriptive, not tests.

**Gate D3.** On 500 paired val3 episodes with 3 student seeds, D3 passes iff (i)
$\hat\Delta_{\mathrm{succ}} \ge \max(-2\,\mathrm{pp},\ -\delta + 1.645\,\mathrm{SE}_s\sqrt{0.5})$; (ii) the same for BI
with ε and a −0.15-nat floor; (iii) every seed's $\hat\Delta_{\mathrm{succ}} > -\delta$. Here δ = min(4 pp, 0.15·D_c)
and ε = min(0.05·BI(G\*), 0.15·D_c^BI), with D_c the pilot random-to-G\* gap; for S12, δ = 0.04 (the cap) and
ε_S12 = 0.3145 (provisional, see Table 1).¹

**H2 and H3 rules.** H2 passes iff the one-sided 95 % lower bound of $g_T(\text{student}) - g_T(\text{RL})$ on test3
0–499 exceeds +0.10 nats. H3 is Hold iff UB95(G(12)) < 0.03, Kill iff LB95(G(12)) > 0.05 (precedence), otherwise
Inconclusive.

**Hyperparameters and implementation.** Table 0 lists the training and estimator settings. Everything is numpy with
hand-written backpropagation, run single-threaded (BLAS threads = 1, one pinned core) on GitHub Actions Ubuntu runners
([CONST?:runner_cpu_model]; [CONST?:python_numpy_versions]). Latency follows a fixed protocol: all configurations are timed
interleaved on the same S12 pilot decision states, 20 warm-up and 500 timed decisions, plus 500 more if any 95 % CI
half-width exceeds 10 % of its median; the pooled 1,000 decisions on 253 pilot episodes came from two runner jobs.

## 5 Results

### 5.1 H3: one-step look-ahead headroom is small

H3 is **Hold**. One-step rollout over the reduced greedy completion gains ≤ 2.2 % of the remaining expected ball
information at κ = 12 (S, n\* = 4; one-sided 95 % UB of G(12) = 0.0221). The point estimate is G(12) = 0.01624, and the
upper bound clears the 0.03 threshold by 0.0079. In absolute terms the gap is 0.041 nats on a mean $V_{G^\*}$ of 2.506
nats (Table 2a). The point estimates follow the descriptive ordering G(0) ≤ G(2) ≤ G(12) (differences not tested), and even the largest is small.

deviating step, consistent with greedy-competitiveness arguments \cite{javdani2012efficient, chen2015submodular} carrying over to this travel-coupled setting at the tested budget.

After the one remedy (round 0 and both DAgger rounds doubled; 7,957 → 17,083 training decisions), the point estimates
moved from −0.199 to −0.096 nats BI and from −5.87 to −4.40 pp success; this change was not tested.

### 5.3 H2: distillation against RL on RL's own objective

On test3 0–499, the student reaches $g_T$ = 5.3678 nats and RL 5.3676; Δ = +0.00023 nats with one-sided 95 % bounds
[−0.3103, +0.3034] (p_greater 0.4883). H2 is **rejected**. The pre-registered fail-branch reading applies: "dense
supervision does not improve on RL with this head", because the paired success ci95 [−3.67, +8.78] pp contains 0; the
alternative pre-written reading required that interval to exclude 0. Passing required an observed Δ > 0.41 nats; with
SE ≈ 0.19, the test had about 80 % power only for a true Δ near 0.57 nats. No equivalence test (TOST) was pre-registered
or run for H2. Overall, no difference between the two learned policies was detected on g_T (Δ = +0.0002 nats, one-sided
95 % bounds [−0.31, +0.30]) or on test success (ci95 [−3.7, +8.8] pp); equivalence was not tested.

The grid evaluator gives a further observation, not a mechanism. Student − RL BI is +0.289 nats (ci95 [+0.117, +0.465])
on test3 0–299, while the g_T difference on 0–499 is undetected with bounds wide enough (±0.31 nats) to include a gap of
that size; RL executes 3.0 probes against the student's 4.0. RL varies more across its 3 seeds than the student (test
success range 7.7 vs 1.0 pp; g_T range 0.48 vs 0.14 nats), and the RL per-seed SD of g_T is about 3.3× the student's
(0.253 vs 0.077 nats; range ratio 3.5). This spread is the likely source of the interval width, inferred from the
per-seed SDs; no variance decomposition was done. The companion rows $g_T$(G\*) = 5.299 and $g_T$(random) = 2.618 on the
same episodes carry no interval and no test, and we do not rank G\* against the learned policies on $g_T$: ball-sPCE is
a contrastive estimate, and grid BI orders G\* above both learned policies. All three RL seeds ran to the 200,064-episode
cap; the plateau rule fired at 102,400, 102,400 and 112,640 episodes, so training continued well past the point at which the registered plateau rule fired.

## 6 Discussion

**What the results say together.** C1 shows that, at this budget and travel cost, one deviating step over the reduced
greedy completion gains ≤ 2.2 % of the remaining expected ball information (one-sided 95 % UB). A greedy teacher is therefore a
defensible amortization target: there is little evidence that a deeper planner would have been a better one. C2 shows
that a defensible target is not enough. With the same belief-free head, neither the distilled student nor PPO was shown
to recover G\*'s success, and C3 shows that the dense per-action labels did not buy a detectable advantage on the
objective RL optimises. The supervision signal that removed credit assignment and exploration from the learning problem
did not, at this data scale, turn into parity with the teacher.

**Why the student falls short.** The pre-registered label-fit rule points to generalisation rather than capacity: the
students fit roughly half of G\*'s training argmax labels, which meets the registered criterion, yet fall below 0.50
top-1 on held-out decisions in four of six cases. Because the held-out set is teacher-visited, covariate shift onto the
student's own states is an inference, not an observation. The H3 pattern suggests a complementary reading: if many
beliefs have several near-equal actions, top-1 agreement is a coarse measure of policy quality, and small score
differences that the student cannot reproduce may still matter for the final pose estimate.

**Unexpected results.** Two observations do not fit a simple story. First, BI separates the student from RL (+0.289
nats) while $g_T$ does not, and RL executes one probe fewer; the two instruments and episode sets differ, so we report
this as an observation only. Second, on test3 the learned policies were not shown to exceed a travel-nearest heuristic, while that heuristic trails G\* in both success and BI; the pre-written reading is reported without its flat-task clause.

**Relation to prior work.** Amortized design methods \cite{foster2021deep, blau2022optimizing} and imitation of
information-gathering experts \cite{choudhury2017adaptive, nguyen2024amortized} propose learned policies as replacements for online design or planning; our controlled single-cell result is a caution that, for contact sensing with a joint
identity × pose belief and travel cost, neither route reached the online planner with a belief-free head.
Semi-amortized designs that refine a learned proposal online \cite{hedman2025step}, or nested-filter amortization
\cite{iqbal2024recursive}, are natural directions, although our latency split shows that the particle filter itself
bounds the speed-up of any scheme that keeps it in the loop.

**Open questions.** Would held-out evaluation on student-visited states confirm covariate shift? Would an equivalence
test or a variance decomposition sharpen H2? Do the findings hold in other cells, such as the asymmetric set or κ = 0?

## 7 Limitations

- **Single cell.** All learning results are S12 only, with $n^\* = 4$. S0 and A12 appear only as incomplete pilot rows
  (provisional PASS on incomplete random rows, n = 62 / 199 / 161 of 200).
- **Evaluator not shown converged.** E′ failed: the one counted flip occurred with the true-identity mass unchanged
  (0.228) and outside the [0.45, 0.55] tie band; the JSON does not record which part of the success criterion changed.
  Sub 4 → 8 flips the success outcome of 7.5 % of the 200 S0 calibration episodes, and no finer grid was checked.
  Sub-8 read-out costs 40.6 s per episode, which limited the grid-scored rows (G5′ re-reads, D3/D3-B, M4 on 300
  episodes).
- **RL comparator selected off-protocol.** The sweep was selected on the trainer's own validation stream rather than
  val3, with 50,048-episode sweep runs; the selected configuration won by 0.919 nats of final validation ball-sPCE.
- **RL probe use differs.** RL executes 3.0 probes against 4.0 for the student; travel is not reported.
- **H3 scope.** H3 measures headroom over a reduced greedy completion, not the optimum; the bias direction of $V_{LA}$
  was not checked; instrument checks (a) and (b) are identical to the previous iteration.
- **Label-fit thresholds are coarse** (top-1 ≈ 0.52 counts as "fits"), and the held-out set is teacher-visited.
- **Latency.** The Lat′ protocol is incomplete (six configurations with CI half-widths above 10 %, two machines pooled),
  and the per-episode latency was not measured.
- **Cross-split comparisons are unpaired.** G\* success is 0.426 on pilot3, 0.446 on val3 and 0.4267 on test3.
- **Missing pre-registered report items.** Training compute including teacher labelling, and the teacher noise-floor
  $\bar R$ and tie fraction, are not reported; without the latter, the scale of $\bar R$ ≈ 0.18 is unknown.
- **Incomplete rows.** The belief-input validation and the sub-8 pilot re-reads were still running at writing
  ([PENDING:12_d3_analysis_belief_student], [PENDING:12_g5_analysis.cells]).
- **No physical demonstration.** All results are in simulation with planar objects; held-out object shapes were not
  tested.

## 8 Conclusion

We asked two questions about active tactile localization under travel cost: how much one-step look-ahead adds over
greedy cost-aware information-gain touch selection, and whether that greedy planner can be amortized by a learned
policy, through distillation from the planner or RL on the same objective. In a pre-registered characterisation on a
single cell, the answers are as follows. Greedy leaves little one-step headroom: rollout over the reduced greedy
completion gains at most about 2.2 % of the remaining expected ball information (one-sided 95 % bound). Neither
amortization route was shown to reach the planner with an identical belief-free head; the distilled student failed its
non-inferiority gate, and RL trails the planner on test. Dense planner supervision did not improve on RL on RL's own
objective, a non-detection rather than a demonstration of equality. The learned head is far cheaper per decision, but not
at equal accuracy, and candidate pruning cannot recover the planner's cost because the particle filter bounds it.
Concrete next steps are evaluation on student-visited states to test the covariate-shift reading, an equivalence test
and variance decomposition for the distillation–RL comparison, more cells and object sets, and semi-amortized schemes
that keep a cheaper filter in the loop. All hypotheses, thresholds and readings were fixed before the runs, and the
scope is one shape set, one travel cost and four probes.

# Stage 18 Peer Review (run 1): evidence audit

Paper: "Can Cost-Aware Information-Gain Touch Selection Be Amortized? Look-Ahead Headroom and Learnability under Travel Cost"

Reviewer stance (system prompt): a balanced conference reviewer who is rigorous about whether the methods match the evidence.

## Summary of the submission

The paper is a pre-registered characterisation study of active tactile localization with a joint identity × planar-pose
particle belief and travel cost c(a) = 1 + κ|Δφ|/π on 512 actions. It asks two things. (H3) How much headroom does
one-step look-ahead leave over the cost-aware greedy planner G\*? (H1/H2) Can G\* be amortized by a belief-free DeepSets
head trained by DAgger distillation or by PPO on a ball-sPCE reward? All learning results come from one cell (set S,
κ = 12, n\* = 4). Headline results: UB95 G(12) = 0.0221 (Hold). The distilled student fails the D3 non-inferiority gate
(−4.40 pp against −1.68 pp). RL trails G\* by 7.0 pp on test. H2 is rejected (Δg_T = +0.0002 nats, LB −0.31). Per
decision, the student is about 2,200× faster than full G\*. H1-fb was withdrawn because the latency split caps pruning
at about 6.7×.

## Reviewer A (methodology and evidence consistency)

### Strengths
1. **Unusually high agreement between the numbers and the results files.** Every headline number I checked in
   Tables 1, 2a, 2b and 2c matches `results/v3/results_v3.json` and the per-analysis JSONs to the stated precision
   (see "Verified spot-checks"). I found no invented metric, test or trial count.
2. **The statistical machinery matches the claims.** The paper claims a crossed episode × seed bootstrap with
   B = 10,000, one-sided 95 % bounds and no multiplicity correction. `touchbed/stats.py::crossed_bootstrap_diff` and
   `analysis_h2_v3.py`, `analysis_m4_v3.py`, `analysis_d3_v3.py` and `analysis_g2_v3.py` do exactly that. TOST and
   Holm exist in the code, and the paper correctly says they were not used for H2. The paper claims no ANOVA or
   t-test it did not run.
3. **The gate logic is reproducible.** For example, the D3 thresholds −0.014345 (first attempt) and −0.016806
   (remedy) follow exactly from −δ + 1.645·SE·√0.5 with the stored SEs. The H3 region logic in `analysis_g2_v3.py`
   (Kill takes precedence over Hold, plus the bias-ratio guard) matches §4 and the frozen `docs/07_hypotheses_v3.md`
   (lines 381–393, 412–415).
4. **Negative results are reported honestly.** H2 is called a non-detection, not equivalence, with an explicit power
   statement. Gap closure is withheld by rule. The flat-task clause is dropped because the evidence contradicts it.
   The off-protocol RL sweep is disclosed in Limitations.

### Weaknesses
1. **The H3 headline claim says more than the estimator measures.** The abstract and §5.1 say "one-step rollout gains
   ≤ 2.2 % of the remaining expected ball information". The estimator (`touchbed/lookahead.py::lookahead_gap`,
   C1 = 32) screens only G\*'s action, three structured set-up moves and random actions up to 32 candidates out of as
   many as 512 feasible ones (about 6 %), and then re-estimates the top 4. G(κ) is therefore a lower bound on the
   one-step-deviation headroom over a random subset of first actions, and the UB95 bounds that quantity, not the
   best one-step deviation. Instrument checks (a) and (c) do not cover this gap. In every notch and travel-notch run
   the winning action is the structured set-up move (action 71 is always `candidates[1]`; `a_la` = 71 in all 5 v2 (a)
   runs). So the checks show the estimator can find a gap when the right move is in the structured list, not when it
   must be found by random screening. The paper needs to state this scope in the abstract and in C1, or add a
   full-512 screen on a subset of beliefs.
2. **(a) and (c) are not independent instrument checks.** By construction (`touchbed/travel_notch.py` docstring), the
   travel-notch is the notch problem with its actions placed on the circle. Both report the identical exact gap
   0.7166450192088315. The paper already says (a) and (b) are byte-identical to v2 (`results/v2/12_g1_analysis.json`,
   confirmed), so all three checks rest on one construction. "Three instrument checks" overstates the validation.
3. **The direction of the bias check is unguarded.** The pre-registered guard only catches over-reading of V_G\*
   (ratio > 1.02). The measured ratio, 0.957, means a 4.3 % under-reading (0.087 nats). That is about twice the
   κ = 12 absolute gap of 0.041 nats. The paper notes this but still treats Hold as clean. V_LA uses the same reduced
   completion and is also a max over noisy re-estimates (upward selection bias). A symmetric check on V_LA, or at
   least a bias-corrected G, is needed before "Hold" can carry the weight that C1 and §6 put on it ("a greedy teacher
   is therefore a defensible amortization target").
4. **The students barely train, and the paper does not report it.** The `results/v3/d3*/S_k12_hp0_*_r*.json` logs show
   that the pre-registered stopping rule (improvement < 0.005 over 3 evaluations) stops every student after 4–16
   epochs. Wall time per round is 3–28 s (round 0: 5 epochs, 3.2 s; remedy round 2: 6–7 epochs, about 15–18 s). By
   contrast, each RL seed trains for 200,064 episodes and 48–68 min. Combined with train top-1 of only 0.51–0.53, the
   label-fit rule's "generalisation, not capacity" verdict is weak: a 0.25 R̄ / 0.50 top-1 threshold cannot tell
   "fits" from "stopped early". The paper lists training compute as a missing pre-registered item. It must report it,
   and it should add one non-binding run with a longer patience or a fixed epoch budget.
5. **The tuning of both learners lands on the edge of the grid.** The RL sweep's selected configuration (cfg 2,
   lr 1.09e-3) is the largest learning rate in a 4-point sweep with 1 seed, won by 0.919 nats on the trainer's own
   validation stream (not val3). The distillation HP grid (`distill.py::HP_GRID`, 4 configurations, seed 501,
   round 0) picked hp0 (lr 1e-3, also the largest) with held-out R̄ 0.3827, against hp2 0.3832 and hp3 0.3872. That
   is a near tie on one seed and one round. Neither comparator is shown to be tuned, which weakens C2 and C3 ("neither
   route reached the planner"). In addition, the distillation HP grid is not described in the paper at all. §3.4 only
   says "Hyperparameters were selected on held-out floored normalised regret".
6. **The paper still contains placeholder and stale values for numbers that exist.**
   - Table 0: `[CONST?:ppo_clip_epsilon]` and `[CONST?:ppo_epochs]`. The RL run configs
     (`results/v3/rl/S_k12_rel_cfg2_sd51x.json`) give clip 0.2 and 4 epochs.
   - §4: `[CONST?:python_numpy_versions]`. The latency header gives Python 3.12.15 and numpy 2.5.4.
   - Table 1, R3: `[PENDING:...random.success]` and `[PENDING:...D_c]`. The JSON already holds provisional values
     (random 0.0754, n = 199; D_c 0.3506).
   - Table 1, R5c: D3-B is PENDING, yet partial values exist (n = 298 of 300: Δ̂_succ −19.9 pp, Δ̂_BI −1.01 nats,
     fail).
   - **Stale footnote 2 and §7:** "n = 62 / 199 / 161 of 200". `results/v3/12_g5_analysis.json` gives random
     n = 162 / 199 / 197 (S0 / S12 / A12). These numbers must be corrected.
7. **Small mismatches between the stated protocol and the code.**
   - "One pinned core" (§4). CPU affinity is set only in the v2 `main.py::cmd_latency`. The v3 path
     (`cli3.py::cmd_latency3`) does not pin. Only the thread variables are 1 (in the workflow and the header).
   - Table 1, R2 is headed "S12 unless stated", but E′ used 16 episodes across S12 and A12. The single counted flip is
     A12/B-fan, episode 1.
   - The Lat′ student timing used the round-0 checkpoint `S_k12_hp0_s501_r0.npz`, not the remedied student. Because
     the architecture is identical this is harmless, but it should be stated.
8. **RL behaviour is not diagnosed.** RL executes exactly 3.0 probes on test, and its final training entropy is 0.0045
   (`S_k12_rel_cfg2_sd511.json`). That is a collapsed, near-deterministic policy that gives up a probe for travel.
   This is the main behavioural difference from the student, and it could explain why g_T ties while BI differs, yet
   travel is "not reported". Report travel per episode and the action distribution.

### Actionable revisions (Reviewer A)
- A1. Rewrite the scope of H3/C1. State that the bound covers G\*'s action, 3 structured set-up moves and random
  actions up to 32 screened first actions, out of up to 512. Either add a full-512 screen on 20 or more beliefs, or

- A5. Correct the "pinned core" statement, the E′ cell label and the latency checkpoint note.
- A6. Report RL travel and probe counts with their distribution, and the final policy entropy.

**Score (Reviewer A): 4 / 10 (Borderline, leaning reject).** The evidence is honest and traceable, but the main

### Strengths
1. **The questions matter.** Whether myopic cost-aware selection leaves headroom under travel coupling, and whether

## Reviewer A: robotics significance and framing

**Score: 3 / 10 (Reject).**

### Strengths
1. The pre-registration discipline is unusually strong. The outcome map, gate rules and fail-branch readings were frozen before the runs, and the paper reports negative results and failed gates (E′, D3 twice) without spinning them.
2. The controlled comparison is the right *kind* of experiment: same head, same action set, same episodes, only the training signal differs. Paired episodes with a crossed episode × seed bootstrap is the correct design.
3. The limitations section is candid and specific: one cell, unconverged evaluator, off-protocol RL sweep, teacher-visited held-out set.
4. The latency decomposition is clean. Assimilation plus neighbour lists take 15 % of G\*'s time, which caps any candidate-pruning speed-up at about 6.7×. The result is useful and checkable.

### Weaknesses
1. **The single cell barely exercises travel, the paper's motivating coupling.** With τ₁₂ = 4.9 and four unit-cost probes, the whole episode has 0.9 strokes of travel, i.e. at most about 13.5° of finger motion at κ = 12. I measured from `results/v3/test3/*.jsonl` that G\* travels **6.1° per episode on average**, the remedied student 1.5°, and RL 23–26° (RL buys that travel by giving up a probe: 3.0 probes). So in S12 the problem is mostly "pick an approach offset ψ near where the finger already is". The introduction's story, that greedy "may strand the finger far from the region that separates the remaining hypotheses", has almost no room to happen at this budget. A small one-step headroom (H3) is close to expected in this regime and says little about travel-coupled planning in general. The title and abstract ("under travel cost") overstate what the cell tests.
2. **A trivial heuristic matches both learned policies.** B-fan stays put and cycles ψ. It reaches 0.36 test success against the student's 0.382 (Δ +2.2 pp, ci95 [−2.8, +7.3]) and RL's 0.357. Combined with point 1, a reader can fairly conclude that the learners recovered "don't move, vary ψ" and nothing of G\*'s belief-dependent selection. The paper reports this, but only as an "exploratory" §5.4. It is central to interpreting C2/C3 and should be in the abstract.
3. **H3 was close to a foregone replication.** `docs/07_hypotheses_v3.md` §0.3 records that the previous iteration (32 × 8 action set) had already measured G(12) = 0.0146 (UB95 0.0208), and the Hold threshold was then set at UB < 0.03 (tightened from 0.05). The design document itself calls this a "low-risk replication". Pre-registering a threshold after seeing a near-identical estimate is legitimate, but the paper does not say so in §5.1 or the contributions. C1 is presented as a new measurement, and the prior estimate must be disclosed next to it.
4. **The amortization framing omits the obvious baseline: a cheaper G\*.** The latency table in `12_lat_analysis` already contains G\* variants with 125–500 particles at 4–40 ms per decision (e.g. g125_128_16 at 7.7 ms, g500_32_4 at 16.5 ms). The pre-registration even lists them as R_b candidates for H1b. Against these, the "about 2,200× cheaper" figure for the student shrinks to roughly 10–50×. No accuracy number is reported for any reduced G\*, so the reader cannot tell whether learning beats simply shrinking the filter. For a robotics audience, that comparison decides whether amortization matters at all. The 2,200× figure appears in the abstract and should not, unless it sits next to the reduced-planner baseline.
5. **No real-robot or MuJoCo-contact validation, and planar objects only.** `code/touchbed/mujoco_eval.py` exists but is not used. At an RSS/CoRL venue, a characterisation result on one simulated planar cell, with no held-out shapes, falls below the bar for significance.
6. **The novelty is incremental.** Greedy EIG/cost touch selection is established (Ottenhaus 2018, Hebert 2013). "Imitation of an information-gathering expert vs RL" has precedent (Choudhury 2017; Nguyen 2024). The new element is the controlled comparison in one cell, and its answer is "neither works, no difference detected". Without mechanism, that is a weak contribution.

### Actionable revisions (A)
- A1. Add at least one cell where travel actually binds: a larger τ at κ = 12, or κ ∈ {2, 6} with probe-matched budgets that allow more than 1 stroke of travel. Report G\* travel per episode in every cell, and the fraction of decisions where G\* moves more than one grid step.
- A2. Report test accuracy for 2–3 reduced-G\* configurations from the existing latency grid (e.g. g125_128_16, g500_32_4), and restate the latency claim as a cost–accuracy frontier: student, reduced G\*, full G\*.
- A3. Move the B-fan comparison into the abstract and §6. State plainly that both learners are statistically indistinguishable from a belief-free stay-and-fan heuristic.
- A4. Disclose the previous-iteration G(12) = 0.0146 (UB 0.0208) next to C1, and call H3 a replication on the 64 × 8 action set.
- A5. Tone the title and abstract down to the tested regime ("at a four-probe budget with ≤ 1 stroke of travel").

## Reviewer B: methodology–evidence consistency

**Score: 4 / 10 (Borderline reject).**

### Strengths
1. Almost every number checks out. I matched more than 40 reported values to `results_v3.json` and found no fabricated metric. The trial counts are right: 500 val3, 300/500 test3, 100 beliefs per κ, 20 bias beliefs, 3 + 3 seeds, 200,064 RL episodes.
2. The statistics are what the paper says: a crossed episode × seed percentile bootstrap, B = 10,000, one-sided 95 % (`touchbed/stats.py::crossed_bootstrap_diff`). The ci90/ci95 labels match α = 0.05/0.025 in `analysis_m4_v3.py`.
3. The method descriptions match the code. The G\* ball-EIG formula, the score-vector label, the distillation loss and σ_s rule, the regret floor, the 64 × 8 action set, the travel cost and feasibility, the D3 thresholds and the H3 ratio-of-means estimator all match (see the spot-checks).

### Weaknesses
1. **The student is barely trained, and the paper's "generalisation, not capacity" reading does not follow.** The training logs (`results/v3/d3/S_k12_hp0_s50x_r{0,1,2}.json`, `d3rem/…`) show:
   - round 0 stops after **5 epochs and about 3 s of wall time**;
   - round 2 after 7 epochs (8.6 s);
   - the remedy round 2 after 6 epochs (15 s).

   Train loss is still falling monotonically when the patience-3 / tol-0.005 rule fires on a noisy held-out R̄. Round 1 drops held-out R̄ from 0.385 to 0.22 mostly through continued optimisation, which shows round 0 had not converged. Meanwhile train top-1 is only 0.51–0.53 against held-out top-1 of 0.48–0.51, a train–held-out gap of about 0.02–0.03. A model at 52 % on its *own training labels*, with almost no generalisation gap, looks under-optimised, not generalisation-limited. The pre-registered label-fit rule (top-1 ≥ 0.50) is too lax to support the §5.2/§6 "generalisation rather than capacity" sentence. That sentence should go. The student also gets seconds of compute while RL gets about 1 h per seed (`wall_s` 2,880–4,069 s). "Only the training signal differs" is therefore not true of the *optimisation budget*.
2. **RL tuning is thin, and the chosen configuration sits at the edge of the sweep.** The sweep has 4 configs on 1 seed. Three of them vary only lr and one varies only entropy; batch and critic lr are fixed. The winner is the *largest* lr tested (1.09e-3) and wins by 0.92 nats. A winner at the grid edge by a large margin means the optimum may lie beyond the grid. The final RL policies have collapsed entropy (0.0045 at 200k episodes, `rl/S_k12_rel_cfg2_sd511.json`) and always execute exactly 3 probes. The RL arm of C2/C3 is therefore not shown to be competitive.
3. **H2 is tested on a saturating metric.** ball-sPCE is capped at log(4096) = 8.32 nats. On test3 0–499, 26 % of G\* episodes, 22 % of student episodes and 15 % of RL episodes lie within 0.05 nats of the cap (computed from `results/v3/h2/*.jsonl`). On this metric G\* (5.299) also scores *below* both learners (5.368), the opposite of the success and BI ordering. That inversion means g_T is not a faithful proxy for the task. "RL's own objective" may be a poor arbiter, and the ceiling compresses differences, which helps produce the non-detection. Report the saturation fraction, and either raise L or discuss the cap as a cause of low power.
4. **The H3 instrument checks do not validate sensitivity in-domain.**
   - (a) and (c) share the identical exact gap 0.7166450192… (`12_g1_analysis.json`). The travel-notch is the notch problem with travel added, and its own docstring says it "does not by itself show that travel creates headroom on the touchbed task". Presenting them as two independent checks is misleading.
   - The estimator over-reads the notch gap by 16 % (0.829 vs 0.717). V_LA is the max over 4–5 noisy re-estimates (`lookahead.py::lookahead_gap`), a winner's-curse upward bias.
   - The only in-domain check (b) tests false positives (gap 0). Nothing shows that the 500-particle, 128-candidate, M = 16 completion *could* detect a real touchbed gap.
   - At κ = 12 the completion's candidate subset has 22.5° angle spacing (`rung_subset(16, 8)`), coarser than the ≤ 13.5° of travel available. The reduced greedy is therefore structurally different from G\* in exactly the regime tested.

   Add an in-domain positive control, or at least discuss these issues.
5. **Some numbers are stale or under-reported where data already exist.**
   - Footnote 2 and §7 give random-row counts "n = 62 / 199 / 161 of 200". `12_g5_analysis` (sub 8) shows 162 / 199 / 197, and sub 4 shows 160 / 200 / 200. The paper matches neither.
   - [PENDING] cells for R3 random/D_c and R5c belief-student can be filled from the current JSON: S12 random 0.0754 (n = 199), D_c 0.3506; D3-B Δ̂_succ −19.9 pp, Δ̂_BI −1.01 nats on 298/500 episodes (marked incomplete).
   - [CONST?] placeholders for the PPO clip and epochs are known from the run configs: clip 0.2, 4 epochs. Python 3.12.15 and numpy 2.5.4 are recorded in every run header.
   - §7 says the teacher tie fraction "is not reported", but every distillation log records `val_tie_fraction` = 0.0 under the paper's own tie definition, so it can be reported.
6. **Pre-registered report items were dropped silently.** The §1.9 test table registers Holm-corrected reported rows (student vs BC, vs abs-frame, vs random) and a secondary TOST of student vs G\* at ±δ_c. Neither appears, and no note says they were descoped by the row-4 routing. The paper's statement "there is no correction across claims" is consistent with the pre-registration, but the Holm family should be listed as not run.
7. **Statistical power is weak overall.** Three seeds per learner with seed resampling makes every learner interval seed-dominated (RL per-seed SD of g_T is 3.3× the student's). Success CIs of ±5–7 pp at n = 300 cannot resolve the 2–4 pp differences the paper discusses. The test3 success rows use 300 episodes because sub-8 grid read-out costs 40.6 s per episode. That is an engineering limit, but it does mean C2 is underpowered.
8. **Minor issues:**
   - E′'s single counted flip is in A12 B-fan, yet Table 1 is headed "S12 unless stated" and does not say so.
   - The "finger-relative" context also includes absolute cos/sin φ_t (`relframe.rel_ctx`), and the paper should say so.
   - Adam state is re-initialised at every DAgger round (`cli3.cmd_distill3`), which is undocumented.

### Actionable revisions (B)
- B1. Retrain the students with a non-trivial optimisation budget, e.g. a fixed 50 epochs or patience on a smoothed metric. Report train/held-out loss curves, and drop the "generalisation rather than capacity" sentence unless train top-1 rises well above held-out.
- B2. Extend the RL sweep beyond lr 1.09e-3, with at least 2 seeds per config and entropy and batch variation. Report the entropy at convergence and the probe-count distribution.
- B3. Report the g_T saturation fraction per method and the G\* < learner inversion on g_T. Discuss the cap's effect on H2 power, or re-run with larger L.
- B4. Merge instrument checks (a) and (c) into one, with a note that they share the exact gap. Add an in-domain positive control for the H3 estimator, or soften C1 accordingly. Discuss the max-selection bias and the 22.5° completion grid.
- B5. Fix the stale n = 62/161 counts. Fill all [PENDING]/[CONST?] cells from the current JSON and run headers, marking incomplete rows. Report the tie fraction.
- B6. List the pre-registered but unexecuted items (Holm family, TOST, BC/abs-frame rows, R_b) with the routing reason.

## Overall recommendation

- **Reviewer A: 3 / 10.** The tested regime does not exercise the travel coupling that motivates the paper, both learners are indistinguishable from a stay-and-fan heuristic, the H3 result replicates an estimate known before registration, and the amortization claim omits the reduced-planner baseline.
- **Reviewer B: 4 / 10.** The reporting is accurate and honest, but the student arm is under-optimised (seconds of training), the RL arm is under-tuned (winner at the sweep edge), H2 is tested on a saturating proxy that inverts the G\* ordering, and the H3 instrument checks lack an in-domain positive control.

Neither reviewer would recommend acceptance at RSS/CoRL (or NeurIPS/ICML) in the current form. The work fits a workshop as a pre-registered negative-results report.
