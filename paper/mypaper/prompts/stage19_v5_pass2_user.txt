# Amortizing a Cost-Aware Information-Gain Touch Planner under Travel Cost: A Pre-Registered Five-Cell Characterisation

## Abstract

Active tactile localization chooses each touch from a particle belief over object identity and pose. A cost-aware
greedy information-gain planner (G\*), which scores every feasible touch by expected information per unit of travel
cost, is a strong but slow way to make that choice. We ask whether a policy distilled from G\* matches it, how it
compares with reinforcement learning (RL) on the same objective and with hand-designed probing heuristics, and how the
gap behaves as travel cost changes. A pre-registered five-cell study covers travel cost κ = 2/4/8/12 on one shape
library and a second library, A16, at κ = 2, with five seeds per learner, a fresh test split (test5), and the earlier
single-cell rounds as prior evidence. The pre-registered gate failed in all five cells (m = 0). The student is below
G\* in every cell on both splits (validation 3.2–6.0 pp, with two cells beyond the 4 pp margin; test5 0.2–2.5 pp, CIs
down to −8.3 pp and too wide to decide a match), so matching G\* is not supported. The student beats the
pilot-chosen heuristic in 4/5 cells after Holm correction (+6.1 to +16.8 pp), but against the best heuristic on test5
the post-hoc margin is +3.5 to +14.5 pp, with no CI. Distillation scores higher on RL's own contrastive objective only in A16-2, where RL collapses,
and no inversion between that objective and the posterior read-out is detected. No trend of the gap with planner
travel is detected, but a change of about 7 pp is not excluded. Per decision the student is 2,523–9,933× faster
(seed-701 timings). Simulation, planar shapes, preliminary.

## 1 Introduction

A robot that localizes an object by touch receives sparse and ambiguous measurements: one contact point and one
surface normal per probe, or a miss. A single contact rarely fixes the pose, and near-symmetric object families make
even the identity uncertain. The standard remedy is to maintain a belief over identity and pose, usually a particle
filter, and to choose the next touch by its expected information gain \cite{petrovskaya2011global, hebert2013next,
zheng2024bayesian, zheng2026bayesian}. Greedy information gathering of this kind is backed by (adaptive)
submodularity arguments for touch-based localization \cite{javdani2012efficient}. Probing is not free, however. The
finger must travel to the next approach direction, and travel time competes with probes inside a fixed time budget.
Cost-aware selection therefore scores each candidate by information per unit cost \cite{ottenhaus2018active}, and
reviews of active haptic perception identify this trade-off as central to practical exploration
\cite{seminara2019active}. The price is computation. A cost-aware planner that re-estimates expected information for
every feasible touch at every decision, by nested Monte Carlo over the particle belief, spends up to seconds of
computation per decision on a commodity CPU.

Amortization replaces that online optimisation with a policy network trained once. Policy-based Bayesian experimental
design learns such a policy from a contrastive information bound \cite{foster2021deep, ivanova2021implicit,
blau2022optimizing, hedman2025step, rainforth2024modern}; tactile RL learns exploration policies end to end for
recognition and localization \cite{riou2024reinforcement, niemann2024when}; and a third route imitates an
information-gathering expert \cite{choudhury2017adaptive, nguyen2024amortized}, typically through DAgger's no-regret
reduction \cite{ross2011reduction}. Distilling a validated cost-aware planner is attractive because the planner
supplies a dense per-action score at every visited belief, which removes long-horizon credit assignment and large-action
exploration from the learning problem. The open question is whether a policy distilled in this way keeps the
planner's quality, how it compares with RL trained on the same objective with the same network, and whether the
answer changes when the price of moving changes.

Earlier rounds of this study could not answer the regime question. Each tested one cell per regime: a stay-and-probe
cell at high travel cost, whose learnability gate failed on the first attempt and after its one remedy, and a
travelling cell at low travel cost, whose gate also failed. A review of those rounds asked for more cells per regime
(several κ values and at least one more shape library) and more learner seeds, and for a fresh test split, an RL
configuration tuned in the travelling regime, every heuristic in every cell, and a check of the evaluator's resolution.
The present round pre-registered all of these before any run, in a written amendment and its addenda, including a
learnability gate (D5) with no remedy step, a test-split hypothesis with Holm correction over cells, a cross-cell
trend hypothesis, and a rule for choosing the reference heuristic on pilot data. The paper therefore reports a
pre-registered measurement. It makes no method claim, and its headline is a negative result for the primary question.
Its novelty is correspondingly narrow: distilling an information-gathering expert and amortizing design by RL are both
established, and what this paper adds is a pre-registered, multi-cell, same-head comparison of the two against the
planner, in a planar simulator with three to four probes per episode. We position it as
a measurement, not as evidence that either route works on hardware.

**Findings.** The pre-registered gate failed in all five cells (m = 0). The student is below G\* in every cell on both
splits (validation 3.2–6.0 pp, with S4 and A16-2 beyond the 4 pp margin; test5 0.2–2.5 pp, CIs down to −8.3 pp), so
matching G\* is not supported. The student beats the pilot-chosen heuristic H\* in 4/5 cells after Holm (+6.1 to
+16.8 pp). In 4/5 cells, though, H\* is not the best heuristic on test5, and against the best test5 heuristic the
post-hoc margin is +3.5 to +14.5 pp, with no CI. On RL's own objective, the student is ahead only in A16-2,
where the reused RL configuration collapses, and no inversion between that objective and the posterior read-out is
detected in any cell. The cross-cell trend test detects no trend of the student − G\* success gap on G\* travel over
5 cells, but a change of up to about 7 pp across the observed travel range cannot be excluded. With seed-701 timings,
full G\* is 2,523–9,933× slower per decision than the student.

**Contributions.**
- **C1. Learnability across five cells.** The gate D5 fails in 5/5 cells; the student is below G\* in every cell on
  both splits and is not shown to lie within the pre-registered margin δ. Together with the two earlier rounds, the
  amortization hypothesis has failed its pre-registered gate in every cell tested.
- **C2. A cheap policy that beats pilot-chosen heuristics.** The student exceeds H\* in 4/5 cells under Holm, reported
  beside the post-hoc margins over the best test5 heuristic and the per-decision latency range.
- **C3. Distillation versus RL on the same objective.** The pre-registered advantage on RL's objective holds only in
  A16-2, where RL collapses; the inversion check is false everywhere; the student − RL success contrasts are
  uncorrected.
- **C4. Travel cost and behaviour.** No trend of the gap is detected and about 7 pp is not excluded; the student
  travels less than G\* in every cell.
- **C5. Methods notes.** The evaluator's finite resolution; a report-only one-step look-ahead screen whose headroom
  over greedy G\* is positive in nats on 32 beliefs per cell, together with an upward max-selection bias of that
  screen (not of G\*'s own scores); the differing orderings of the contrastive bound and the posterior read-out; and
  the practice of reporting the validation and test splits separately. These describe the measurement, not the answer,
  and are placed in Sections 4 and 6 rather than in Section 5's decision rows.

Section 2 reviews related work. Section 3 defines the task, the planner, the shared head, the two learners, the
heuristics and the five cells. Section 4 gives the pre-registered protocol and the decision record, Section 5 the
results, and Sections 6–8 discuss, bound and conclude.

## 2 Related Work

**Tactile localization and next-best touch.** Estimating object pose from touch is usually posed as Bayesian
filtering. Scaling-series Monte Carlo achieves global localization from sparse contacts on polygonal meshes
\cite{petrovskaya2011global}; manifold particle filters sample on the contact manifold to avoid degeneracy in planar
contact manipulation \cite{koval2015pose}; and Tac2Pose returns a pose distribution from a single high-resolution touch
\cite{bauza2023tac2pose}. On top of such estimators, information-driven selection picks the next touch. The next best
touch maximises information gain on pose uncertainty \cite{hebert2013next}; Bayesian frameworks jointly track class
and pose with customised particle filters and choose exploratory actions by expected information
\cite{zheng2024bayesian, zheng2026bayesian}; recent work selects contacts by expected information gain for rigid-body
pose and shape \cite{gordon2026active}, or learns one exploration policy on hardware that balances global search
against local refinement \cite{kamtikar2026tactful}. Cost-aware information gain combines expected gain and expected
cost in one score \cite{ottenhaus2018active}, and a review of active haptic perception summarises the space, including
the coverage-style heuristics that remain the usual reference points \cite{seminara2019active}. Several of these
systems run on hardware with 3D geometry and real contact; our study is planar and simulated, and we use it to isolate
the selection question rather than to compete with them. In this line cost-aware greedy selection is common, but it
runs online at every decision. How well it can be amortized, and how that changes with travel cost, is rarely
measured.

**Greedy information gathering and its guarantees.** Submodularity explains why greedy selection works well for touch:
Shannon entropy reduction is submodular under certain assumptions, and adaptive submodularity gives online guarantees
for touch-based localization \cite{javdani2012efficient}. Hierarchical action structure can be folded into the same
framework through sequential submodular optimisation \cite{vien2015touch}. Outside touch, submodular surrogates
approximate the value of information when the objective itself is not submodular \cite{chen2015submodular}, and
semi-adaptive policies show how much adaptivity is needed to retain guarantees \cite{esfandiari2019adaptivity}. Travel
cost couples decisions, because where the finger ends up after one probe sets the price of the next, which is exactly
the kind of structure that weakens these guarantees. All of these results concern the greedy planner itself, not a
learned copy of it. We therefore treat G\* as the reference that a learned policy is measured against, not as an
optimum, and we make no claim about how far G\* is from the optimal policy. A planner with fewer particles, candidates
or outcome samples is the obvious cheap alternative to a learned copy; we time two such configurations but did not
measure their accuracy, so the comparison between shrinking the planner and learning from it remains open.

**Amortized Bayesian experimental design.** Deep adaptive design trains a design network on contrastive information
bounds so that each decision is one forward pass \cite{foster2021deep}. Implicit-likelihood and RL formulations remove
the need for likelihoods or a differentiable simulator \cite{ivanova2021implicit, blau2022optimizing}, and RL has been
applied to sequential and robust experimental design more broadly \cite{asano2022sequential, shen2023reinforcement}.
Semi-amortized variants refine the policy online \cite{hedman2025step}, nested particle filters amortize design in
dynamical systems \cite{iqbal2024recursive}, and transformer policies amortize inference and acquisition jointly
\cite{huang2026aline}. A recent review covers the field \cite{rainforth2024modern}. In this literature a contrastive
bound of the sPCE type is usually both the training objective and the reported quality of a learned policy
\cite{foster2021deep, blau2022optimizing}. Whether that bound ranks policies the same way as a posterior-based
localization read-out is rarely checked, and the bound is seldom compared with a planner-supervised alternative
trained on the same network.

**Learned tactile exploration and imitation of information gathering.** In touch, RL agents explore for recognition
with rewards on correct classification \cite{riou2021seeing, fleer2020efficient}, on classification, exploration and
pose \cite{riou2024reinforcement}, or learn when to stop \cite{niemann2024when}. TANDEM3D co-trains exploration and
decision making on 3D contacts \cite{xu2023tandem3d}, and Tactile MNIST offers a benchmark that includes localization
\cite{schneider2025tactile}. A second route imitates an information-gathering expert: imitation of a clairvoyant oracle
\cite{choudhury2017adaptive} and deep imitation of a nonmyopic active-search expert \cite{nguyen2024amortized}, both
building on DAgger \cite{ross2011reduction}. The two routes have been compared in spirit, but we know of no
pre-registered, multi-cell comparison of planner distillation and RL that uses the same head and action set, evaluates
both on the objective RL itself optimises, and reports the posterior-success read-out beside the contrastive bound.
This paper supplies that comparison across five cells, and it supplies it as a measurement rather than as a new
method.

## 3 Method

### 3.1 Task, belief, cost and metrics

**State, actions and observations.** The object is a planar polygon with identity $k \in \{1,\dots,K\}$ and unknown
pose, so the state is $s = (k, x, y, \theta)$, with a uniform prior over identity, position in a bounded box and
orientation. The finger rests on a stand-off circle at angle $\phi_t$, with a start angle shared by all methods on an
episode. An action $a = (\phi', \psi)$ selects a target angle $\phi'$ and an approach offset $\psi$ from a discrete
grid of 64 target angles (5.625° apart) × 8 approach offsets (−35° to +35° in 10° steps), i.e. 512 actions; probes
start on a circle of radius 100 mm. The
finger travels the shorter way to $\phi'$, probes inward along direction $\phi' + \pi + \psi$, and then
$\phi_{t+1} = \phi'$. A probe returns a contact point and a surface normal, both with Gaussian noise, or a miss
$y = \varnothing$. Every method uses the same softened likelihood $p_\varepsilon(y \mid s, a)$, so differences between
methods come from which actions they choose, not from different sensor models. The belief is a particle filter
$b = \{s_n, w_n\}_{n=1}^{N}$ over identity and pose \cite{petrovskaya2011global, koval2015pose}.

**Travel cost and budget.** In units of one probe stroke,

$$c(a) = 1 + \kappa\,\frac{|\Delta\phi|}{\pi}, \qquad \Delta\phi = \mathrm{wrap}(\phi' - \phi_t) \in (-\pi, \pi],$$

so κ is the time to travel half the circle. With
remaining time $\tau_{\mathrm{rem}}$, the feasible set is $\mathcal{F}(\tau_{\mathrm{rem}}) = \{a : c(a) \le
\tau_{\mathrm{rem}}\}$; every method chooses only within it, and the episode ends when no action is feasible. The
budget τ of each cell is set by a calibration rule so that G\* executes about $n^\*$ probes: τ is derived from G\*'s
mean unbudgeted travel time over 200 calibration episodes (Table 0).

**Metrics.** From the evaluator's posterior after the last probe, the identity estimate is the maximum-mass identity
and the pose estimate is refined locally. *Success* requires the correct identity and position and orientation errors
inside fixed tolerances, orientation taken modulo the shape's symmetry group; it is read from an adaptive grid
posterior at subdivision 8 (sub 8). With $B(s_0)$ the success ball around the true state, the *ball information* is
$\mathrm{BI}(h) = \log \hat P(B(s_0) \mid h) - \log P(B(s_0))$ in nats. The RL objective $g_T$ (ball-sPCE, Section 3.3)
is computed without the grid. *Travel* is the total angular distance the finger covers in an episode, including the
first leg from the continuous start angle onto the action grid, which no policy chooses; it is logged in radians and
degrees. We also log the number of probes.

**Cells.** Shape library S has $K = 8$ identities, four near-symmetric base shapes and one symmetry-breaking variant of
each. Library A16 is the second library, with $K = 16$ identities: star-shaped irregular polygons with 8 to 14
vertices, each with a trivial symmetry group, rejection-sampled for a minimum edge length and a cap on
self-confusability (`code/touchbed/shapes.py`). A16 therefore differs from S in three ways at once, the shape family
(asymmetric irregular instead of near-symmetric with variants), the number of identities $K$ and the probe count
$n^\*$, and it is run at one travel cost only; the A16-2 cell cannot separate these factors from one another or from
travel cost. The five pre-registered cells are listed in Table 0. For A16 the
probe count $n^\* = 3$ came from the pre-registered rule, and a keep check at κ = 0 and τ = $n^\*$ on 200 calibration
episodes gave G\* success 0.8, inside the pre-registered keep range [0.30, 0.90].

**Table 0. The five cells.** τ is set by the calibration rule from G\*'s mean unbudgeted travel time (stroke units) over
200 calibration episodes; the S2 and S12 values come from the earlier rounds.

### 3.2 Planner G\*

G\* selects

$$G^\*(b) = \arg\max_{a \in \mathcal{F}(\tau_{\mathrm{rem}})} \frac{\mathrm{EIG}_B(a; b)}{c(a)},$$

where the ball EIG, the expected one-step increment of $\log P(B(s_0)\mid h)$, is estimated by nested Monte Carlo with
$j_m \sim w$ and $y_m \sim p_\varepsilon(\cdot \mid s_{j_m}, a)$:

$$\mathrm{EIG}_B(a;b) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Big[\log\!\!\sum_{n \in B(s_{j_m})}\!\! w_n\, p_\varepsilon(y_m \mid s_n,a)
- \log\!\!\sum_{n \in B(s_{j_m})}\!\! w_n - \log \sum_n w_n\, p_\varepsilon(y_m \mid s_n, a)\Big].$$

The deployed configuration is `g2000_512_64`: $N = 2{,}000$ particles, all $C = 512$
candidate actions scored, and $M = 64$ sampled outcomes per candidate. Two reduced configurations, named the same way,
`g125_512_16` and `g500_512_4`, are timed in the latency study (Section 4) and nowhere else; their accuracy was not
measured in this round, so they serve as timing references and not as evaluated baselines. At each decision G\*
exports the score vector $s(a) = \max(0, \mathrm{EIG}_B(a;b))/c(a)$ over feasible actions (zero elsewhere), whose argmax
is G\*'s action; this vector is the distillation label. **Complexity.** Per decision, G\* evaluates $O(MNC)$ likelihoods for $C$ scored
candidates and builds ball-neighbour lists in $O(N^2)$, on top of assimilating the last observation; the latter two
terms do not depend on $C$. G\* is a greedy reference: we measure a learned policy against it and make no claim that
it is optimal \cite{javdani2012efficient, ottenhaus2018active}.

### 3.3 One shared head, two learners

**Head.** Both learners use the same belief-free DeepSets head [CITE-MISSING: DeepSets (Zaheer et al. 2017)]. Each
touch in the history is encoded in the finger frame (contact indicator, contact point, normal and the action that
produced it), summed, and concatenated with a context of the relative angle, the remaining-budget fraction
$\tau_{\mathrm{rem}}/\tau$ and the absolute finger angle $\phi_t$. The head outputs one logit per action, masked to
$-\infty$ outside $\mathcal{F}(\tau_{\mathrm{rem}})$. Architecture, input and action set are held fixed, so the
training signal is the isolated variable. Re-encoding the history costs $O(T^2H^2 + THA)$ for $T$ touches, hidden width
$H$ and $A$ actions, with no particle filter in the loop.

**Distillation.** The student $\pi_\theta$ minimises a soft cross-entropy to tempered teacher scores plus a hard term on
the teacher's argmax,

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\sum_{a \in \mathcal{F}} q(a)\log \pi_\theta(a \mid h) - \lambda \log \pi_\theta(a^\* \mid h),
\qquad q = \mathrm{softmax}\big(\beta_T\, s(a)/\sigma_s\big)_{a \in \mathcal{F}},$$

with $\sigma_s$ the per-decision standard deviation of $s$ over feasible actions and $a^\* = \arg\max_a s(a)$. Held-out
quality is the floored normalised label regret

$$r = \frac{s_{\max} - s(a_\pi)}{\max(s_{\max} - s_{\mathrm{med}},\ \rho\, s_{\max})},$$

averaged over held-out decisions to $\bar R$. No success-based selection is used. Training follows Algorithm 1 with
seeds 701–705 and the same hyper-parameters (hp 0) in every cell \cite{ross2011reduction, choudhury2017adaptive}.

> **Algorithm 1 (DAgger distillation of G\*, v5 recipe).**
> 1. Round r0: roll out G\* on label episodes, storing $(h, \phi_t, \tau_{\mathrm{rem}}, \mathcal{F}, s, a^\*)$ at every
>    decision; hold out a fixed block of decisions for $\bar R$.
> 2. Train $\pi_\theta$ on $\mathcal{L}$ for a fixed number of epochs; after each epoch evaluate held-out $\bar R$;
>    **keep the checkpoint with the lowest held-out $\bar R$**.
> 3. For rounds r1 and r2 with a decaying mixing coefficient $\beta_{\mathrm{DAgger}}$: roll out episodes executing
>    G\*'s action with probability $\beta_{\mathrm{DAgger}}$ and $\pi_\theta$'s otherwise; run G\*'s filter alongside,
>    label every visited belief with $s$, aggregate, and repeat step 2.
> 4. Freeze the round-r2 kept checkpoint; record its weight hash before any test episode.
>
> Budgets: 3,000 labelled G\* episodes per cell (300 held out for $\bar R$, so round r0 trains on 2,700); two DAgger
> rounds of 800 episodes each ($\beta_{\mathrm{DAgger}}$ = 0.5, then 0; 3,500 and 4,300 training episodes); a fixed 200
> epochs per round. hp 0: Adam, learning rate 0.001, batch 256, hidden width 128, $\beta_T = 4.0$, λ = 0.5, regret
> floor ρ = 0.05.

Keeping the best held-out checkpoint is the pre-declared answer to an over-fitting finding of the previous round,
where held-out regret rose after an early minimum under a last-checkpoint recipe. One qualifier applies to the label
"DAgger". The held-out block on which $\bar R$ is measured consists of G\*-visited decisions, so the selection metric
cannot register what the DAgger rounds are meant to add, namely labels on student-visited beliefs. In the training
record the kept $\bar R$ of round r2 is hardly distinguishable from that of round r0 in any cell (Section 5.1,
report-only), so the deployed student is a DAgger-trained model that, on this metric, is not separable from its
behaviour-cloning round.

**RL.** The RL policy uses the same head and action set and is trained with PPO \cite{schulman2017proximal} and a
critic baseline. Its reward is the increment of ball-sPCE, a contrastive estimate of ball information in the style of
\cite{foster2021deep, blau2022optimizing}: with $L$ contrastive states $s_{1:L} \sim p$ and $J$ states $s_0^{(j)}$ drawn
uniformly in $B(s_0)$,

$$g_t = \log \hat p_B(h_t \mid s_0) - \log\Big[\tfrac{1}{L+1}\Big(\hat p_B(h_t \mid s_0) + \sum_{l=1}^{L} p(h_t \mid
s_l)\Big)\Big], \qquad \hat p_B(h \mid s_0) = \tfrac{1}{J}\sum_j p(h \mid s_0^{(j)}),$$

and $r_t = g_t - g_{t-1}$, so the return telescopes to $g_T$, RL's own objective. The configuration was swept on S2
only: 8 configurations, each trained with a single seed (719) for 100,096 episodes (100,032 at batch 64) and selected by the earlier rounds'
rule, highest final ball-sPCE on the trainer's validation stream (92,160 episodes), ties within 0.02 nats to the lower
index. The rule selected cfg 6 (learning rate 1.257e-3, entropy coefficient 9.504e-4, batch 256, critic learning rate
4.925e-4), with final validation ball-sPCE 6.300 against 6.241 for cfg 0, the configuration of the earlier rounds. On
the sampled-policy version of the same metric, cfg 1 scores higher (6.316 against 6.205 for cfg 6). cfg 6 is reused in
every cell with seeds 711–715, each trained to the 2 × 10⁵-episode cap (200,192 episodes logged for every run; PPO clip 0.2,
4 epochs per update, hidden width 128). A
control arm, `rl_success`, replaces the reward by a terminal-success reward and is run in S2 and S12 only. Nine of its
ten runs were stopped by the trainer's wall-clock time guard (80 % of the 330-min job budget; addendum v5.1c) before
the episode cap, and the trainer has no resume, so the time-guard-saved weights are the models of record; their
per-seed training lengths are printed beside its results (Section 5.3). The reward contains no explicit term for probe
count or travel; both enter only through the budget.

### 3.4 Heuristics

Random selects uniformly among feasible actions. Three heuristics reserve budget for the remaining probes.
**B-spread** (belief-free) maximises angular distance to the previous probe angles. **B-fan** (belief-free,
travel-nearest) stays at $\phi_t$ when allowed and cycles the approach offset, otherwise moving to the nearest allowed
angle. **B-cov** (belief-using) maximises coverage under an online filter. In each cell the reference heuristic H\* is
the best of the three on pilot episodes 0–199 (sub 8), with ties broken B-spread < B-fan < B-cov. The pilot chose
B-cov in S2 and S8 and B-fan in S4, S12 and A16-2 (Table 1). All three heuristics and Random are run on test5 in every
cell.

### 3.5 Decision rules

Five pre-registered decisions are read: the validation gate D5, the test5 hypothesis H5a (student − G\* success, Holm
over the five cells), the student − H\* contrast (Holm over the five cells), H2-v5 (student − RL on $g_T$) with its
inversion check, and the cross-cell trend H5b. Section 4 states each rule and Table 1 records each outcome.

## 4 Experiments

**Pre-registration.** Amendment v5.0, written before any run of this round, fixes the five cells, the seeds, the fresh
test split test5, the gate D5, the hypotheses H5a, H5b and H2-v5, the H\* selection rule and the Holm families. Amendment
v5.1 fills in values that the frozen rules produce: the budgets τ, the A16 probe count $n^\*$ and the RL configuration
cfg 6. Its addenda fix the E7 design and require the `rl_success` training lengths to be printed beside every
`rl_success` result. Addendum v5.1e cuts test5 from 500 to 300 episodes per method and cell, under the frozen reduction
order and before any test5 episode was run. Every decision in this paper is read off a rule fixed in these documents;
values that no rule produced are labelled report-only, exploratory or post hoc.

**Deviations from the registration.** Four departures are stated here rather than left to the reader. (a) Order of
reductions. The frozen reduction order was (1) skip E7, (2) cut test5 from 500 to 300 episodes, (3) drop S8, (4) cut
val3. E7 was decided and run in addendum v5.1d (about 1 CPU-h), one run before test5 was sized for the first time in
addendum v5.1e; when that sizing projected more than the compute limit, step (1) was no longer available, so step (2)
applied and the primary test split lost 200 of its 500 episodes per method and cell. The cut was made before any
test5 episode and depended only on wall-time records, but a different ordering of the two addenda would have kept the
larger test split. (b) The latency–accuracy frontier. Amendment v5.0 promised a latency–accuracy frontier figure for
E6, and addendum v5.1f kept the frontier as a read-out of the job-1 timings. No reduced-G\* accuracy was measured on
test5, so the reduced configurations were timed only and no frontier is reported. (c) Flip rates per contrast.
Amendment v5.0 asked that every success contrast be reported next to the measured flip rate. E5 measured flip rates
in two cells (S2, S12) for one student seed (701) only, so the other cells and seeds have no flip rate of their own and
the E5 caveat is applied to them generically. (d) The `rl_success` control was truncated by the trainer's time guard
in 9 of its 10 runs (addendum v5.1c); its rows describe shorter training than registered.

**Splits.** All splits are disjoint episode streams, and within a split every method runs on the same episodes with the
same initial state, start angle and observation stream (a paired design). D5 uses validation episodes val3 0–499 (500
per cell). H5a, the heuristic and RL contrasts, H2-v5 and H5b use test5 0–299, with 300 episodes per method per cell;
every test5 block, including the $g_T$ blocks, is complete. H\* is chosen on pilot3 0–199 (200 episodes per heuristic)
and τ on calibration episodes cal3 0–199. The G\* validation rows of D5 do not all come from this round: S2 uses the
previous round's files, S12 those of the round before, and S4, S8 and A16-2 new runs. Before any test5 job, the weight
hashes of every learner were recorded, 15 per cell in S2 and S12 (students, RL and `rl_success`) and 10 per cell in S4,
S8 and A16-2; that the recording preceded test5 is taken from the run context.

**Gate D5.** On the 500 paired val3 episodes with five student seeds, D5 passes iff three clauses hold. (i) The success
deficit clears an SE-dependent threshold, $\hat\Delta_{\mathrm{succ}} \ge \max(-0.02,\, -\delta + 1.163\,\mathrm{SE})$ with δ = 0.04
(for example −0.01454 in S2). (ii) The BI deficit satisfies $\hat\Delta_{\mathrm{BI}} \ge -0.15$ nats. (iii) At least 4 of the 5
student seeds have $\Delta_{\mathrm{succ}} > -\delta$. The
SE is the standard deviation of an episode × student-seed bootstrap; because the threshold adds a multiple of SE to −δ,
it becomes stricter as the paired SE grows. At the paired SEs observed on val3 the effective threshold of clause (i)
lies between −1.2 pp (A16-2) and −1.7 pp (S12) (Table 1), so the gate is far tighter than the nominal δ = 4 pp
suggests: a cell whose deficit lies inside the 4 pp margin can still fail clause (i), as S8 does at −3.6 pp while
passing clauses (ii) and (iii). No remedy round follows a failure. The number m of cells that pass D5 is
the registered H5a validation count.

**Statistics.** Test5 contrasts use a crossed episode × seed bootstrap for the two-sided ci95 and p-value, with
`n_seeds` = [5, 1] against G\* and H\* (G\* and the heuristics are run once per cell) and [5, 5] against RL, with 10,000
resamples. Bootstrap p-values resolve to 0.0002, so a reported p of zero is printed as
p < 0.0002. Holm's step-down procedure over the five cells is applied to two families only, student − G\* success (H5a)
and student − H\* success [CITE-MISSING: Holm (1979) step-down procedure]. H2-v5 is met iff the one-sided 95 % lower
bound of $g_T(\text{student}) - g_T(\text{RL})$ exceeds +0.10 nats. The registered inversion check asks, per cell,
whether $g_T$ and the posterior success read-out order the student and RL in opposite directions, and it is claimed
only where both orderings are resolved, that is, where the ci95 of the $g_T$ contrast and the ci95 of the success
contrast both exclude 0 (amendment v5.0, H2-v5); it is run for cfg-6 RL in every cell and for `rl_success` in S2 and
S12. H5b is a weighted least-squares slope of the five test5 success deficits on G\*'s mean test5 travel, read through
the ci95 of the slope. Only the weighting rule was fixed in advance (addendum v5.1f, before any test5 number was read):
each cell is weighted by the inverse bootstrap variance of its test5 success deficit, held fixed across replicates.
The weight values themselves are therefore estimated from test5. All BI, RL, `rl_success` and $g_T$ contrasts are uncorrected and
exploratory [CITE-MISSING: bootstrap confidence intervals (Efron)].

**Methods note: evaluator resolution (E5).** Success is a thresholded read-out of a finite-resolution grid posterior.
E5 re-reads 200 single-seed episodes of G\* and student seed 701 in S2 and S12 at sub 16 instead of sub 8. The success
outcome flips on 8 % (G\*) and 7 % (student) of S2 episodes and on 7.5 % and 6.5 % of S12 episodes. G\* success moves
from 0.54 to 0.54 in S2 (8 gains, 8 losses) and from 0.48 to 0.505 in S12 (10 gains, 5 losses); the student moves from
0.465 to 0.485 (9, 5) and from 0.455 to 0.45 (6, 7). The mean |ΔBI| is at most 0.004034 nats. Re-reading at sub 16
therefore flips 6.5–8 % of single-seed outcomes and shifts the seed-701 student − G\* contrast by +0.02 (S2) and −0.03
(S12) on 200 episodes, about 0.8× the test5 gap in S2 and about 2× in S12; its effect on the 5-seed test5 contrast was
not measured. Every test5 success contrast in Section 5 should be read next to this caveat.

**Methods note: look-ahead headroom over greedy (E7, report-only).** E7 does not measure G\*'s own value estimates. It
asks how much a one-step look-ahead could add to greedy selection (the H3 headroom question of the earlier rounds). On
32 G\*-reached beliefs per cell (beliefs 0–31) in S2 and S12, a rollout look-ahead screens C1 first actions, completes
each episode with the greedy comparator, and takes the maximum screened value $V_{\mathrm{LA}}$; both the selected
look-ahead action and G\*'s own first action are then re-valued on an independent stream with 256 outcome samples.
Two settings are run on the same beliefs, C1 = 32 (the estimator of the earlier rounds) and C1 = 512 (all feasible
first actions; mean candidates 29.0 and 302.0 in S2, 30.5 and 85.75 in S12). The *independent-estimate gap*,
independent $V_{\mathrm{LA}}$ minus the independent value of G\*'s action, is the look-ahead's headroom over greedy in
nats. At C1 = 512 it is +0.0658 nats per belief, ci95 [0.0297, 0.1084], in S2 and +0.04216, ci95 [0.02503, 0.06138], in
S12, both excluding 0; as a ratio of means the relative gap is 0.03390, ci95 [0.01415, 0.06532] (S2), and 0.01961,
ci95 [0.01053, 0.03411] (S12). At C1 = 32 the gap is +0.01743, ci95 [−0.008133, 0.04600], in S2 and +0.02386, ci95
[0.008307, 0.03959], in S12, and the paired 512 − 32 difference is +0.04838 nats, ci95 [0.01681, 0.08697], in S2 and
+0.01830, ci95 [0.004013, 0.03652], in S12 (n = 32). The *selection bias*, selected minus independent estimate of the
look-ahead's gap, is a max-selection bias of the screen itself: it is positive with a CI excluding 0 in S2 (0.01926,
ci95 [0.001030, 0.04193], at C1 = 32; 0.02179, ci95 [0.004270, 0.04013], at 512) and is not detected in S12 (0.004470,
ci95 [−0.007718, 0.01884]; −0.002040, ci95 [−0.01574, 0.01258]). Three limits bound the reading. The headroom is in
nats of ball information on 32 beliefs per cell, the look-ahead works on 500 particles resampled from G\*'s decision
filter rather than in the deployed `g2000_512_64` configuration, and whether the look-ahead headroom translates into success was not measured. E7 thus shows that one
step of look-ahead improves on greedy in the information metric in both cells; it says nothing about noise or bias in
G\*'s own EIG/c scores, and it supports no claim about G\*'s headroom in success terms.

**Latency protocol (E6).** Job 1 times student seed 701, full G\* and the two reduced configurations interleaved on the
same G\*-reached decision states, n = 1000 decisions in each S cell and 500 in A16-2 (its job-1 completeness flag is
true in A16-2 and false in the four S cells). Job 2 times students 702–705 separately, n = 500 decisions each. A
per-cell ratio is reported with its bootstrap ci95 over decisions and the pre-registered half-width check; this
bootstrap uses B = 2,000 resamples (`code/analysis_e6_v5.py`), not the 10,000 of the test5 contrasts. The latency is
per decision of compute on a 2-core runner; no physical time model of a probe (approach, contact, retract) is part of
the protocol, so the timings are not converted into task time. The pre-registered latency–accuracy frontier was not
produced (see Deviations above).

**Implementation.** Everything is numpy with hand-written backpropagation and single-threaded BLAS, run on
GitHub-hosted 2-core runners (CPU model not logged); runners differ across cells. Table S lists the settings that the
results record holds; the remaining design constants (action grid, planner sizes, label and DAgger budgets, RL
episodes per seed, bootstrap resamples) are taken from the code and the amendments, not from a results field.

## 5 Results

### 5.1 C1. Does the student match G\*?

At 300 episodes per method, the test5 student − G\* intervals span roughly ±5–6 pp around their point estimates
against a margin δ of 4 pp, so the main test5 comparison can decide neither "matches G\*" nor "is worse than G\*"; only
the validation gate D5 is resolved, and the C1 answer rests on it.

**D5 on validation.** The pre-registered gate failed in all five cells (m = 0; Table 1). In every cell the success
deficit is below its threshold, while the BI clause passes in 5/5. All 25 per-seed success differences are negative,
from −0.068 to −0.01, and no A16-2 seed passes the per-seed clause. Validation point gaps are 3.2–6.0 pp, with S4 and
A16-2 beyond the 4 pp margin. The G\* validation rows come from three rounds (S2 from the previous round, S12 from the
round before, the other cells from this one), so the five validation gaps are not drawn from one G\* run.

**H5a on test5.** On the fresh split the student is again below G\* in every cell (Table 2b): test5 point gaps are
0.2–2.5 pp, with 95 % CIs from −8.3 to +6.1 pp, and no pooled estimate was computed. Holm rejects in 0/5 cells, and
equivalence is not shown: the CIs are 9.6–12.3 pp wide. A shortfall of up to 6.1 pp (the tightest lower bound, S8) is
consistent with the data in every cell, and up to 8.3 pp in S4. Test5 point gaps are smaller than the validation gaps
in every cell. A post-hoc, approximate check, which is not a registered read-out, applies clause (i) of the D5 rule to
test5 with each SE approximated as the ci95 half-width divided by 1.96; under that approximation the rule would still
fail in about 4 of 5 cells, so test5 does not contradict the D5 verdict. The exception is A16-2, which is the worst
cell on validation (−6.0 pp, 0/5 seeds within δ) and has the smallest test5 point gap (−0.2 pp). This reversal
between the splits is the largest disagreement in the record. Its G\* validation rows were run in this round, so it is
not an artefact of mixing G\* sources; with test5 intervals of about ±6 pp in that cell it is compatible with sampling
noise, and we do not interpret it further. Each of these success contrasts sits within the evaluator-resolution caveat
of E5 (Section 4).

**BI on test5 (uncorrected).** The student − G\* BI ci95 excludes 0 in S4 and S12 only (Table 2b). In A16-2 the BI
point estimate is positive, with a CI that includes 0.

**Prior cells, the previous test gap, and the teacher's variance.** The two earlier rounds failed their gates in S12
(twice) and S2 (Table 1). The previous round's test3 gap (−10.2 pp, ci95 [−16.6, −4.3]; different test split and
training recipe) lies outside the v5 S2 test5 ci95 [−7.6, +2.7] pp; the two were not formally compared. With this
round, the amortization hypothesis has now failed its pre-registered gate in every cell tested across three rounds.
G\* was run once per cell (`n_seeds` = [5, 1]), so any run-to-run variability of G\*, which uses nested Monte Carlo
estimates, is missing from the student − G\* CIs, and those CIs may be too narrow. This variability was not measured
in this round. E7 does not measure it either: E7 is a report-only check of a one-step look-ahead screen, which shows
positive look-ahead headroom over greedy in nats in S2 and S12 (+0.0658 and +0.04216 nats at C1 = 512, both CIs
excluding 0) and a max-selection bias of that screen in S2 (Section 4). Success-terms headroom was not measured, so
E7 bears on G\* as a reference rather than an optimum, not on the width of the student − G\* intervals.

**Checkpoint sensitivity (report-only).** By construction the kept checkpoint is never worse than the last epoch on
held-out $\bar R$; the training record shows the size of that difference. In every seed × round run of every cell, the
last epoch's held-out $\bar R$ exceeds the kept one, by 0.0002753 to 0.03172. Kept $\bar R$ lies in 0.2773–0.2857 in
S2 (1161 held-out decisions), 0.2230–0.2378 in S4 (1180), 0.1764–0.1859 in S8 (1179), 0.1647–0.1778 in S12 (1196) and
0.1123–0.1178 in A16-2 (880), and the kept epochs range from 0 to 187. Whether the last checkpoint would change success
was not tested.

**DAgger rounds and held-out regret (report-only).** The same record shows how little the DAgger rounds move the

## 6 Discussion

**What the five cells say about amortization.** Across four travel costs on one library and a second library, a cheap
distilled policy sits below its teacher in every cell and is not shown to lie within the 4 pp margin. The two splits
tell the same story at different magnitudes: the test5 point gaps lie below the validation gaps but come with wide
intervals, and neither split, read under its own rule, supports a match. We report the splits separately and computed
no pooled estimate, because the validation rows of G\* come from three rounds and test5 is the only split on which
every method ran in this round. Compared with the earlier single-cell rounds, the added cells do not reveal a regime in
which the gate passes; they replace two anecdotes with five measurements that agree in sign.

**What the student does buy.** Against hand-designed heuristics chosen on a pilot, the student wins in 4/5 cells under
Holm, and it remains ahead of the best test5 heuristic in every cell on point estimates, although by a smaller,
post-hoc margin with no interval. It does so at a per-decision cost far below that of full G\*, by a factor that differs between cells (Section 5.2).
For a practitioner, the result is therefore a cheap policy that sits between the heuristics and the planner on
success, not a substitute for the planner; whether the remaining shortfall is acceptable depends on the application
and on the evaluator caveat of E5. Two qualifiers limit even this reading. First, the latency is reported per decision
of compute, without a physical time model: we did not model probe duration, nor whether planning runs in series with
or in parallel to motion, so the speed-up of 2,523–9,933× is not translated into any saving of task time, and with
three to four probes per episode a planner that takes seconds per decision may or may not dominate the time of a real
probing cycle. Second, the reduced G\* configurations are already more than an order of magnitude faster than full G\* (Table 4),
and their success was not measured, so this study cannot say whether learning is needed to obtain a fast policy at
all, rather than a cheaper planner.

**What the RL comparison says.** RL on the same objective, with one configuration tuned on S2, does not transfer to
A16-2, where it collapses to fewer probes than the budget allows. The one cell in which distillation scores higher on
RL's own objective is therefore the cell in which the comparator failed, and in the other cells no advantage either way
is resolved on $g_T$. The contrast between the routes is thus a statement about this RL setup, not about RL in general
\cite{blau2022optimizing}.

**The contrastive bound and the posterior read-out (C5).** On test5 point estimates, G\* has the lowest mean $g_T$ of
the three policies in all four S cells, while it has the highest success (Tables 2a and 3). Every G\* − learner $g_T$
CI includes 0 in the S cells, and the registered inversion check is false everywhere. The observation is only that the
contrastive bound and the posterior read-out can rank policies differently; it is not a finding. Amortized design work
that reports sPCE-type bounds as the quality of a learned policy \cite{foster2021deep} may still wish to check the
ordering against a posterior-based metric, and semi-amortized schemes that refine a policy online
\cite{hedman2025step} or keep a cheaper nested filter in the loop \cite{iqbal2024recursive} are natural designs in
which that check could be built in. A second methods note concerns the reference itself. The report-only E7 check
finds that a one-step look-ahead screen improves on greedy G\* in nats of ball information on 32 beliefs per cell in
both S2 and S12, and that the screen's own maximum is biased upward in S2. This is consistent with treating G\* as a
reference rather than an optimum; because success-terms headroom was not measured, it is not evidence that G\* is
improvable in success, and it carries no information about noise in G\*'s own EIG/c scores.

**Open questions.** Does label regret measured on student-visited states, rather than G\*-visited ones, explain the
shortfall? Would per-cell RL tuning change the RL rows outside S2? How much would a second G\* run per cell widen the
student − G\* intervals? Why does the student travel less than G\* in every cell while staying below it on success?
Do the reduced G\* configurations keep full G\*'s success at a fraction of its latency, and what does the comparison
look like under a physical time model of a probe?

## 7 Limitations

1. **Power and equivalence.** Test5 has 300 episodes per method and cell (cut from 500 by addendum v5.1e), its
   student − G\* CIs are 9.6–12.3 pp wide, and no equivalence test was run.
2. **G\* run once.** G\* was run once per cell, so its run-to-run variance is missing from the student − G\* CIs, which
   may therefore be too narrow; that variance was not measured. E7 does not measure it: E7 is a report-only one-step
   look-ahead screen on 32 beliefs per cell, in nats, with a 500-particle look-ahead rather than the deployed
   configuration; it shows look-ahead headroom over greedy in nats and a selection bias of the screen, and
   success-terms headroom was not measured.
3. **Evaluator.** Success is read at sub 8, and the sub-16 check (E5) covers 2 cells, 200 episodes and one student seed,
   so its effect on the five-seed test5 contrasts is unknown.
4. **Heuristic selection.** H\* came from a 200-episode pilot and is not the best test5 heuristic in 4/5 cells; the
   margins over the best test5 heuristic are post hoc and untested. B-spread scores below Random in 4/5 cells, so it
   is a weak reference, and no information-based cheap heuristic was included.
5. **RL.** One configuration, selected on S2 by a sweep with a single seed (719), is used everywhere and collapses in
   A16-2; the `rl_success` arm exists only in S2 and S12, and nine of its ten runs were stopped by the trainer's
   wall-clock time guard before the episode cap (training lengths in Section 5.3).
6. **Multiplicity.** More than 30 BI, RL, `rl_success` and $g_T$ contrasts are reported without correction.
7. **Latency.** The CI rule is met in 1/5 cells, the ratios rest on one student seed, their bootstrap uses
   B = 2,000 resamples, and the runners differ across cells. No physical time model of a probe is used, so no task-time
   benefit is shown. The reduced G\* configurations were timed but never evaluated on test5, so the cheapest
   alternative to learning, a smaller planner, is unevaluated, and the pre-registered latency–accuracy frontier was
   not produced.
8. **Validation sources.** The G\* validation rows of D5 come from three rounds (S2 previous round, S12 the round before).
9. **Scope.** Simulation only, planar shapes, a single fingertip on a fixed circle, a likelihood shared by planner,
   filter and evaluator, horizons of three to four probes, two shape libraries, one κ for A16, the sub-8 read-out, and
   H5b fitted on five points; no hardware, 3D objects or held-out shapes were tested. A16 changes the shape family, K
   and $n^\*$ at once, so library effects cannot be separated from one another or from travel cost.
10. **Not run, by decision.** Retraining or remedy rounds, per-cell RL re-tuning, more test5 episodes, a G\* rerun,
    latency re-timing, a test of the post-hoc margins, and any new BI or $g_T$ test were not run.
11. **Design constants.** Several design constants (action grid, planner sizes, label and DAgger budgets, RL episodes per
    seed, bootstrap resamples) are not yet in a citable results field; they are taken from the code and the
    amendments.
12. **Deviations.** The test5 cut followed from E7 having run before the test5 sizing; the latency–accuracy frontier
    and per-contrast flip rates were registered but not delivered (Section 4).
13. **DAgger.** Checkpoints are selected on regret over G\*-visited decisions, on which the r0 and r2 kept $\bar R$
    ranges overlap in every cell, so the benefit of the DAgger rounds is not shown on the selection metric.

## 8 Conclusion

We asked whether a policy distilled from a cost-aware greedy information-gain touch planner can stand in for it under
travel cost, how it compares with RL on the same objective and with hand-designed heuristics, and whether the gap
changes with travel cost. A pre-registered study across five cells gives four answers. First, the pre-registered gate
fails in all five cells, so matching G\* is not supported; the student is below G\* in every cell on both splits.
Second, the student beats the pilot-chosen heuristics in 4/5 cells and decides 2,523–9,933× faster per decision
(seed-701 timings), with smaller, post-hoc margins over the best test5 heuristic. Third, distillation scores higher on
RL's own objective only where RL collapses, and no inversion between that objective and the posterior read-out is
detected. Fourth, no travel-cost trend in the gap is detected, and a change of about 7 pp is not excluded. Concrete
next steps follow from the record rather than from new claims: regret measured on student-visited states, per-cell RL
tuning, repeated G\* runs to put the teacher's variance into the intervals, a finer evaluator check on all seeds, an
accuracy measurement of the reduced planner configurations under a physical time model, and more test episodes for an
equivalence test. The contribution is a measurement in a planar simulator, not a method. Pre-registered; simulation;
preliminary.

## Reviewer A (methodology and evidence audit)

**Summary.** The paper is a pre-registered, five-cell measurement. It asks whether a DeepSets policy distilled by
DAgger from a cost-aware greedy EIG/c touch planner (G\*) matches that planner, and how it compares with PPO on the
same head and with three heuristics. The cells are κ ∈ {2, 4, 8, 12} on library S and κ = 2 on a 16-shape library
A16. The registered gate D5 fails in all five cells. On test5 the student is 0.2–2.5 pp below G\*, with CIs about
10–12 pp wide. The student beats the pilot-chosen heuristic in 4/5 cells under Holm. H2-v5 holds only where RL
collapses (A16-2), and no trend with travel is detected.

**Strengths.**
1. The pre-registration discipline is real and auditable. The rules in `docs/07c_amendment_v5_0.md` are implemented
   line for line in `code/analysis_d5_v5.py` and `code/analysis_test5_v5.py`: the D5 threshold, Holm families, the
   H2-v5 one-sided LB, the H5b WLS and the H\* tie order. The freeze hashes and their place in `PROGRESS.md` precede
   the test5 dispatch.
2. The reporting is unusually candid. There is a Deviations paragraph, validation and test are reported separately,
   the G\* sources are mixed across rounds and flagged, the `rl_success` truncation is printed per seed, the D5
   threshold is shown to be tighter than δ, and the DAgger rounds are shown not to move the selection metric.
3. Every headline number I checked reproduces from the JSON and, where I recounted them, from the raw logs (checks
   3–4, 38–43).
4. The design is paired (shared episodes, start angle and observation stream), and the bootstrap crosses episodes
   and seeds, so student-seed variance is in the CIs.

**Weaknesses.**
1. **The reduced-planner question is misstated (mismatch 1, major).** The paper says four times that the accuracy of
   `g125_512_16` and `g500_512_4` is unknown, and it calls the missing frontier a deviation forced by missing data.
   Addendum v5.1f, which the paper cites for this point, says the opposite. The v4 E5 S12 rows are the registered
   accuracy side of the frontier, and those rows exist. They show both reduced planners 5–7 pp below full G\* on S12
   test3, with the g125 CI excluding 0. They also show both within about 3 pp of the v4 students on the same
   episodes. The split, round and student recipe all differ from v5, so these rows cannot be compared like for like
   with the v5 student. They are still exactly the evidence the Discussion says does not exist. They bear directly
   on whether learning is needed, rather than a cheaper planner.
2. **The RL tuning is thin, and the description of it is inaccurate (mismatch 2).** The sweep has 8 configurations,
   one seed and 10⁵ episodes, and selection rests on a 500-episode validation read at 92,160 training episodes, not
   on "a 92,160-episode stream". The cfg 6 vs cfg 0 margin, 0.059 nats on a single seed, is barely above the
   0.02-nat tie band. On the sampled metric cfg 1 would win. With RL collapsing in A16-2 and using about 3 probes in
   every S cell, the comparison of distillation against RL is against a weakly tuned comparator, outside S2 at least.
   The paper acknowledges this, but the C3 framing still reads as a comparison of two routes.
3. **The gate cannot pass where the paper's language suggests it could.** With SE ≈ 0.02, clause (i) needs a deficit
   of about −1.5 pp or better. That is stricter than the "4 pp margin" named in the abstract and contributions. The
   paper explains this in §4, but the abstract still says "beyond the 4 pp margin" as if 4 pp were the bar. Report
   the effective thresholds (−1.2 to −1.7 pp) in the abstract, or drop the δ framing there.
4. **The G\* run-to-run variance is missing from every student − G\* CI.** The paper says so, and it is the dominant
   unquantified term: G\* is nested Monte Carlo with M = 64 and one run per cell. The validation G\* rows also come
   from three rounds. The gap between validation and test5 point estimates is consistent across cells (test5 smaller
   in 5/5), so a G\* rerun on val3 in one cell would be cheap and informative.
5. **Smaller inaccuracies.** The inversion definition is two-sided in the text but one-directional in the code
   (mismatch 4). The latency seed spread is confounded with job and runner (mismatch 5). Limitation 11 is wrong about
   provenance (mismatch 3). "About 4 of 5" in §5.1 should read "4 of 5 (A16-2 would pass)". Three
   `[CITE-MISSING]` placeholders remain (DeepSets, Holm, Efron bootstrap). The $g_T$ estimator sizes (J = 16,
   L = 4,095; `code/touchbed/gt3.py`) are not stated.
6. **Length.** About 10,700 words with tables is well beyond 9 NeurIPS pages. §4 (2,356 words) and §5 (2,883 words)
   carry many report-only items (checkpoint sensitivity, DAgger regret ranges, E7 in full) that belong in an
   appendix. No section is under-length.

**Actionable revisions.**
- A1. In §5.2, §6, §7.7 and deviation (b), report the v4 E5 S12 reduced-G\* rows (success, ci95, n = 300, test3,
  prior recipe) as registered prior evidence, as addendum v5.1f specifies. Replace "unknown" and "unevaluated" with
  "measured once in the prior round on S12 only (−5.0 and −6.7 pp vs full G\*); not measured on test5 or in other
  cells". Correct the paraphrase of v5.1f.
- A2. In §3.3, replace "validation stream (92,160 episodes)" with "500-episode trainer validation stream, last read
  at 92,160 training episodes".
- A3. Delete or correct Limitation 11 and the matching §4 sentence. Cite `results_v5.json > 16_report_rows.json`

- A4. State the inversion rule as in the code: "RL higher on $g_T$ and student higher on success, both ci95

- A5. In §5.2 say the ≈20–25 % difference is seed 701 (job 1) against seeds 702–705 (job 2, another runner). Among
  seeds 702–705 the spread is ≤ 3 %.
- A6. Put the effective D5 threshold range in the abstract and the C1 bullet.
- A7. Move the checkpoint sensitivity, DAgger regret ranges and the E7 detail to an appendix to fit 9 pages. Fill the
  three citations.

**Score: 4/10 (borderline, leaning reject).** The methodology and transparency are above the norm, and the evidence
audit is clean of fabrication. The primary result is a well-documented negative in a narrow planar simulator, with a
weak RL comparator and one evidential misstatement about cheap planners.

## Reviewer B (significance, novelty, positioning)

**Summary.** The paper asks whether amortizing a cost-aware greedy touch planner by distillation keeps the planner's
quality across travel costs, and compares it with RL and heuristics. The answer is "not shown, and probably not
within 1.5 pp", plus a 2,500–9,900× per-decision speed-up.

**Strengths.**
1. It is on topic throughout. No environment or infrastructure issue is presented as a contribution, and E5 and E7
   are labelled methods notes.
2. The negative result is reported honestly, with explicit power statements: "not detected", not "absent", and
   about 7 pp not excluded.
3. Using the same head and action set for DAgger and PPO isolates the training signal, which is the right control.

**Weaknesses.**
1. **The novelty is narrow, as the paper concedes.** Distilling an information-gathering expert via DAgger
   (Choudhury et al. 2017; Nguyen et al. 2024) and amortizing design with RL (Foster et al. 2021; Blau et al. 2022)
   are both established. The contribution is a measurement on a 2D, 3–4-probe, shared-likelihood simulator. With
   horizons this short, the long-horizon credit-assignment argument for distillation is barely exercised.
2. **The practical claim is not demonstrated.** No physical time model exists, so the speed-up is not shown to
   matter for a probing cycle. The reduced planners, which run in 22–194 ms, are already fast enough for many
   robots. Prior evidence (S12, v4) says they lose 5–7 pp against full G\*, about the same as the v4 student. The
   paper omits this, so the reader cannot judge whether the student buys anything over a smaller planner
   (mismatch 1).
3. **The heuristic baselines are weak and noisily chosen.** The pilot choice of H\* is wrong in 4/5 cells on test5.
   B-spread is below Random in 4/5 cells, and no information-based cheap heuristic is included (for example, EIG
   with a handful of particles). "Beats heuristics in 4/5 cells" therefore says little about the frontier of cheap
   policies.
4. **The regime question is unresolved by design.** Five points, one of them a different library that changes K,
   n\* and shape family at once, cannot carry a slope estimate. The CI spans about ±7 pp over the travel range.
5. **The RL comparison is confounded.** One S2-tuned configuration collapses in A16-2, and it under-uses probes
   (about 3 of 4) in every S cell. The only cell where H2-v5 holds is the cell where RL failed, so C3 says almost
   nothing about RL as an approach.

**Actionable revisions.**
- B1. Add the v4 reduced-G\* rows to Table 4 or a side table, and frame C2 against cheap planners as well as
  heuristics.
- B2. Add one cheap information-based heuristic or a reduced-particle G\* on test5 in at least one cell if compute
  allows; otherwise state the omission in the abstract.
- B3. Retitle or reframe toward "a pre-registered negative result on amortizing a greedy touch planner", and move
  C5 out of the contribution list (it is methods notes).
- B4. Give a physical time model, even a parametric one (probe duration T_p, planning in series), so the latency
  result can be read in task terms.

**Score: 3/10 (reject).** It is a rigorous and honest measurement, but its significance for NeurIPS/ICML is limited:
the setting is planar and simulated, horizons are very short, the RL comparator is weak, and the practical

## 1. Reviewer A: significance and positioning for robotics

### Summary
The paper asks whether a DeepSets policy distilled by DAgger from a cost-aware greedy ball-EIG/c touch planner (G\*) matches that planner. It also compares the policy with PPO trained on a ball-sPCE reward using the same head, and with three probing heuristics. There are five pre-registered cells: one planar library S at κ = 2/4/8/12, and A16 at κ = 2. The primary gate D5 fails in all five cells. On test5 the student is 0.2–2.5 pp below G\*, with CIs about 10–12 pp wide. It beats the pilot-chosen heuristic in 4/5 cells under Holm. It beats RL on RL's own objective only where RL collapses (A16-2). No trend of the gap with travel is detected. The student is 2,523–9,933× faster per decision.

### Strengths
1. **Rigour of a kind rarely seen in robot-learning papers.** The amendments were written before the runs. The reduction order was frozen before sizing. Weight hashes were recorded before test5 (I verified two of them). The Holm families are declared, and the deviations are stated openly (Section 4, items a–d). The paper does not over-claim, and its headline is a negative result.
2. **The same head is used for both learners.** The DAgger student and the PPO policy share `RelDeepSets`, the action set and the masking (`code/touchbed/train_v2.py:99-100`, `code/touchbed/distill.py:136-146`). So the "training signal is the isolated variable" claim holds at the code level.
3. **The contrastive bound and the posterior read-out are reported together.** In the S cells G\* has the lowest g_T but the highest success. That is a useful caution for the amortized-BED community, and the paper correctly frames it as an observation, not a finding.
4. **The limitations section is honest and specific** (13 items). Teacher variance, evaluator resolution, the time-guarded `rl_success` arm, and the DAgger rounds being indistinguishable from BC on the selection metric are all stated.

### Weaknesses
1. **The robotics contribution is too narrow for RSS/CoRL.** The setting is a planar polygon, a single fingertip on a fixed 100 mm circle (`code/touchbed/geometry.py:12`) and 3–4 probes per episode. The world is simulated with exactly the softened likelihood the planner and filter use (`code/touchbed/likelihood.py:1-8`, 64-73), so there is no model mismatch, no contact dynamics, no 3D and no hardware. The related systems the paper cites (Tac2Pose, TACTFUL, Zheng et al., Gordon et al.) all deal with at least one of these. With a 3–4 step horizon and a 512-action set, the problem is close to a contextual bandit with budget coupling. The paper's own result (no regime dependence detected, small gaps) suggests the setting is not hard enough to separate the routes.
2. **The motivating premise is not established.** The paper argues for amortization because G\* takes "up to seconds" per decision (median 0.91–4.88 s, Table 4). But no physical probe-time model is used. More importantly, the obvious alternative, a smaller planner, is called "unevaluated" (Section 5.2, Discussion, Limitation 7). That is not quite right: the repository already holds reduced-planner accuracy (see Reviewer B, M1). On S12 test3 in v4, `g125_512_16` was −6.7 pp below G\* (ci95 [−12, −1]) and `g500_512_4` was −5.0 pp ([−11, +1.3]). Neither was better than the v4 students. This is the one comparison a robotics reader most wants, and the paper sets it aside.
3. **Significance of the negative result.** "A distilled policy is 1–3 pp below its greedy teacher, the CIs cannot tell, and RL is worse" is not surprising. Prior DAgger/BC-of-planner work (e.g. Choudhury et al. 2017, Nguyen et al. 2024) would predict it. The registered gate (an effective −1.2 to −1.7 pp threshold) is so tight that failing it carries little practical information. A practitioner would likely accept a 2–3 pp loss for a 10³–10⁴× speed-up. The paper does not say what margin matters for a robot task.
4. **The RL comparator is weak by construction.** One configuration was swept on S2 with one seed and reused everywhere. It collapses to 2 probes in A16-2 (4/5 seeds). On the sampled-policy metric the selection rule's winner (cfg 6) is not even the best (cfg 1: 6.316 vs 6.205). "Distillation vs RL" therefore reduces to "distillation vs an untuned RL outside S2". The authors acknowledge this, but it removes most of C3's value.
5. **The A16 "second library" is less independent than presented.** `make_set_A(0, 16)` contains the v3 library A (K = 8) as its first 8 shapes (`code/touchbed/shapes.py:98-99, 192-199`). A16 also changes K, n\* and the shape family at once and is run at one κ, so it adds little evidence about generalisation.
6. **Length and presentation.** The draft has about 10,760 words plus 7 tables and a 4-panel figure, well over the 8-page RSS / 9-page CoRL budget. The abstract (269 words) is over typical limits. There are three `[CITE-MISSING]` placeholders (DeepSets, Holm, Efron). Much of the text reads like a pre-registration audit log ("report-only", "PH", "DEC", "GATE") rather than a paper.

### Actionable revisions
1. Add the reduced-planner comparison:
   - Report the existing v4 E5 S12 accuracy rows and the v3 `14_rb_frontier` rows as prior-round evidence, which addendum v5.1f designates as the accuracy side of the frontier.
   - Or run `g125_512_16` / `g500_512_4` on test5 in at least two cells.
   - Without this, the case for learning over a smaller planner is unsupported.
2. State a task-level margin: what success loss is acceptable for what saving in time. Then convert latency into episode time under at least one explicit probe-time model (for example 1–2 s per probe stroke, planning in series).
3. Re-position the contribution as a "measurement / negative-result" short paper, or extend it to a setting where amortization plausibly matters: longer horizons, 3D, or model mismatch between simulator and planner likelihood.
4. Tune RL per cell, or remove the "distillation versus RL" framing from the title and contributions.
5. Cut the paper to the page budget. Move the decision-record minutiae, E7 and the checkpoint/DAgger audit to an appendix. Fix the three missing citations.

### Score and verdict (Reviewer A)
**Score: 3 / 10 (Reject).** The study is careful and honest, but the robotics significance is low: planar simulation, no model mismatch, 3–4 probes, a negative primary result and an untuned RL comparator. The key practical alternative (a cheaper planner) is set aside even though data exist. Not recommended for RSS / CoRL in this form. It could suit a workshop or a negative-results / reproducibility track.

## 2. Reviewer B: methodology–evidence consistency

### Summary of verification
I re-derived the headline numbers from the analysis JSON. I recomputed the S4 and A16-2 student − G\* success contrasts and the G\* travel from the raw test5 JSONL (`results/v5/test5/`). I read the statistics code and the D5, test5 and E6 analysis scripts. I checked the run manifests in `experiments/` against the described hyper-parameters, and two weight hashes against `13_freeze.json`. **The numbers in the paper match the evidence almost everywhere.** Trial counts, seeds, tests and hyper-parameters are as stated. I found no fabricated results. The problems are omissions and a few imprecise descriptions, listed as M1–M5 below.

### Strengths
1. **Trial counts and seeds are exactly as claimed.**
   - test5: 300 episodes for every method in every cell, and g_T is complete (`14_test5_analysis.json > <cell> > episodes_per_method`, `gt_episodes_per_method`).
   - val3: 500 episodes (`12_d5_analysis.json`).
   - Students use seeds 701–705, RL 711–715 and `rl_success` 711–715.
   - 25/25 RL cfg-6 runs reached 200,192 episodes (`16_report_rows.json > rl_main_cfg6`).
2. **The statistical tests exist and are implemented as described.** The crossed episode × seed bootstrap uses 10,000 resamples (`code/touchbed/stats.py:13-31`). Holm step-down is applied to two families (`code/analysis_test5_v5.py:171-177`). H5b is a WLS with inverse-bootstrap-variance weights (`analysis_test5_v5.py:123-151`). H2-v5 uses a one-sided α = 0.05 lower bound (`:106`). D5 uses the 1.645·√0.5 = 1.163 factor (`code/analysis_d5_v5.py:46`).
3. **Deviations are disclosed rather than hidden.** These are the test5 cut (consistent with addendum v5.1e), the missing frontier, flip rates for only one seed, and the `rl_success` truncation (training lengths match `16_report_rows.json` exactly).

### Factual mismatches and imprecisions (ranked)
- **M1 (substantive omission). Reduced-planner accuracy is described as "unevaluated" / "not measured", and deviation (b) misstates the registration.**
  - Addendum v5.1f (`docs/07c_amendment_v5_1.md:115-116`) says: "the accuracy side of the frontier is test5 success / BI (G\*, students) and, for the reduced configs, the v4 E5 rows only where they exist (S12); no new reduced-G\* accuracy runs are added in v5."
  - Those rows exist in evidence (a2), `results/v4/results_v4.json > 14_e5_analysis.json`. On S12 test3 (n = 300): `g125_512_16` − G\* = −0.0667 [−0.12, −0.01]; `g500_512_4` − G\* = −0.05 [−0.11, +0.013]. Both are 2–5 pp below the v4 students. There are also v3 `14_rb_frontier` pilot rows.
  - The draft instead says "No reduced-G\* accuracy was measured on test5, so the reduced configurations were timed only and no frontier is reported" (Section 4 (b)). It also says "the most obvious cheap alternative to learning unevaluated" (Section 5.2), "this study cannot say whether learning is needed" (Section 6), and "the cheapest alternative to learning, a smaller planner, is unevaluated" (Limitation 7).
  - The registered frontier did not need test5 reduced-G\* runs. A frontier (S12, prior-round accuracy) was deliverable but not delivered, and the existing evidence bears directly on the paper's main practical question.
- **M2 (description vs code). The inversion check tests only one direction.**
  - The paper says the check asks "whether g_T and the posterior success read-out order the student and RL in opposite directions".
  - The code claims inversion only if `rl_minus_student_gT` ci95 > 0 **and** `student_minus_rl` success ci95 > 0 (`code/analysis_test5_v5.py:110-112`). This matches the docstring ("RL higher g_T, lower success") but not the bidirectional wording.
  - No cell would flip, because there is no student-higher-g_T / RL-higher-success cell. The wording should still match the code.
- **M3 (undisclosed overlap). A16 contains the earlier library A.**
  - `make_set_A(0, 16)` generates from the same seed stream as library A (K = 8) of v3, so A16's first 8 identities are the v3 library A (`code/touchbed/shapes.py:98-99, 192-199`). Library A was used in v3 calibration (`results/v3/results_v3.json > 12_cal_analysis > n_star.A`, `tau.A_k12_*`).
  - Section 3.1 presents A16 as "the second library". It should say that half of its identities are the earlier A library.
- **M4 (undisclosed reuse). S2 and S12 round-0 labels are reused from v4 and v3.**
  - The r0 distillation commands read `results/v4/labels/S_k2_r0_*` (60 shards) and `results/v3/labels/S_k12_r0_*` (30 shards), with the held-out block from the same files (`experiments/v5_d5_r0_S2_S12_rerun.json`).
  - Amendment v5.0 E1 allows this. But the paper discloses only the reuse of G\* *validation* rows, while Algorithm 1 / Table S read as 3,000 fresh labels per cell.
- **M5 (minor numeric rounding). Student latency spread.**
  - "differ by up to about 24 % within a cell": the largest within-cell spread, including seed 701, is 24.8 % (A16-2: 0.3261 vs 0.407 ms; `14_e6_analysis.json`). Write "about 25 %".
  - "Other seeds' student medians (0.36–0.51 ms)": job-2 medians are 0.363–0.513 ms. That is correct.

### Methodological weaknesses (not mismatches)
1. **The primary comparison is under-powered by design.** CIs of 9.6–12.3 pp against δ = 4 pp, together with an effective D5 threshold of −1.2 to −1.7 pp, mean that neither "match" nor "worse" could be decided on test5. That should have been predictable from the v4 SEs before the 500 → 300 cut. Counting m = 0 as the registered answer while test5 shows 0.2–2.5 pp gaps makes the headline ("matching is not supported") depend mostly on the gate design.
2. **Teacher variance is excluded.** G\* was run once per cell (`n_seeds` [5, 1]). G\* uses nested-MC scores (M = 64), so its run-to-run variance is part of the comparison and the CIs are too narrow. The authors say so, but one rerun in one cell would bound it.
3. **The validation G\* rows are mixed across rounds** (S2 from v4, S12 from v3; `12_d5_analysis.json > gstar_source`). Addendum v5.1b justifies this as the same code path. Still, the v4 S2 gate with seeds 601–603 gave Δ̂ = −0.028, and the v5 S2 gate with the new best-checkpoint recipe gives −0.0328. The pre-declared remedy did not help S2, and the paper does not say this.
4. **The DAgger selection metric is blind to DAgger.** Checkpoints are selected on G\*-visited held-out decisions, and several r1/r2 rounds keep epoch 0–5 (verified: A16-2 s701 r2 epoch 0, S2 s705 r2 epoch 5, S12 s703 r2 epoch 4). The "DAgger" student is effectively BC plus fine-tuning. The paper discloses this. An on-policy regret read-out is the obvious fix.
5. **Unused evidence that would help C2 and C4.** The E6 job-1 logs record the action each method picks on the same states (`results/v5/lat/*_s701_gstar_d1000.jsonl > actions`). I computed top-1 agreement with full G\* (reviewer computation, exploratory, not in the paper):
   - student 0.18 / 0.30 / 0.46 / 0.51 / 0.33 (S2 / S4 / S8 / S12 / A16-2);
   - `g125_512_16` 0.10 / 0.18 / 0.24 / 0.31 / 0.15;
   - `g500_512_4` 0.07 / 0.12 / 0.21 / 0.24 / 0.09.

   On G\*'s own states, the student agrees with G\* more often than either reduced planner does in every cell, and agreement rises as κ grows. This is cheap, already-recorded evidence for the "student vs smaller planner" question and for the travel story. It should at least be reported as report-only.
6. **Multiplicity and many report-only rows.** More than 30 uncorrected contrasts are reported. That is acknowledged, but the main text should keep only the registered ones.
7. **"RL on the same objective" is imprecise.** G\* greedily maximises ball-EIG per unit cost, while RL maximises the terminal ball-sPCE g_T under the budget. The figure caption calls PPO's reward "G\*'s information objective". These are related but not the same objective.

### Actionable revisions
1. Correct deviation (b) and Section 5.2 / Section 6 / Limitation 7 to report the registered prior-round S12 frontier (v4 E5) with its caveats: different split (test3), v4 students. Add the action-agreement read-out from the E6 logs as report-only.
2. Reword the inversion definition to "RL higher on g_T and lower on success (both ci95 excluding 0)", or implement the symmetric check.
3. Disclose that A16 ⊃ v3 library A, and that S2/S12 round-0 labels and held-out blocks were reused from v4/v3.
4. State that the v5 recipe did not improve the S2 validation gap over v4 (−2.8 → −3.3 pp, different seeds).
5. Run one G\* rerun in one cell (cheap at test5 scale), or add a G\*-vs-G\* paired difference, to bound teacher variance.
6. Fix the three `[CITE-MISSING]` placeholders and the 24 % → about 25 % rounding.

### Score and verdict (Reviewer B)
**Score: 4 / 10 (Borderline, leaning reject).** The evidence is internally consistent and honestly reported, and I found no fabrication. But the primary comparison is under-powered by design. The main practical comparator (reduced planner) is called unevaluated although registered prior-round evidence exists. Several reuse and overlap facts are not disclosed. The DAgger and RL arms are each weakened by acknowledged design choices. As a methods/measurement record it is exemplary; as an RSS/CoRL contribution it does not yet answer a question a roboticist would act on.
