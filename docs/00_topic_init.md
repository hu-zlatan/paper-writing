## Topic

Amortized, non-myopic active tactile localization: a robot that must find out *where* (pose) and *which* (identity among a known set) an object is by touching it, using a learned policy that chooses each next touch to maximize the expected information gain (EIG) over the whole remaining touch budget — at a decision cost of one network forward pass instead of an online particle-filter optimization.

## Scope

**In scope**
- Known object library (K rigid shapes, given as meshes/polygons); unknown pose and identity; static object (it does not move when touched).
- Touch = a guarded probe of a fingertip along a chosen line; observation = contact point (+ surface normal) with sensor noise, or *no contact* (negative information).
- Sequential Bayesian experimental design (BED) view: prior over (identity, pose) → T touches → posterior. The policy is trained offline in simulation on a lower bound of the total EIG (sequential PCE), so it is non-myopic and amortized.
- Two simulation fidelities, both CPU-only: (i) a fast vectorized geometric probe model (ray–shape intersection, numpy) used for training and large sweeps; (ii) a MuJoCo physics check where a fingertip actually moves until a contact sensor fires, to show the learned policy transfers to physically simulated contacts.
- Planar SE(2) first (x, y, θ, identity), then SE(3) orientation subsets if time allows.

**Out of scope**
- Real-robot experiments (no hardware available to the pipeline — stated as a limitation).
- Vision-based tactile image rendering (GelSight-style images need GPU rendering); we model the *output* of a tactile sensor (contact location/normal), which is what localization uses.
- Objects that move under contact, deformable objects, unknown shapes (shape completion).

## SMART Goal

- **Specific:** Build and evaluate a policy-based BED method for active tactile localization that selects each touch from the touch history in one forward pass, trained on the sPCE bound of total EIG over a T-touch budget; compare against random, heuristic coverage, and greedy (myopic) particle-filter EIG baselines, with ablations of every claimed component.
- **Measurable:** after T ∈ {3, 5, 8, 10} touches report translation error (mm), rotation error (deg), identity accuracy, success rate (pose error < 5 mm and < 5°), posterior entropy / sPCE estimate of EIG, and per-decision wall-clock latency on CPU; ≥ 5 seeds; ≥ 10–15 distinct experimental conditions (object sets × noise × budget × fidelity).
- **Achievable:** all training and evaluation fits on a 2-vCPU / 7 GB sandbox plus free GitHub Actions CPU runners (geometric model is vectorized numpy; policy is a small MLP/DeepSets network trained on CPU).
- **Relevant:** touch-based localization is a prerequisite for dexterous manipulation under occlusion; existing EIG-based touch selection is either myopic and slow (particle filters, nested Monte Carlo) or local (Fisher-information around an MLE), while RL-based exploration lacks an information-theoretic objective.
